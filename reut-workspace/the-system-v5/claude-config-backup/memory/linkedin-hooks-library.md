## Hook Categories

### 1. Contrarian ("Everyone says X. I did Y.")
Best for: Challenging conventional wisdom. Works when Lior has real experience that contradicts common advice.

### 2. Story ("Last week/year, X happened...")
Best for: Specific moments Lior lived. Concrete time markers pull readers in.

### 3. Stat-Shock ("$XM in revenue. 0 marketing hires.")
Best for: When a number alone is surprising enough to earn the scroll-stop.

### 4. Listicle Promise ("5 lessons from scaling to $50M")
Best for: Tactical content. Reader knows exactly what they're getting.

#### Ruben Hassid — 2026-04-18 — 1,944 likes, 263 comments (13.5% ratio)
> "The $20/month Claude plan is enough. But only if you stop making these 17 mistakes: 1: You upload PDFs raw. One page = 3,000 tokens."

**Topic:** #ai-tools
**Why it works:** Two techniques stacked. (1) Odd specific number (17, not 10 or 20) signals real counting and adds credibility. (2) Item #1 lives INSIDE the hook — the reader gets value above the fold before deciding to expand. Most listicle posts tease the list; this one already started delivering.
**Bonus technique:** Contrarian framing built into the claim ("$20 is enough, but only if..."). The "but only if" structure keeps the reader reading to avoid being the person making the mistakes.
**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ruben-hassid_the-20month-claude-plan-is-enough-but-activity-7450770082126819328-RRmx
**Note:** Creator not on the 11-list. Saved for pattern value.

### 5. Question ("Why do most founders fail at [X]?")
Best for: Opening a loop the reader has to finish by reading.

#### Justin Welsh — 2026-04-18 — 2,454 likes, 879 comments (35.8% ratio — exceptional)
> "You hit the big goal. Now what? That's the question that very few people think about in their entrepreneurial journey."

**Topic:** #founder-journey
**Why it works:** Existential question, not tactical. "Now what?" opens a loop the reader has been avoiding asking themselves — and that's exactly why they engage. The follow-up claim ("very few people think about this") legitimizes the reader's private unease. The 35.8% comment ratio is not a post success, it's a shared cultural moment.
**When NOT to use:** Shallow questions ("What's your biggest pain point?") trigger 0 engagement. Must be a question the reader has genuinely been avoiding.
**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/justinwelsh_you-hit-the-big-goal-now-what-thats-activity-7450872788380479488-EZbT
**Creator on 11-list:** ✅ Justin Welsh

### 6. Confession ("I was wrong about [X] for 3 years.")
Best for: Vulnerability + authority. Must be genuinely felt, not manufactured.

### 7. Observation ("Noticed something odd about 250-person teams.")
Best for: Pattern recognition from Lior's unique vantage point (250-employee SaaS).

### 8. Bold Claim ("Most SaaS pricing is broken. Here's why.")
Best for: Strong opinion backed by evidence in the body.

#### Bruno Estrella (Head of Marketing @ Clay) — 2026-04-18 — 1,099 likes, 162 comments (14.7% ratio)
> "The future of growth marketing is go-to-market engineering. I say this as someone who has led growth marketing at startups for the past 10 years."

**Topic:** #ai-tools #saas-scaling #marketing
**Why it works:** Pairs a bold future claim with an immediate credibility anchor. The "I say this as someone who..." is the disarming move — it neutralizes "who are you to say this" before the reader thinks it. Works for identity-level topics where readers debate the claim in comments.
**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/brunoestrella_the-future-of-growth-marketing-is-go-to-market-activity-7447287934724153345-Ags8
**Note:** Creator not on the 11-list. Saved for pattern value.

### 9. Callout ("If you're still doing [X], stop.")
Best for: Direct, assertive. Use sparingly — can feel aggressive.

### 10. Vulnerability ("Almost lost my company in 2022.")
Best for: Real low moments Lior lived. Earned empathy.

### 11. Dialogue-Scene ("Person A: [provocative line]. Person B: [escalation].")
Best for: Dramatized internal conversation that opens mid-tension. Works when the topic has a universal workplace dynamic (manager vs IC, CEO vs VP, founder vs investor). Rare on LinkedIn — breaks pattern.

#### Conor Paulsen — 2026-04-18 — 834 likes, 138 comments (16.5% ratio, exceptional)
> "Head of Sales: I'm firing Marcus tomorrow. CEO: You mean our number 1 rep? Are you insane? Head of Sales: Ya.. Same guy."

**Topic:** #hiring #leadership #saas-scaling
**Why it works:** Dialogue format is rare on LinkedIn — stops the scroll. Mid-scene tension forces the reader to find out what happens. Provocative premise (firing top performer) guarantees comment debate. Specific details (Henderson deal, Jessica in January, 22% team drop) give credibility. PS at the end zooms out to a universal lesson ("the reps you tolerate define the culture more than the ones you celebrate").
**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/conor-paulsen_head-of-sales-im-firing-marcus-tomorrow-activity-7450913481471528960-3lWE
**Note:** Creator not on the 11-list. Saved for pattern value, not creator tracking.
