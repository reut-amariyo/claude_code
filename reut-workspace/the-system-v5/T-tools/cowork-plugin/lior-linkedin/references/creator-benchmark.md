## Reut's question answered: where do Zain Kahn's followers come from?

**Not from his posts.** He has 1,001,483 followers and his last five posts drew 40, 43, 38, 51
and 121 reactions. That is roughly 6 reactions per 100K followers, the weakest engine in the
entire sample by a factor of three.

Look at what he is actually posting:

- "We tracked 2 million newsletter clicks in the last 3 months."
- "Google generated 460+ leads from ONE newsletter."
- "Wispr Flow tracked $3.9M in sales from ads in ONE newsletter."
- "Guidde raised $50M+ in funding."

**These are not audience posts. They are advertising case studies.** His LinkedIn is a sales
channel selling ad slots in the Superhuman AI newsletter, not a growth channel. The follower
count and the content have nothing to do with each other.

So where did a million followers come from? Three pieces of evidence found on the profiles:

1. **His headline is a follow CTA, not a job title.** "Follow me to learn how you can leverage
   AI to boost your productivity and accelerate your career."
2. **His banner is SUPERHUMAN branding** — the newsletter, reported at 1M+ subscribers.
3. **Hamna Aslam Kahn's headline is the same formula**: "Follow me to learn how to use AI at
   work and beyond. Join the world's biggest AI newsletter with 1M+ readers ↓". She has
   280,636 followers. Awais Khan is listed as co-founder at Superhuman AI, Chris Pelissier as
   partnerships. It is a **multi-account network pointing at one newsletter.**

**The conclusion: the newsletter built the LinkedIn following, not the other way round.**
The audience was imported from another channel and captured with a follow CTA in the headline.

The same shape almost certainly explains Allie K. Miller at 2M followers and 17 reactions per
100K. Her headline stacks external assets: an AI-first course with 400K+ students, plus
Instagram, X and TikTok.

### Why this matters more than anything else in this document

**We have been trying to solve on LinkedIn a problem the biggest accounts solved somewhere
else.** And we already own the asset they used: 100,000+ Instagram followers against 11,607
on LinkedIn.

The Instagram-to-LinkedIn port stops being a nice idea and becomes the observed mechanism
behind the largest accounts on the platform. Paired with a follow-CTA headline, which is the
cheapest change available to us, it is the most credible path to a step-change in follower rate.

**Warning that follows directly:** you cannot copy Zain Kahn's growth by copying his posts,
because his growth did not happen in his posts. Copying his post style copies the dead half.

## Verdicts

### Reut's chosen reference set

**Allie K. Miller · Ruben Hassid · Hamna Aslam Kahn** — the AI-artifact lane. Reut's call,
confirmed across three separate picks. What transfers:
- Screenshot of the actual thing as the visual. This is our personal-artifact family, legal.
- Named entity in line 1. Gamma, Claude, ChatGPT, Reddit. Matches Rule A.
- Imperative "stop doing X" openers. Hamna: "Stop copy-pasting AI text into slides."
- 1,000-1,500 chars, matching the save-first extension.
- The steal-this posture: here is a thing, take it.

🛑 What must change: **the payload.** Their posts are consumer AI tool tips, which our rules
ban and where a 250-person CEO has no advantage against a full-time newsletter operation.
Lior's version keeps the architecture and swaps the payload to insider operator access, the
documented 93K MD→HTML exception: insider intel, operator confession, strategic close.
Also drop the 🚨 emoji openers and the free-tool and cost angles.

**Chris Donnelly** — the manifest structure. 🛑 Never the lead-magnet layer.

**Jasmin Alić** — line craft only, best engine measured. 🛑 No ♻️ CTAs, no coach voice.

**Sahil Bloom** — Reut's pick for the personal quarter, and the right one. 720K, 116 per 100K.
His hooks are the shortest in the sample: "Growth feels like destruction." · "Become a beginner
again." · "Nobody talks about looking ordinary." Four to six words, second person, 829-1,971
chars. Take hook compression and second-person address.
⚠️ Two guardrails: his lane is life philosophy, which is Motivator-adjacent, and one of his
hooks is "You're not stuck. You're zoomed in.", a textbook X-not-Y construction from the
banned contrast family. Take the compression, not the constructions.

## Caveats

- Five recent posts per account is a snapshot, not a trailing average.
- Reactions per 100K is a rough proxy. It cannot see saves, which is the metric we actually
  want and which is invisible on other people's posts.
- Allie K. Miller's 2M is from her own headline, not the profile counter.
- The Superhuman network read is inference from headlines, banners and the People-you-may-know
  panel. It is strongly suggested by the evidence, not confirmed by the company.
