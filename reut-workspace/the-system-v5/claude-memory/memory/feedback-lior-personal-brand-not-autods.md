# Lior's content is his personal brand, not AutoDS marketing

**Rule:** Everything we write for Lior — posts, replies, frameworks, application suggestions — is for **Lior Pozin's personal brand as a founder**. It is NOT marketing for AutoDS.

**Why:** AutoDS is the company. Lior is the person. His personal brand is built on his journey, lessons, mistakes, and perspective as a founder who scaled to 200 employees. If his content reads as product marketing ("use AutoDS to do X," "our $49 plan replaces Y"), it stops being personal brand and becomes demand gen — which reduces trust, reduces followers, and misses the entire point of why we're building the brand.

**How to apply:**

**DO write about:**
- Lior's founder journey, decisions, mistakes
- Lessons from scaling to 200 employees
- Hiring, firing, management at scale
- SaaS/ecommerce industry perspective
- Macro management, OKRs, systems (his "Build to Scale" course territory)
- Founder psychology — loneliness, decisions, responsibility
- Industry observations from his vantage point

**DON'T write:**
- Product tutorials or use cases for AutoDS
- "How our platform does X" content
- Pricing comparisons involving AutoDS plans
- Feature-level marketing of any product
- Anything that reads as "drive signups"

**Applying patterns from inspiration creators:**
When we study a top post and think about "how Lior could apply this" — always translate to Lior's founder-voice, not product-voice.

- ❌ Wrong: "The $49 AutoDS plan can replace a product research team. But only if you stop making these 11 mistakes."
- ✅ Right: "The solo founder stage is survivable. But only if you stop making these 11 mistakes." (from journey)
- ✅ Right: "Your first VP hire is enough. But only if you stop making these 9 mistakes." (from leadership experience)
- ✅ Right: "Scaling to 200 employees didn't require a new product. It required killing 7 habits that got me here." (from his journey)

**Context:** Reut flagged this after an analysis suggestion used AutoDS as the product example. Going forward, every Lior application idea should pass the test: "Would this sound like marketing for his company, or like wisdom from his career?"
