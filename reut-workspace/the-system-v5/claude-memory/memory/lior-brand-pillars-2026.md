# Lior Pozin — Brand Pillars (confirmed 2026-05 brand session)

**Source:** Brand characterization meeting with Lior, week of 2026-05-04. Confirmed by Reut.

These three pillars define Lior's personal brand. They are the foundation, not a suggestion. Every post — LinkedIn, X, IG, podcast pitch, reply — must reflect at least ONE of these pillars. Posts that don't ladder up to a pillar get killed in pre-publish review.

---

## Pillar 1 — "I live on 200" (THE UNIQUE DIFFERENTIATOR)

**What Lior said:** "I live on 200, that's my uniqueness."

**What it means:**
Lior operates at maximum intensity by default. Not 100%. 200%. Always-on, full throttle, always shipping, always pushing. He's not a balance-and-boundaries founder. He's a 200-mph operator who built AutoDS that way and still runs at that gear.

**How to use it in content:**
- Show the pace: hours, decisions, meetings, real volume of output
- Contrast against "balance" / "founder wellness" / "hustle is dead" content — Lior's POV is the opposite of the sleep-8-hours founder narrative dominant on LinkedIn right now
- Make pace the proof, not just a claim. "I live on 200" works only when paired with what he actually did this week/quarter
- The contrarian fuel: most senior founders posting on LinkedIn won't admit they live this way. Lior will. That's the differentiation.

**Watch out for:**
- Don't romanticize burnout — pillar is about intensity by choice, not martyrdom
- Don't preach it as advice for others — he lives this way; he's not telling everyone to
- Don't repeat "I live on 200" as a slogan. It's the truth behind the post, not the tagline.

**Post archetypes that fit naturally:**
- Volume-of-output posts ("here's what shipped this week")
- Pace-vs-peers posts ("most founders take a sabbatical at $X ARR. I'm at $Y and I haven't")
- Decision-velocity posts ("we made the call in 30 minutes. it would have taken 3 weeks at our size if we had a process")

---

## Pillar 2 — "Zero to one" (the founder he is)

**What Lior said:** He loves taking something from 0 to 1.

**What it means:**
Lior is a build-from-scratch founder. The thrill is the first version, the first customer, the first dollar, the first hire. He's at his best when there's nothing yet and he has to figure out how to make it exist. Not a scaler-from-1-to-100, not an optimizer — a creator from nothing.

**How to use it in content:**
- Stories about the earliest days of AutoDS, first product attempts, first customer wins
- Stories about new initiatives inside AutoDS that he's personally driving from scratch (a new product, a new market, a new motion)
- Posts about founders who lose their edge once the company is built — and the discipline required to stay in 0-to-1 mode even after Series B/C
- Lessons from building that only apply at the 0-to-1 stage, where Lior has hard-won standing

**Watch out for:**
- Don't generalize his 0-to-1 stories into "every founder should do X" — keep it about his experience
- Don't conflate 0-to-1 with reckless — Lior is also Pillar 3 (focus). The 0-to-1 work is disciplined, not chaotic
- The dollar-bug story (lior-story-dollar-bug.md) is canonical 0-to-1 content. Reuse-friendly.

**Post archetypes that fit naturally:**
- Origin-story scene posts (first customer, first hire, first failure)
- "I built this from scratch and here's what I learned" lessons
- Contrarian takes on what late-stage founders forget about early-stage building
- Founder-archetype posts ("I'm a 0-to-1 founder, not a scaler — and that's a real thing")

---

## Pillar 3 — Focus / מיקוד

**What Lior said:** He believes in focus.

**What it means:**
Depth over breadth. One thing done excellently beats five things done okay. Lior makes hard call-it-quits decisions on initiatives, products, and people that aren't working. He doesn't chase shiny objects. The discipline to say no is the discipline that compounds.

**How to use it in content:**
- Stories about killing a product, channel, or initiative — and what survived because of it
- Posts about saying no to opportunities most founders chase (a tempting partnership, a viral side bet, a pivot)
- Frameworks for deciding what to focus on — but only if Lior actually used them
- Contrast against "do everything" / "be everywhere" / "expand into Y new markets" content

**Watch out for:**
- Don't conflict with Pillar 1 ("live on 200"). Pillar 1 is about intensity; Pillar 3 is about narrowness of target. Together: 200% effort on one thing, not 50% effort on four.
- Don't preach focus as a generic principle — show the specific thing Lior chose NOT to do
- Don't kill any hiring or strategic moves in the post itself — focus posts shouldn't expose live business decisions

**Post archetypes that fit naturally:**
- "We killed X to focus on Y" stories (with real numbers if possible)
- Deal-killer moments where lack of focus was the disqualifier
- Investor / advisor conversations where Lior pushed back on expansion advice
- Lessons on the cost of distraction at scale

## How the three pillars combine

The compounding statement: **Lior runs at 200% intensity (Pillar 1), inside a tightly focused scope (Pillar 3), on the work where 0-to-1 creation matters most (Pillar 2).**

Posts that hit two or three pillars at once outperform single-pillar posts.

**Strongest combinations:**
- Pillar 1 + Pillar 3 → "Most founders try to do everything at half-speed. I do one thing at 200%."
- Pillar 2 + Pillar 3 → "I love 0-to-1 — and the only way to actually get there is to kill everything that isn't 0-to-1 work."
- All three → A scene post where Lior, at 200% pace, kills a side project to stay focused on a 0-to-1 initiative.

---

## Pre-publish filter: pillar check

Before any Lior post ships, ask:
1. **Which pillar does this hit?** If the answer is "none" or "kind of all of them" — kill it.
2. **Is the pillar shown, not stated?** "I live on 200" is the truth; the post should prove it through action, not announce it.
3. **Does it conflict with another pillar?** A post advocating "do many things at once" violates Pillar 3 even if Pillar 1 is loud.
4. **Would Lior credibly own this?** The pillars are HIS lived experience, not aspirations. If he can't claim the proof, kill it.
