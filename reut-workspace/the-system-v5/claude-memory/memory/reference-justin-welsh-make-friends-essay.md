**Thesis:** people who sneer at successful creators promoting each other have spotted a real advantage and resent it instead of building it. You can't build a network you resent. Build a network of peers BEFORE anyone in it is powerful; the friendships compound as everyone grows.

**His receipt:** started writing online late 2018, befriended Austin Belcak, Scott Leese, Scott Barker while none were known online. Ordinary rituals: a standing quarterly notes-compare call, a weekly live show started 2020, dinners -> investing in a friend's firm -> helping grow his Substack. Point: nothing special, just staying in touch for years.

**Practical moves he names:** recurring call to compare notes, a joint project, cross-promoting each other's launches, solving each other's biggest problem. Timeframe 5-10 years.

**Beat structure (donor candidate for an essay-style post):**
1. Observed public behavior people get angry at - concrete scene, book launch + pile-on comments
2. Reframe: of course friends help friends
3. Name the hidden advantage the haters spotted
4. One-line principle on its own line ("can't build the network you resent")
5. Own receipt: year + 3 named peers + ordinary rituals
6. Second principle line: network before anyone is powerful
7. Projection: who these strangers become in 5-10 years
8. Close on 2 short lines - the future key people don't know you exist yet

Note the hook is an observed outrage, not "I". Every principle sits alone on its own line, matching [[feedback-linkedin-line-spacing-rules]].
