Reut, 2026-09-02, during the Fable 5.1 LinkedIn post: "When I'm sending you a new version, it
means that I changed that and I want it to stay. You can improve the content, but don't come
back to versions that you did two posts ago. Keep what I've changed. If I changed it, I don't
want it anymore."

**Why:** every edit she makes is a decision, not a typo. Restoring my earlier phrasing silently
overturns her decision and forces her to re-make it, which is what made the Fable 5.1 post take
eight rounds. Her edited version is the canonical text from the moment she sends it.

**How to apply:**
- Treat her returned version as the base. Diff against HER text, never against mine.
- Only change what is actually broken: grammar, tense, spelling, internal inconsistency,
  a hard house rule, or a factual error. Say what was fixed and why, in a line each.
- Improvements must build on her wording. If a line of mine was better, offer it as an
  option below the draft, never by putting it back in the body.
- If her edit breaks a house rule she herself set, flag it once, then respect the choice.
  She overrode the 6-word parenthetical cap three times on the Fable 5.1 post; it stayed.
