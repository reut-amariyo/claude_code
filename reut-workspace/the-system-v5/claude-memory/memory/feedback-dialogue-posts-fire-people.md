# Dialogue-scene posts: person > customer

**Rule:** When drafting dialogue-scene format posts for Lior, default to a PERSON-centered conflict (firing, demoting, letting go of an employee). Avoid customer-centered conflicts (firing the biggest customer, killing a feature) as the primary dramatic axis.

**Why:** Readers project themselves onto "the person being fired." They think: "this could be me — am I the one who's stopped growing? Am I the one the team is working around?" Customer stories keep them intellectually engaged but emotionally distant — they're reading about a business decision, not a human one. The goal of the dialogue format is emotional identification, and that requires a human being at the center of the tension.

**How to apply:**
- For dialogue-scene posts: the fired character is always a person (employee, co-founder, long-tenured manager, recently-promoted IC)
- Bonus power: the person should have something the reader might have — tenure, loyalty, past wins, a relationship with the founder. The more the reader can imagine being them, the harder the post hits.
- For customer/feature-centered conflicts: use a different structure (stat-shock, framework, opinion-with-evidence) — not dialogue-scene.

**Context:** Reut confirmed this after reviewing a draft where the conflict was "firing the biggest customer." She said: "people connect more to firing a person because it meets them at 'this could be me.'"
