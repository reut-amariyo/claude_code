## Iron-Clad Failure Patterns (confirmed 3+ times each)

1. **Zero personal stake = failure.** Every Lior post below 1,000 impressions has zero personal stake. No exceptions in 8 data points. Every post MUST include a specific Lior moment, number, or lived experience — not a generic take.

2. **Arrow-listicle (→) = template content.** Killed Posts 17, 19, 20 (800 impressions each). "→ Anthropic built... → Cursor scaled... → Notion hit..." Senior audience recognizes template structure and scrolls past. Do NOT use arrow-list formatting on Lior's LinkedIn even when the topic begs for it.

3. **LinkedIn template phrases kill voice.** "Read that again." / "Not X. Not Y. Z." / "One person... One team..." — overused LinkedIn-bro patterns that contradict Lior's authentic voice.

4. **Graph/chart images hurt performance.** Post 17 died partly because of a graph. Use personal photos or scene context instead.

5. **Zero friction = zero comments = algorithm death.** If no one can disagree, no one engages.

6. **Celebrity commentary is the weakest archetype.** Summarizing Jensen/Elon/Sam Altman = zero differentiation. Avoid unless Lior has a direct personal counter-experience.

7. **Off-ICP topics hit a ceiling (~300-2K).** Niche AI tools (OpenClaw) fail. ICP cares about SaaS scaling, pricing, revenue, founder journey.

8. **Story-to-hiring-CTA pivot = dead post.** Posts that open with a vulnerability/founder-journey hook and pivot into a "we're hiring [Role], link in comments" CTA underperform badly. Confirmed 2026-04-28 ("I got rejected" / Director of Partnerships, ~860 imp / 35 likes / 1 comment at 18h). Bait & switch breaks trust, closes the engagement gap (only candidates can react), violates THE REAL PERSON PRINCIPLE, and the algorithm reads it as a recruiting ad. Split into two posts: (a) standalone founder story with open ending, (b) standalone hiring post with managed expectations. See `feedback-no-hiring-ads-as-story-posts.md`.

## Confirmed Winning Archetype: "AI Tool Experiment + Contrarian Reframe"

**Pattern:** Lior runs a real 1-4 week experiment with a named AI tool → describes the system enthusiastically → flips on one specific failure mode → derives a universal lesson about humans/judgment → softly reframes the marketing narrative around the tool category.

**Why it works for Lior's audience:** Senior SaaS founders are drowning in AI hype. They want a peer who actually tested the thing and has a counter-take grounded in experience. Lior's 250-employee vantage point gives him the authority to say "I tried to remove humans from the loop and here's what broke."

**Required ingredients:**
1. Named tool (Paperclip, not "an AI tool") — credibility
2. Time anchor ("For 2 weeks") — proves real test, not theoretical
3. One memorable analogy ("telephone game", "blurry copy of a blurry copy") — the quotable line
4. Parallel-sentence insight ("Real people push back. Agents don't.")
5. Fair-shake reframe (tool is good, the *story around it* is marketing) — avoids hot-take trap
6. Open gap close — rhetorical question that invites debate

**Do NOT use this archetype if:**
- Lior didn't actually run the experiment (audience smells fake test)
- The conclusion is a flat "AI is overhyped" — needs a specific failure mode, not vibes
- The tool isn't named — kills credibility
