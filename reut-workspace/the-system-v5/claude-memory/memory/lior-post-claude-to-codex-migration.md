## The 8 beats

1. Two-number hook, 9 words. "9 months with Claude. Moved to Codex in 4 min."
2. Line-2 parenthetical counter-hook, 5 words. The one legal use of parentheses.
3. Permission line. "I know. You love Claude. So do I." Disarms the reader who disagrees.
4. Operator confession. He ignored everyone telling him to move, because his systems lived there.
5. The trigger, one line. Something external changed, so the resistance stopped being rational.
6. The named cost of staying, borrowed vocabulary. Setup debt, credited to the discourse not coined by us.
7. Three real numbered steps with real commands. Not softened, not abstracted.
8. Strategic close that refuses the obvious verdict. "Claude is not dead. It has a colleague."

## The three things that made the how-to legal

- The confession opens it, so it reads as an operator passing an edge rather than a guru teaching.
- Every command was verified against primary source before publishing, not taken from the donor.
- The close is about running two suppliers, which is a management idea, not a product idea.

## The rule the ending enforced, three separate times

Reut killed three lines for contradicting the close: "make the switch", "Codex is better than
Claude Code", and my own "Astra eats Claude" visual concepts. If the post ends on "it has a
colleague", nothing above it may say he left. **Check every draft against its own last line.**
