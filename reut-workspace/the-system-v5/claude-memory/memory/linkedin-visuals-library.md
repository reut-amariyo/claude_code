# LinkedIn Visuals Library

**Purpose:** The visual decides whether the hook gets read at all. This library tracks what works on the LinkedIn feed scroll.

**How to add:** Auto-populated by `/linkedin-learn`. Visual analysis is done on the actual image/video.

**How to use:** After drafting a post, check this library for visual types that pair well with the structure/topic. Flag to Reut what kind of asset to produce.

### Banned visual on Lior's profile
**Designed-by-Lior-marketing-team graphics.** Canva quote cards. Branded promotional images. The litmus test: would a journalist citing the same image in a Bloomberg article also use it? If no — it's a marketing graphic, kill it.

### Polish rule
Polished graphics ARE allowed when sourced from a third-party authority (Anthropic, McKinsey, WSJ, government data). NOT allowed when made by Lior's team.

### The Red-Circle Annotation Rule (confirmed by 2 of 8 top posts)
When the visual has a small detail the reader would miss, mark it with a **hand-drawn red circle**.

**How:** Markup / Preview, NOT Figma vector. Looks intentional but unpolished.

**Why it works:** Three jobs in one — (1) tells the scrolling eye exactly where to look, (2) turns the image into a 1-second puzzle (= dwell time = algorithm signal), (3) reads as "I noticed this myself" — operator-on-the-ground energy, not designed marketing asset.

**When to use:**
- Visual proof of the post is a small/specific detail (a date, a logo, a number, a wristband)
- Post hinges on the reader catching what Lior caught

**When NOT to use:**
- Visual is already obvious (a portrait, a quote card)
- Post is voice-driven, not detail-driven
- More than 2 circles in one image = busy, kills the effect

### "Receipt Stack" pattern (confirmed by post #2 Nike-Amazon)
Two real news article screenshots from different dates, stacked vertically, same product photo on both, dates circled in red marker. Use for Big-Brand Reversal Case Study posts where the visual job is to make the reader feel the timeline gap.

### Tool-icon-as-meme-caption (confirmed by post #6 AI agents)
Embed your product logo INTO a custom meme as part of the joke (Paperclip icon overlaid 4x on the Brad Pitt church meme). Subtle brand placement without pitch posture. The meme must be Lior-made, not stock. Product pitched in the text = banned.

## Generic Visual Types (legacy — kept for non-Lior reference / creator scans)

### 1. No Visual (Text-Only)
Pure text posts. Rarer than expected but sometimes outperform when the hook is strong enough.

**When it works:** Ultra-sharp hook, short-punchy structure, topic is divisive/thought-provoking. Also works for dialogue-heavy posts where a visual would distract from the dramatic rhythm.

#### Conor Paulsen — 2026-04-18 — 834 likes, 138 comments
**Post length:** ~380 words
**Pairing logic:** Dialogue format needs full reader attention on the text. Any visual would compete with the dramatic beats. The lack of visual is part of what makes it feel like a "script" — raw, unpolished, real.
**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/conor-paulsen_head-of-sales-im-firing-marcus-tomorrow-activity-7450913481471528960-3lWE

#### Bruno Estrella — 2026-04-18 — 1,099 likes, 162 comments
**Post length:** ~340 words
**Pairing logic:** Opinion-with-evidence posts that coin a new term work better text-only — the coined phrase ("GTM Engineering") IS the visual asset. A graphic would dilute the phrase's power as a shareable handle. When the thesis itself is the takeaway, text-only forces the reader to sit with the idea.
**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/brunoestrella_the-future-of-growth-marketing-is-go-to-market-activity-7447287934724153345-Ags8

### 2. Single Static Image — Portrait of Creator
Professional portrait, usually the creator in their environment (office, stage, product).

**When it works:** Personal story posts, milestone announcements, "state of the business" content.

### 3. Single Static Image — Text-on-Image (Quote Card)
Big text quote on branded background. Usually the key line from the post.

**When it works:** Opinion/insight posts. Gives a shareable, screenshot-able moment.

### 4. Single Static Image — Screenshot
Real screenshot of a tool, metric, message, or receipt. Feels authentic and unpolished.

**When it works:** Stat-shock hooks, before/after, build-in-public content.

### 5. Single Static Image — Infographic / Diagram
Clean diagram explaining a framework or process.

**When it works:** Tutorial or framework posts. Reader saves the post.

### 6. Single Static Image — Meme / Humor
Pop culture reference or humorous visual with minimal text.

**When it works:** Observation posts, industry callouts. Risky — must feel native, not forced.

### 7. Carousel (Multi-slide)
3-10 slides. Usually slide 1 = hook, middle slides = content, final slide = CTA or reframe.

**When it works:** Listicle posts, frameworks, step-by-step content. LinkedIn favors carousels heavily in the algo.

### 8. Native Video (Under 90 seconds)
Talking head or screen-recording with captions.

**When it works:** Personal voice, vulnerability, founder stories. High completion rate = high reach.

### 9. Long Video (90s-10min)
Usually repurposed podcast clip or interview.

**When it works:** When the clip has a strong open and the creator has audience authority.

### 10. Photo Carousel (No Text)
Multiple lifestyle/event photos, no overlay text.

**When it works:** Event recaps, team photos, personal milestones.
