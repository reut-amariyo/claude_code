# Lior's Top LinkedIn Post — Nike's Amazon Reversal

**Topic:** Nike walked away from Amazon in 2019 to go DTC. 6 years later, they're back. Why.
**Performance:** Top performer (confirmed by Reut 2026-05-04 as a winning reference).

## Full text (canonical reference)

```
In 2019, Nike walked away from Amazon

They had a good reason to do it - they went all-in on DTC. Nike wanted to completely own the customer relationship and the profit margins.

6 years later, they're going back to Amazon.

What changed?
1. 𝐑𝐞𝐬𝐞𝐥𝐥𝐞𝐫𝐬 - Nike itself left Amazon, but resellers are still selling it on Amazon. Which means Nike is basically 𝒍𝒐𝒔𝒊𝒏𝒈 a huge playground to small players it can't control.

2. 𝐀𝐦𝐚𝐳𝐨𝐧 - it got even bigger. More than 50% of U.S. product searches now start on Amazon. Not being there was like 𝒏𝒐𝒕 𝒆𝒙𝒊𝒔𝒕𝒊𝒏𝒈 in a major part of the internet's mall.

3. 𝐑𝐞𝐯𝐞𝐧𝐮𝐞 - In Q3 2025, Nike's DTC revenue dropped 12% YoY to $4.7B.
Digital sales fell 15%.
12-month Revenue is now $47.8B - 𝒂 7% 𝒅𝒆𝒄𝒍𝒊𝒏𝒆 from FY 2024 ($51.4B).
Plus, their stock is down.

It only took 6 years and a big drop in revenue to make this decision.

Nike isn't giving up on DTC. Hell no. It just realized they can't completely control distribution.

DTC can co-exist with marketplaces. It should.
```

## Why it works — the 6-beat architecture

1. **Year + brand + reversal hook.** "In 2019, Nike walked away from Amazon" — date + famous brand + counterintuitive action. No fluff. Reader immediately wants to know why.
2. **Steelman their original reason.** "They had a good reason to do it — they went all-in on DTC." Lior validates Nike's original logic before dismantling it. This earns trust; he's not dunking, he's analyzing.
3. **The reversal punch (one line).** "6 years later, they're going back to Amazon." Short. Standalone. The whole post pivots here.
4. **Numbered 3-point breakdown with specific numbers + bolded keywords.** Resellers / Amazon / Revenue. Each point names ONE force, supports it with a real stat (50% of U.S. searches, 12% YoY drop, $47.8B revenue, 7% decline), and uses Unicode bold + italics to make scanning effortless.
5. **The verdict line.** "It only took 6 years and a big drop in revenue to make this decision." A single sentence that tells the reader what to feel about Nike's pace.
6. **Founder POV closer (no question, just a stance).** "Nike isn't giving up on DTC. Hell no. It just realized they can't completely control distribution. DTC can co-exist with marketplaces. It should." Strong opinion + parallel structure + last word is a mic-drop ("It should.").

## What this post does — that the Shopify post doesn't

- **No personal Lior story.** This is pure case study, but it lands because the topic is Lior's daily reality (DTC vs. marketplace = the AutoDS world). Lior has standing to opine.
- **No question CTA.** Closes with conviction instead of an open question. The conviction itself ("DTC can co-exist with marketplaces. It should.") is the engagement gap — readers debate it in the comments because they have a stake.
- **Unicode bold + italics inside the body.** 𝐑𝐞𝐬𝐞𝐥𝐥𝐞𝐫𝐬, 𝒍𝒐𝒔𝒊𝒏𝒈, 𝐀𝐦𝐚𝐳𝐨𝐧, 𝒏𝒐𝒕 𝒆𝒙𝒊𝒔𝒕𝒊𝒏𝒈 — visual rhythm without using regular bold. Makes a wall-of-stats post feel scannable.
- **Real numbers > vibes.** Q3 2025 DTC -12%, digital -15%, FY revenue -7%, $47.8B vs $51.4B. Specificity = credibility.
- **"Hell no" voice break.** One casual interjection in an otherwise analytical post. Signals a real human, not a McKinsey deck.

## What this post does NOT do (still applies)

- Does NOT criticize Amazon or Nike — observes their moves, doesn't moralize.
- Does NOT pitch AutoDS even though distribution-vs-marketplace is core AutoDS territory.
- Does NOT use em dashes (uses regular hyphens with spaces).
- Does NOT use arrow listicles or "Read that again." LinkedIn-bro phrases.
- Does NOT name "200 employees" or stuff in social proof.

## Confirmed winning archetype: "Big-Brand Reversal Case Study"

**Pattern:**
> Year + named brand + counterintuitive action → steelman their original logic → one-line reversal punch → numbered 3-point breakdown (each point: 1 force + 1 specific stat + bolded keyword) → verdict line on the cost of the lesson → founder-POV closer with conviction (not a question).

**Why it works for senior SaaS founders:**
The audience is built on case-study consumption (HBR, Stratechery, etc.). They scroll past opinion posts but stop for "here's exactly why Brand X reversed Strategy Y." When Lior decodes a public reversal in his own category (commerce, marketplaces, DTC), he's positioned as a peer-analyst — same level as the brand, not a fan watching from the cheap seats.

**Required ingredients:**
1. Famous brand reversing a famous decision — must be public, recent, verifiable
2. Year anchors at both ends ("In 2019…" / "6 years later…") — makes the timeline visceral
3. Steelman beat — show their original logic was reasonable
4. Exactly 3 numbered points, each with a real stat (revenue %, market share, search share, etc.)
5. Bolded keyword + italicized verb-of-impact in each point — Unicode formatting for visual rhythm
6. Verdict line — one sentence on what the reversal cost
7. Founder-POV closer — short, confident, last word is a 1-3 word punch ("It should." / "Hell no.")
8. Topic must be inside Lior's standing-zone: commerce, DTC, marketplaces, SaaS pricing/distribution, hiring/team scale, AI tooling. Don't case-study a category Lior doesn't operate in.

**Do NOT use this archetype if:**
- The brand reversal isn't real or is just rumor — credibility dies
- The 3 points don't have hard numbers — becomes opinion
- Lior has no operational standing in the topic — reads as commentary, not analysis
- The closer hedges ("interesting times…") — must end with conviction
- You stuff in 5-7 points — 3 is the cap; more = essay, not LinkedIn post
