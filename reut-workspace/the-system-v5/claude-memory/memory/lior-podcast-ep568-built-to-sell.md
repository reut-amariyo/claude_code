This is his first deep M&A interview, about both buying and selling. It's the best source for any post on valuation, PE, earn-outs, bankers or acquisitions.

**New public facts, said by Lior on the record:**
- "More than 50 million in annual revenue" today, growing fast, with FEWER employees than at the acquisition thanks to AI. At acquisition: ~200 employees, ~$20M. This replaces $20M+ ARR as the current figure.
- AutoDS made "more than 10 million in profit at some point". During the Fiverr process: started ~12, then ~25 revenue, ~10M EBITDA.
- For the first 4-5 years everything went back into growth. "The bank was on zero always. And if it wasn't on zero, I was not satisfied."
- The co-founders' equity split was NOT equal thirds, and they don't disclose it.
- The host read Fiverr's filing as $55M cash + $36M earn-out, and Lior said "something like that". In brand content the number stays $92M ([[lior-exit-amount-92m-vs-sec-filing]]).
- The earn-out was revenue + EBITDA, checked every 6 months (e.g. Jan 2027, Jul 2027), on a 3-year horizon. "We achieved everything very quickly."
- He calls AutoDS B2C ([[feedback-autods-is-b2c]] holds).

**Stories, each with a number:**
1. First sale within 3 hours of copying the phone listing from one site to eBay. 7 years of e-commerce, then AutoDS: a "black screen" running 100 stores. It found trending products on Amazon Movers & Shakers and listed them on eBay at ~30% markup.
2. Tel Aviv conference: everyone showed strategies, he showed the black screen. He saved people's numbers as "eBay dropshipping 1, 2…". Beta 2 months later. No website and no billing system, so users got an invoice and paid for the past month.
3. The first partner left early. For ~1.5 years he did legal, marketing, dev and accounting alone. CTO options included a famous CTO 30 years older who would have guaranteed fundraising. He chose his friend Michael: "I decided that I want to enjoy the journey." Ofir joined for "some SEO questions": "I don't have work for you… okay, come join."
4. How the three decide: try to decide together; if they can't, Ofir decides in marketing, Michael in tech, and in a three-way conflict "I'm deciding because I'm the CEO." The title decides, not the shares.
5. COVID 2020: competitors froze and fired people, AutoDS went all in. Within a year "the whole dropshipping market was ours" and revenue grew 100%+ a year.
6. Buying the market leader: two brothers, business-school grads, older and funded. Offer, then "no"; a year later "maybe"; at 2 years they were close but walked; closed after ~2.5 years with ~10% of the business left. The earlier offer was ~10x the final price. The founder's reason: "he didn't have trust in me." Beers plus 2.5 years of proof built that trust. Since then: at least 2 more competitors bought, more with Fiverr's help.
7. Valuation by traffic, not an ARR multiple: if his traffic is worth X, their SimilarWeb at 200K means 2X. "Time, money and reputation." Paid cash plus an earn-out; the founders hit more than expected. Return: "thousands of percent".
8. PE deal: unsolicited, offer went from $20M to ~$38M at under $10M ARR ($5-10M), part cash, part retention, part milestones. It took 5-6 months and the company was messy because it was bootstrapped. Every question was about who to fire and which services to cut. "Optimize it, and then milk it." Early employees had worked for "half or even less than half of the market" salary. He walked one day before signing.
9. The straw that broke it: he asked for MORE money if he beat their ambitious revenue targets, and they refused. To him that proved they didn't want growth. The PE CEO was "very, very upset" and didn't keep the door open. He compared it with Assaf Rappaport of Wiz walking from Google, where Google kept the door open.
10. Night after night for 5-6 months he told his girlfriend, now his wife, "we are going to exit soon," then "no, I don't want this one." She backed him the whole way. His lesson: "I'm going to manage the business… as if I manage it forever", never dressing the slides for a buyer.
11. Banker #1: a big process, Boston, 24 meetings back to back. On bid day the email came that the whole 4-person team had moved to another bank. Their emails were blocked and the momentum was gone. "People buy founders, people buy people."
12. Banker #2 at CIBC, someone they already trusted, after passing $10M ARR. He asked the banker to promise he wouldn't leave. 50-60 presentations, multiple offers, some higher than Fiverr. Fiverr won on trust, reputation and the founders: whiskey at their nearby office at 8-9pm. "They're not buying the company to milk it."
13. Offers on the table: a Chinese company wanting 1%, buyers wanting 51%, a secondary for 20-30%. PE buyers wanted him gone in 6 months; serious and public buyers wanted 2-3 years. Seeing every term sheet is what let him decide whether he wanted to sell at all.
14. The earn-out freed him: "now it's not my money." He could focus on growth and employees with no owner-style EBITDA hoarding. He sold as "insurance", knowing he'd have made more by keeping it.
15. The exit watch: a Patek Philippe. He nearly walked out without it, even though it holds value. His wife: "It's your exit watch." The root is his mom, who still says "Money can disappear". His therapist's assignment was to walk into Lululemon and buy whatever he'd been thinking about. "It was a process." Fits [[feedback-lior-no-fear-confessions]], fear about money allowed.
16. After the exit: travel was the one big spend, plus wellness (WHOOP, meditation, ice baths, Tony Robbins in Fiji). He works harder than ever: "that's my game."
