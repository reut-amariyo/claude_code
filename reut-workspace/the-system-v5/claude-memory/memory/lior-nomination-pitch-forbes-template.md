## Structure that worked (7 blocks, in this order)
1. **Lead paragraph** = one-breath legend: self-taught, coding at 12, founded alone at 22, no investors until the exit, global #1, headcount. Every claim a number or a named entity.
2. **Hard fields**: age, DOB, role, company, URL.
3. **Role line** carries the validation: acquirer + acquirer's stature (Fiverr, NYSE, ~$1B cap). Borrowed authority.
4. **"What's unique / the innovation"**: explain the category for a lay judge FIRST, then the product A→Z, business model, platforms, acquisitions he made, scale stats.
5. **Childhood → entrepreneur**: immigrant parents, Beer Sheva Shchuna Daled, dad AC tech / mom supermarket, sister 13 yrs older in Canada. Hardship framed as fuel, never pity. Origin moment at 14: phone on a Chinese site vs eBay, first deal in hours, with ONE direct quote.
6. **Company growth arc**: founded alone, built for himself first, funded it with day jobs, co-founders join, free YouTube education + influencer partnerships as marketing, revenue ladder by year (2M 2020 → 5M 2022 → ~20M 2024).
7. **Personal fields**: education (none, self-taught = a feature), army (Intelligence, programmer → dev team lead), family status.

Judges' hooks it hits: underdog origin, bootstrapped, exit, global scale, numbers per year, a human detail at the end.

## Lesson from ice 40U40 2026, Reut 2026-10-25
Leave Forbes 30U30 out of short pitches to OTHER outlets' lists. A rival outlet's list makes the candidate look already covered, and lists want discoveries. Keep it for the follow-up interview, or for non-competing forms like conferences and awards.
