## Core ideas (external framework)

- Benign Violation Theory (Peter McGraw): humor = a violation + it's safe + both register at once.
- Golden Rule 1 — Be specific: niche inside jokes; "make the unsaid public" — say what the
  audience all thinks privately but never posts. (Orbach's Viral Post Generator: 2M users.)
- Golden Rule 2 — Be surprising: borrow a recognized format, insert your idea, deliver a twist.
  Deadpan delivery beats telegraphing the joke.
- 6-step launch: know audience → common ground → give the joke a job → start small →
  keep it real and simple → keep trying.
- Scaling formula once something lands: standardize topics, formats, voice (2-3 adjectives),
  and always/never content rules.
- Best surfaces: social, billboards, email subject lines, 404/career pages, blog (least
  competitive). 72% of people prefer funny brands; Fiverr funny job titles +91% page views.
- Never: sensitive subjects, punching down (unless underdog), humor everywhere at once,
  big swings before small tests, jokes disconnected from positioning.
- If not naturally funny: borrow from comedians, funny coworkers, community memes,
  industry creators, culture translation.

## How this applies to Lior (reconciled with existing brand rules)

- Humor for Lior = dry operator deadpan, never Motivator energy. Fits [ life is beta ]
  and the "cultural meme" visual family already approved in [[lior-top-8-10k-playbook]].
- "Make the unsaid public" IS the operator-confession pattern that already wins for us
  (the 93K MD→HTML post). Use it deliberately: what do SaaS founders with 200 employees
  all think but never post? That list is a hook goldmine.
- "Give the joke a job": in Lior content the job is always attention or relatability in
  line 1, never the payload. One idea per post still rules; the joke serves it.
- Deadpan delivery aligns with anti-AI rules: no telegraphing, no "haha", no exclamation
  pileups. Let the reader discover it.
- Punching down is already banned via [[feedback-lior-never-arrogant]], never criticize
  Shopify/Lovable. Lior at $92M exit is NOT an underdog; self-deprecation about past
  failures is the safe lane (matches vulnerability = real past failures only).
- Start small: test humor in X posts and replies before any LinkedIn big swing.
  Billboard Jul 29 is a surprise-format opportunity (cf. Exa "10^18 > 10^100", 22M views).
- Voice adjectives to standardize per the scaling formula: dry, specific, understated.

## Tom's hook formula, added 2026-08-30 at Reut's request

Reut: his hooks match our own rule about what works, on the content side.
**He shrinks the effort and inflates the result.** Every hook is a big promise attached to a
suspiciously small action.

His examples, as she sent them:
- "How I write *ridiculously* good B2B copy:"
- "I know it sounds \"stupid\" but if you want to go viral on LinkedIn..."
- "I tried this weird URL trick and got tons of new X followers 🧪"

### The mechanics, so it can be reproduced
1. **Effort word, deliberately small and unimpressive.** trick, thing, one line, weird,
   stupid, lazy, accidental, two minutes, by mistake. This is the whole engine. It removes
   the reader's excuse. If HE barely did anything, THEY could do it too.
2. **Result word, deliberately oversized.** ridiculously good, tons of, went viral, doubled.
   The gap between the two halves is the click.
3. **Self-deprecation carries the brag.** "I know it sounds stupid" pre-empts the eye-roll,
   which lets him make a large claim without arrogance. He never says he is smart, he says
   the thing is dumb and it worked anyway.
4. **A colon or an ellipsis, so the payload is withheld.** The hook promises, the body pays.
5. **Curiosity noun over category noun.** "this weird URL trick" beats "my LinkedIn strategy".
   Unnamed and specific at once.
6. **Deadpan.** No exclamation stack. One emoji maximum, used as a prop like the 🧪.

### Translating it to Lior without breaking his voice
- His version of the effort word is an operator decision that sounds like negligence:
  "I have never asked what time anyone starts." "I stopped doing the thing I was best at."
  The small action is a *choice a serious founder is not supposed to make*, which is our
  benign violation and his big-promise-small-effort at the same time.
- His version of the result word is his own real number: 200 people, the Fiverr exit,
  $20M+ ARR. Never inflated language on top of a real number, the number IS the inflation.
- **Do not import his self-deprecation about intelligence.** Lior's safe lane is past
  failures and weaknesses he owns, see [[feedback-lior-never-arrogant]] and
  [[feedback-lior-no-imposter-syndrome]]. "It sounds stupid" is fine. "I am an idiot" is not.
- Format cautions for LinkedIn: asterisk italics do not render, and parentheses are banned
  for us outside the line-2 counter-hook, see [[feedback-no-parentheses]]. Quotation marks
  around a word carry the same wink and cost nothing.
- This slots directly into the donor-structure rule, [[feedback-build-on-proven-structures]]:
  Tom is the hook donor even when the body structure comes from somebody else.

**Shape to reach for:** [small, almost embarrassing action] + [large, specific outcome] + [colon].
