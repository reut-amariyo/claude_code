Reut, 2026-10-20, after the $38M record post: "You improved a lot in copy, but you're still far behind in
choosing the graphic. In the end it's a hook like anything else."

**Why:** the record post ([[lior-post-built-to-sell-38m-record]]) ran on a plain podcast frame of Lior. Designed
cards flopped ([[feedback-skills-giveaway-flop-2026-10]]). In the same session I still proposed a polished
checklist card and then a text-heavy page screenshot for the next post. Reut had to correct both.

**How to apply:**
- Choose the image at the SAME time as the hook, not after the copy is done. Judge it with the hook test:
  does it stop the thumb in 1 second on a phone, with one face / one moment / one line?
- Default order: (1) real photo or video frame of Lior, candid, at most one short line of real subtitle;
  (2) original meme that continues a winner (e.g. the grandpa .md meme sequel); (3) first-party artifact as a
  document carousel (exit deck). Never: designed cards, text-heavy screenshots, diagrams of the post.
- Always give 2-3 options ranked, each tied to the past post it resembles and that post's numbers.
- After each post, log image type + results so picks are data-driven. Related: [[feedback-linkedin-visual-one-artifact-not-diagram]], [[linkedin-media-format-rules]].

**Audit 2026-10-20 (O-output/linkedin-visual-audit-2026-10-20.md):** 0 designed graphics in the top 16 posts; designed cards median
2,215 imp (n=12). Real Lior photo median 4,077 and 7 of the top 16. Winning memes are FAMOUS templates (Base44 147K, grandpa 93K,
Brad Pitt 30K); AI-generated cartoons sit at the bottom. Strongest rule: the image PROVES the hook (record post = Lior + Fiverr's
CEO under the Fiverr logo, not the podcast frame).
