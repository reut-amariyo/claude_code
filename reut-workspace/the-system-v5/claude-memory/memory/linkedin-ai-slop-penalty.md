# LinkedIn penalizes AI-sounding posts algorithmically (Aug 2026)

**Source:** Tom Orbach, Marketing Ideas newsletter, 2026-08-24. Reut sent it with "learn from
this, especially the LinkedIn parts". Numbers are his reporting, not our measurement.

**The facts:**
- LinkedIn added a "seems like AI slop" report option under the 3-dot menu on any post.
- It has been pressed 1M+ times. Posts LinkedIn classifies as slop get about **40% fewer views**.
- One phrasing LinkedIn reportedly suppresses automatically: **"it's not X, it's Y"**.
- Post analytics now shows a **warning when readers report your post** as AI.

**Why it matters:** [[feedback-anti-ai-writing-rules]] Rule 1 was a credibility rule - senior
readers smell AI. It is now also a distribution rule enforced by the platform. Sounding like AI
costs reach mechanically, on top of costing trust. Same logic strengthens the em-dash, the
"Honestly," opener, and the aphoristic-concept-naming bans: every AI tell raises slop-report risk,
and LinkedIn is the platform where Lior is measured on net new followers, so a 40% view haircut
kills the top of that funnel.

**How to apply:**
- Treat the anti-AI checklist as a hard publish gate on LinkedIn, never a style preference.
- After Lior publishes, check post analytics for an AI-report warning. If a post gets flagged,
  log which patterns it contained in the performance log and treat that draft shape as burned.
- A post that suddenly underperforms with normal engagement rate may be slop-classified, not
  throttled and not off-topic. That is a third diagnosis to consider alongside
  [[linkedin-interest-graph-2026]] topic mismatch and [[project-x-account-partial-limit]]-style throttling.
