**The 7 rules to apply:**
1. **Sell the dream, not the drill.** The emotional sell is the arc from one eBay store to the $92M exit. The product offer, like trying AutoDS for $1, rides behind it.
2. **Credibility insert.** Keep the proven tactical formats, like the startup-list carousel, but add ONE line of his seat: "from someone who built and sold a $92M company" / "kicked out of school at 14". Her example: the 10 startups carousel at 161 likes "could be 5,000". Proven tactic + lived experience = the winning combination. Use only verified facts from [[lior-pozin-os]]. Her "from his bedroom" line is NOT verified.
3. **Yes/No content pile.** YES requires all four: only Lior can say it / his face is in it / a first-time viewer knows it is him / it follows his speaking-style guidelines. Assume every viewer is seeing him for the first time.
4. **Animated reels test.** Put the same cartoon voiceover over hyper-realistic footage of his face or his childhood photos. A/B test the format against the story. Her bet: the story wins whatever plays under it. Ties to [[project-ai-animated-reels-the-promise]].
5. **One repeating, recognizable format.** Bethany Frankel shoots her face to camera off the cuff, so people stop because they know it is her. Lior needs his one ownable repeat.
6. **Make the signature ownable and searchable.** The beta shirt is the right instinct, but "beta" alone returns a million results and anyone can buy a beta shirt. Make it "the guy in the beta shirt", with a matching domain that redirects to Lior. Precedents: Hormozi's Acquisition.com chest, Jobs' glasses, Emily's own hair. Once it is set, NEVER change it. Wear it consistently, more than now.
7. **Podcasts are story mining.** A peer interviewer pulls the best stories: the failure, the near-bankruptcy, the day after the exit. Reuse every answer as content. She offered an intro to Richard, a UK billionaire whose podcast is filmed driving in his car. Name unverified.
