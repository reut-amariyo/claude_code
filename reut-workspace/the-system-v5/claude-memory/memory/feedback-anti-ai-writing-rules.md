# Anti-AI Writing & Voice Rules for Lior Posts

**Why:** Reut caught multiple AI-watermark patterns and voice missteps across drafts. Senior LinkedIn audience smells AI from a mile away. Every one of these patterns kills credibility instantly. Apply to every post, every reply, every comment.

**How to apply:** After drafting, scan for each rule below. If you find a hit, rewrite. Read the post out loud as the final test.

---

## Rule 1 — Kill the "It's not X, it's Y" contrast pattern

THE most recognizable AI watermark. ChatGPT defaults to this construction constantly.

**Banned templates:**
- "The real X isn't Y. It's Z."
- "People don't want X. They want Y."
- "It's not about X. It's about Y."
- "Stop X. Start Y."

**Examples Reut killed:**
- ❌ "The real conversion killer isn't friction. It's guilt."
- ❌ "People don't want optimized checkouts. They want permission to spend."

**The fix:** Use a single declarative sentence instead. Or merge the pivot into one line.
- ✅ "guilt kills conversions"
- ✅ "the real lever was psychological the entire time"

**Watch out:** Subtler forms ("When X happens, the brain Y. But when Z happens, the brain Q.") are the same DNA. Avoid back-to-back contrast structures even when words differ.

**Now algorithmically enforced (added 2026-08-24, source: Tom Orbach newsletter):** LinkedIn's
"seems like AI slop" report button has been pressed 1M+ times, slop-classified posts get about
40% fewer views, and "it's not X, it's Y" is reportedly one of the phrasings LinkedIn suppresses
automatically. This rule now costs reach, not just credibility. Post analytics shows a warning
when readers report a post as AI - check it after publishing. See [[linkedin-ai-slop-penalty]].

## Rule 2 — No LinkedIn cringe philosophy

Aphoristic "deep" lines that reduce a complex idea to a clever metaphor. Sounds profound, reads as posturing. Lior is a cynical CEO who gets it, not a discount philosopher.

**Banned moves:**
- "X isn't just Y. It's Z." (double-banned with Rule 1)
- Two-word concept names ("placebo power of guilt relief", "art of perceived value")
- Renaming a phenomenon to sound smart
- "That's the [adjective] [noun] of [thing]" constructions

**The fix:** State the operator observation plainly. Cynicism > philosophy.

---

## Rule 3 — Structure: the 1:3:1 rhythm

Don't write a poem (line, line, line, line). The eye gets tired. Choppiness reads as AI list-mode.

**The pattern:** 1 line (punchy) → BREAK → 3 lines (paragraph that flows) → BREAK → 1 line → BREAK → 3 lines.

**The test:** Read the post out loud. If you keep losing breath because every line is short, structure is broken. Merge into 3-line paragraphs and let breaks land.

**Caveat:** Lists of 3-4 items (rapid-fire) are exempt — different rhythm device.

---

## Rule 4 — Odd numbers > even numbers in hooks

The brain locks onto odd numbers harder. Apply to:
- Stats in the hook (11% beats 12%)
- "X reasons / mistakes / lessons" lists (3, 5, 7 — not 4 or 6)
- Specific examples ("3 things" beats "4 things")

**Caveat:** If real data is even (12%, 70%), don't fabricate. Use as-is.

---

## Rule 5 — CTAs must be effortless

Reader must answer in under 1 second without mental work. If CTA requires translating Lior's example to their world, half won't bother.

**Bad (requires analogy):**
- ❌ "What's the $5 silicone case in your store?" (analogy mapping required)
- ❌ "How does this apply to your business?" (vague + work)

**Good (instant):**
- ✅ "What's the most boring item in your inventory?"
- ✅ "What did your team do this week that surprised you?"
- ✅ "Curse or hidden opportunity?" (binary)

---

## Rule 6 — Read it out loud

After every draft, read the full post out loud as if Lior is saying it on a podcast. Listen for:
- Where you lose breath → structure too choppy (Rule 3)
- Where you stumble → not natural speech, rewrite
- Where it sounds like TED talk → philosophy alarm (Rule 2)
- Where it sounds like a textbook → AI explainer voice, rewrite as operator
- Where you'd never actually say it that way → cut it

If Lior wouldn't say a sentence on a real podcast, he shouldn't say it on LinkedIn either.

---

## Rule 7 — Never use em dashes, en dashes, or double dashes

Use a regular hyphen (-) instead. Em dash (—) and en dash (–) are AI tells. Don't match Reut's natural style.

**How to apply:** When a sentence calls for the rhythm of an em dash, rewrite (split into two sentences, use a comma, or use a colon) instead of inserting one.

---

## Rule 8 — "I" hooks: banned by default, ONE exception

**Default:** First line (hook) of any post/reply/comment does NOT start with "I", "I'm", "I've", "I was", etc.

**Why:** Inward-pointed hooks make readers scroll faster. The hook is the only line competing for the swipe — lead with what the reader cares about, not who Lior is.

**Lead instead with:**
- A stat ("Most founders waste an hour a day...")
- A pattern ("Every new AI chat starts the same...")
- A scene ("3 AM. The pricing engine just broke.")
- The reader ("You know what kills your output...")
- A named entity ("Shopify just removed over 10,000 of our reviews.")

### The ONE exception — Provocative-Confessional "I" (confirmed 2026-05-16 via top-8 audit)

**Allowed:** "I" opener when the verb is provocative-confessional AND the body is build-in-public truth (including what failed).

**Allowed examples:**
- ✅ "I set up AI agents to replace my entire team. Including me. And let them run." (30K imp)
- ✅ "I fired our top sales rep last week."
- ✅ "I killed the feature my best customers were using."
- ✅ "I made the mistake of [bold action]."
- ✅ "I gave up [thing] for 30 days."

**Provocative-confessional verbs that earn the "I":** set up / fired / killed / replaced / gave up / quit / shut down / made the mistake of / paid $X to learn / spent a year doing the wrong thing

**Still banned (vanity "I"):**
- ❌ "I built X and scaled to $Y."
- ❌ "I learned that…"
- ❌ "I believe…"
- ❌ "I'm excited to share…"
- ❌ "I just hit [milestone]…"

**The test:** Could a humble-bragging founder use this exact "I" hook? If yes → vanity → kill. Does the "I" verb make the reader instinctively think "wait, what?" → confession → allowed.

Mid-post "I" is fine as personal proof anchor (see Rule 9). Applies to LinkedIn, X, Bluesky, Threads, IG captions — every platform.

---

## Rule 9 — Reduce first-person "I"

Cut "I/me/my" to the minimum. Reframe to "you/your" wherever possible. Personal anecdotes earn their place when load-bearing (one specific moment as a hook), but the BULK of the post must speak TO the reader, not ABOUT Lior.

**Why:** Less "I" = more pulling. People scroll past content that feels like someone else's diary. They stop on content that sounds like it's about THEM.

**How to apply:**
- Default voice = second person ("you", "your")
- After drafting, count "I" + "me" + "my" + "I'm" + "I've". If more than ~3 in a sub-1,000-char post, rewrite.
- Convert "I learned that X" → "X is the thing most founders miss."
- Convert "I believe Y" → "Y is true."
- Keep ONE personal anchor (the story that proves you've earned the lesson). Rest = reader-focused.
- Applies to LinkedIn, X, IG captions, Bluesky, comments, replies.

---

## Rule 10 — Never open a sentence with "Honestly"

Banned in every Lior draft (confirmed by Reut 2026-07-13, DotDev post session). "Honestly," as a sentence opener is filler sincerity — it implies the rest of the post isn't honest, and it's an AI/influencer tell.

**How to apply:** Delete the word; the sentence almost always stands alone. "Honestly, I'm just excited" → "I'm just excited." Same treatment for cousins used as sincerity filler: "Frankly," / "Truthfully," / "To be honest,".

---

## Rule 11 — Banned AI vocabulary (the red list)

Added 2026-08-29 from a viral LinkedIn post Reut flagged ("12 AI tells to delete"). These are
single words and stock phrases that out a draft as AI-written before anyone reads the idea.
Treat as a hard find-and-kill list on every draft, in every language of output.

**Banned words:**
- delve
- crucial, pivotal
- tapestry
- robust, seamless, realm
- also still on the list from earlier sessions: leverage, unlock, landscape, journey (as metaphor), navigate (as metaphor)

**Banned stock phrases:**
- "Here's the thing"
- "Hope this helps"
- "After careful consideration"
- "Just to provide a quick update" / "to give a quick update" - give the update
- "Most people..." as a hook - lazy oversimplification, and it flags as generic

**Banned move - adverb abuse:** "X quietly runs Y", "quietly became", "steadily grew".
The adverb is doing work the verb should do. Cut the adverb or pick a real verb.

**Banned move - the reframe couplet:** "This isn't a budget. It's a statement of intent."
Same DNA as Rule 1, different clothes. Any two-sentence pattern where sentence 2 renames
sentence 1 at a higher altitude is dead on arrival.

**The test:** would Lior say this word out loud, on a podcast, in his second language?
He has never said "delve", "tapestry", or "realm" in his life. Neither should his posts.

## Quick scan checklist before publishing

Before any Lior post ships:

1. [ ] No "It's not X, it's Y" patterns (Rule 1)
2. [ ] No two-word concept-naming or aphoristic philosophy (Rule 2)
3. [ ] Structure has paragraph breath (1:3:1), not poem-mode (Rule 3)
4. [ ] Hook number is odd if data allows (Rule 4)
5. [ ] CTA answerable in <1 second without translation (Rule 5)
6. [ ] Passes the read-out-loud test (Rule 6)
7. [ ] Zero em dashes, en dashes, double dashes — only hyphens (Rule 7)
8. [ ] Hook does NOT start with "I" — UNLESS provocative-confessional verb (set up / fired / killed / gave up / made the mistake of) AND body is build-in-public truth (Rule 8)
9. [ ] "I/me/my" count ≤3 in a sub-1,000-char post (Rule 9)
10. [ ] No sentence opens with "Honestly" or similar sincerity filler (Rule 10)
11. [ ] Red-list sweep: no delve / crucial / pivotal / tapestry / robust / seamless / realm, no "Here's the thing" / "Hope this helps" / "After careful consideration" / "Most people...", no adverb abuse, no reframe couplet (Rule 11)
12. [ ] Slop-risk sweep: every AI tell above is also a LinkedIn reach penalty now (see [[linkedin-ai-slop-penalty]])

If any one fails, fix it before publishing. These are hard rules, not preferences.
