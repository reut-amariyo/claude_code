## Structure Patterns

### 1. Short-Punchy (under 300 words)
One hook, one insight, one line to land it. No filler.

**When it works:** Strong standalone insight. Lior's voice of authority.
**Typical length:** 50-300 words.

### 2. Long-Story (800-1500 words)
Narrative arc: setup → tension → turn → lesson. Reader invests because they care about what happened.

**When it works:** Lior has a genuine story with a turn. Must be true, specific, emotionally honest.

#### Justin Welsh — 2026-04-18 — 2,454 likes, 879 comments (Existential Story variation)
**Hook:** "You hit the big goal. Now what?"
**Body pattern (the Justin Welsh signature):**
1. Existential question (1 sentence)
2. Generalization ("very few people think about this")
3. Personal setup ("I've spent two decades...")
4. **Rhythm of 3** — three short parallel items ("A sales team to train. A revenue goal. A business to grow.")
5. Self-description (what you became)
6. Pivot ("Then I figured it out...")
7. **Rhythm of 3 again** (mirrors step 4)
8. Acknowledge success
9. **Turn — the paradox** (the emotional reveal that reframes success)
10. 3 introspective questions stacked
11. Emotional naming ("that's a weird place to be")
12. Story anchor (a specific moment — "friend asked me over dinner")
13. **Validation frame** ("If you've ever...")
14. CTA — newsletter (or, for Lior: open question / reflection close — see [project-lior-channels.md](project-lior-channels.md))
15. Soft tease close

**Justin's voice signatures (tag for future posts of his):**
- Rhythm of 3 used twice per post
- Short sentences mixed with longer reflective ones
- Lists of introspective questions
- "If you've ever..." validation hook
- Soft CTA ("may change how you think"), never pushy

**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/justinwelsh_you-hit-the-big-goal-now-what-thats-activity-7450872788380479488-EZbT
**⚠ Adaptation note for Lior:** Replace newsletter CTA with open question or reflection close. See [project-lior-channels.md](project-lior-channels.md).

#### Lior Pozin — 2026-04-19 — 30K impressions (Experiment Narrative variation)
**Hook:** "I set up AI agents to replace my entire team. Including me. And let them run."
**Body pattern (the "Experiment Narrative" variation — short Long-Story, ~250 words):**
1. Hook = experiment premise in 3 lines (action + self-deprecation + surrender)
2. Time anchor + tool name ("For 2 weeks, I've been using... Paperclip.")
3. Tool description in 2 short parallel sentences ("You sit on the board. They run the company.")
4. Initial enthusiasm beat ("Day one, I was hooked.")
5. Cascade of action ("CEO agent → CTO → engineers") — shows the system in motion
6. **Tension flip** ("Until I checked the output.") — single sentence pivot
7. **Memorable analogy** ("blurry copy of a blurry copy" / "telephone game") — the line readers will quote
8. Mechanism breakdown ("Every handoff dropped context.")
9. **The insight in parallel sentences** ("Real people push back. Agents don't. They just execute the drift.")
10. Universal lesson ("AI can do everything except apply your judgment.")
11. Reframe + tool fairness ("Paperclip is a great tool... But the 'zero human company' story? Marketing.")
12. **Open gap close** — rhetorical question, not generic ("Is this not the greatest time in history to be building?")

**Voice signatures to keep for Lior:**
- Short sentences. Two-word sentences ok ("Including me.").
- Parallel structure ("Real people push back. Agents don't.")
- One memorable analogy per post (telephone game, blurry copy)
- Tool gets a fair shake before contrarian reframe — credibility move
- No "Read that again" or arrow-listicle templates
**Why it works:** Personal experiment = automatic stake. Naming the tool = credibility. The turn ("Until I checked the output") is one sentence — fast tension shift. Telephone game analogy is the quotable line. Contrarian close ("zero human company story? Marketing") gives the reader a position to share. Final question is genuinely open, not a forced CTA.
**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/liorpozin_i-set-up-ai-agents-to-replace-my-entire-team-activity-7450172732035457024-4YOo
**When to use this variation:** Lior tested an AI tool / system / process for 1-4 weeks and has a contrarian conclusion. Requires a real experiment, not a theoretical take.

### 3. Listicle-N (numbered list, usually 3/5/7/10)
Promise in hook → N items delivered → tie-back conclusion.

**When it works:** Tactical/educational content. Reader wants the goods fast.

#### Ruben Hassid — 2026-04-18 — 1,944 likes, 263 comments
**Hook:** "The $20/month Claude plan is enough. But only if you stop making these 17 mistakes: 1: You upload PDFs raw. One page = 3,000 tokens."
**Body pattern:**
1. Bold claim + "but only if" contrarian twist
2. Oddly-specific number (17 — not 10, not 20)
3. Item #1 inside the hook (above-the-fold value, no click-to-expand required)
4. 17 numbered items, each 1-2 lines: mistake + specific why
**Length:** long (17 items × ~2 lines each)
**Two techniques to steal:**
- **Odd number**: "11 lessons" beats "10 lessons" — signals counted, not rounded
- **Item #1 in hook**: deliver value before the reader has to commit. This is the single biggest driver of read-through on listicle posts.
**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ruben-hassid_the-20month-claude-plan-is-enough-but-activity-7450770082126819328-RRmx

### 4. Before-After (Transformation)
Where we were → what changed → where we are. Concrete metrics on both ends.

**When it works:** Lior has clean before/after data. Revenue, team size, process time.

### 5. Framework (Named Model)
Name a framework → break down its parts → apply it. Reader leaves with a tool.

**When it works:** Lior has a repeatable system. The naming is the magic — gives it stickiness.

### 6. Tutorial / How-To
Steps 1-N with specificity. Reader can act immediately.

**When it works:** Tactical SaaS/hiring/ops content. Must be executable, not abstract.

### 7. Opinion-With-Evidence
Strong claim → 2-3 evidence points from experience → sharp close.

**When it works:** When Lior disagrees with industry consensus and has the receipts.

#### Bruno Estrella — 2026-04-18 — 1,099 likes, 162 comments
**Hook:** "The future of growth marketing is go-to-market engineering. I say this as someone who has led growth marketing at startups for the past 10 years."
**Body pattern (the "Coin-a-Term" variation):**
1. Bold future claim (1 sentence)
2. Credibility anchor (1 sentence, disarms "who are you to say this")
3. Original promise of the field ("when X started, the promise was...")
4. Reality check — concrete list of what the work actually became
5. **Pivot line** ("The world where that made sense no longer exists.")
6. Explanation of why the old model broke (AI, automation, agents)
7. New definition ("In {year}, {field} means...")
8. Concrete examples of the new work
9. **Coin/name the thing** as the final line ("That's GTM Engineering.")
**Close:** Single declarative sentence that names a concept people can adopt and debate.
**Why it works:** Coining a term gives the post a "handle" readers reuse. Creates identity-debate ("am I still a X or am I a Y?") which drives comments. The credibility anchor upfront neutralizes resistance before the bold claim can trigger it.
**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/brunoestrella_the-future-of-growth-marketing-is-go-to-market-activity-7447287934724153345-Ags8
**Meta-lesson:** This variation only works if the coined term is genuinely useful and not already in circulation. Without a real new handle, the same structure reads as a generic opinion post and vanishes.

### 8. Question-Cascade
Open with a question → body is a series of follow-up questions → close with a reframe.

**When it works:** Topics where the reader needs to think, not be told.

### 9. Rant-Controlled
Builds tension through 3-5 escalating statements → release with a reframe.

**When it works:** Pet peeves. Must land the release — bare rants read as complaints.

### 10. List-of-Contrasts
"What looks like X is actually Y." Pattern repeated 5-10 times.

**When it works:** Reframing common misconceptions. Very shareable.

### 11. Dramatized-Dialogue
Two characters in an imagined conversation. Opens mid-tension, escalates through Q&A, reveals deeper truth, ends with a "PS:" reframe that generalizes the lesson.

**When it works:** Universal workplace dynamics (manager/IC, CEO/VP, founder/investor). The format itself is a pattern-break on LinkedIn. Strongest when the "character" speaking (Head of X, CFO, etc.) represents a perspective the audience rarely hears internally.

**Typical length:** 300-500 words.

#### Conor Paulsen — 2026-04-18 — 834 likes, 138 comments
**Hook:** "Head of Sales: I'm firing Marcus tomorrow. CEO: You mean our number 1 rep? Are you insane?"
**Body pattern:**
- Opens mid-scene with provocative premise
- 8-10 alternating dialogue exchanges
- Each exchange escalates + adds specific proof (Henderson deal, Jessica's exit interview, 22% team drop)
- Tension resolves with decisive one-word reply ("Do it.")
- PS: one-line universal reframe
**Close:** "PS: Your top performer is either lifting the team up or holding it down."
**Why it works:** Dialogue is extreme pattern-break on LinkedIn. Mid-tension open forces continued reading. Specific details give credibility. PS lets every manager feel the lesson.
**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/conor-paulsen_head-of-sales-im-firing-marcus-tomorrow-activity-7450913481471528960-3lWE
