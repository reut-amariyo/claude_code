# Social Media Post Writing Style

## The Real Person Principle (ALL PLATFORMS - Most Important Rule)

People don't follow accounts for opinions. They follow real people on a real path, just like them. This applies to X, LinkedIn, Instagram, Bluesky - everywhere.

- **Stories beat opinions.** A specific moment always outperforms a generic take
- **Progress beats perfection.** The messy middle creates more connection than polished advice
- **Journey content compounds.** Chains of updates showing real progress build invested audiences
- **Forced content fails.** If the idea doesn't come naturally, the audience can tell. Best posts come from genuine sparks
- **The test:** Does this show Lior as a real person on the same path as the reader? If not, add a real story, a real number, or a real moment

## Key Patterns (from reference examples)

### Structure
1. **Personal hook** - Start with personal experience ("I've been trying...")
2. **Value statement** - What's great about it and why
3. **Concrete example** - Show a real use case with specifics
4. **Expansion** - Hint there's more ("And there's a lot more")
5. **CTA** - Call to action with link ("If you want to try it, sign up here")

### Tone
- Conversational and authentic (not salesy)
- Enthusiastic but grounded in real experience
- First person, personal perspective
- Short sentences, easy to scan

### Formatting & Visual Rhythm
- Line breaks between ideas (not a wall of text)
- Use "Here's an example..." to introduce specifics
- End with a clear call to action + link
- Keep it concise - every sentence earns its place
- NEVER use em dash (—) - always use a regular hyphen-dash (-)
- **Vary line lengths like a rhythm** - alternate short, medium, and long lines
- Short punchy lines (2-5 words) for impact
- Longer lines (15-25 words) for context and explanation
- Then short again - creates a visual "glide" effect
- If all text blocks look the same size, rewrite - the post needs visual variety
- Think of it as: long, medium, short, long, short, medium, short, long - like music
- The post should look "beautiful" when you zoom out - varied block sizes, not uniform
- One-word lines can be powerful closers

## Length & Brevity
- **Shorter is almost always better** - cut aggressively
- Keep only the emotional core + the key facts
- If a post has a list of details, compress into one punchy line (e.g. "240+ employees. An exit to @Fiverr. Helping thousands make a living online.")
- Don't over-explain - trust the reader to fill in gaps
- Hiring posts / CTAs: don't list every role - just say "We're hiring" and point to the link
- A great post is 6-8 lines, not 15-20

### "AI agents Paperclip" post - 10,000 impressions, 12 likes, 0 comments
- **Topic:** Testing Paperclip (AI agent company simulator) for 2 weeks, finding that multi-agent handoffs lose context like a telephone game
- **Why impressions were high:**
  - Viral topic (AI agents replacing humans) stops the scroll
  - Strong narrative arc: hook, build-up, twist, insight
  - Balanced take (not hype, not doom) = credible
- **Why engagement was low:**
  - Closed ending ("Humans aren't going anywhere" + "greatest time to be building") - people agree silently, nothing to add
  - Post delivers the full answer: story + problem + conclusion = no gap for the reader to fill
  - Too balanced - no friction, no debate trigger
- **Fix:** End with an open question or a sharper take. Leave a gap the reader wants to fill. "Article" format = impressions. "Conversation starter" format = comments.
- **Pattern:** High-impression low-engagement = strong content but closed loop. Always leave an entry point for the reader.

## Winning Posts (High Performance)

### "Israel #1 in AI usage" post - 12,000 impressions
- **Topic:** Anthropic ranking Israel #1 in global AI usage, Israeli founder culture of speed
- **Why it worked:**
  - Strong data hook (Anthropic ranked Israel #1) - concrete, credible, shareable
  - National pride angle drives shares and comments
  - Short punchy lines with great rhythm ("Ship. Iterate.")
  - Reframes the data into an insight ("adoption speed is the real edge")
  - No self-promo - purely value/opinion content
  - Relatable to founders and tech audience
- **Pattern:** Data point + cultural insight + reframe = high distribution

### "3 employees quit" post - 4,000 impressions (with Gemini graphic)
- **Topic:** 3 employees left AutoDS to start their own businesses, framed as a culture win
- **Why it worked:**
  - Counterintuitive hook ("3 employees quit" + "I'm writing this with a smile") creates instant tension
  - Flips a negative (people leaving) into a positive (culture proof)
  - Humor element (cigarette/condom joke) makes it shareable
  - Self-deprecating closer ("worst retention strategy or best proof") = likeable
  - Authentic founder vulnerability - not hiding attrition, reframing it
  - No self-promo, no CTA - pure story/values content
  - Gemini graphic paired with it (visual + text combo)
- **Pattern:** Counterintuitive hook + reframe + humor + values statement = strong engagement

## Winning Lanes (data-confirmed from performance log — approved 2026-06-01)

### The contrarian-AI lane (n=3, confirmed repeatable — Lior's highest-reach lane)
- **Confirmed by:** Paperclip (30K imp) → Anthropic (92K) → Google/Base44-killer (144K, all-time record). Each iteration went higher.
- **The formula a winning AI post MUST follow:**
  1. Name a trending AI tool in the **first 7-11 words** (news-anchor hook)
  2. Add a **contrarian or personal frame** — founder-fear, a failure report, or an insider scoop
  3. **No tactical "how-to" / productivity tips** in the body
  4. Minimal CTA or none (a bare "Agree?" works on a universal stake)
- **The failure mode (never do this):** a news-anchor hook with a tactical/prescription body (checklists, "use X", connector counts). It reads as vendor marketing and bombs every time — confirmed by Master Prompt (546), find-skills (792), and Microsoft+Anthropic-workshop (1,112 🔴) — all in the AI lane but tactical, all flopped.
- **Next improvement to test:** graft one concrete AutoDS production scene/number into the body (the only ingredient the record posts haven't tried yet).

### Hiring-as-gift (the only way a hiring CTA wins)
- **Confirmed by:** Tough-days-Israeli-tech (13,400 imp, 15 reposts — most-shared post in the log) vs. "I got rejected → Director of Partnerships" (2,066 🔴, bait-and-switch).
- **Rule:** a hiring CTA is allowed **only** when the post delivers full value FIRST (empathy, insight, a real story), and the ask is phrased as a **gift to the reader** — "my DMs are open, send me a CV, I'll route it personally" — never as a role spec with "link in comments."
- The hiring ask must read as an offer of help layered onto a complete post, never as the payload the hook baited.

## Content Type Insights (Algorithm vs. Audience)

### Teaser/hype posts get low impressions but high like ratio
- Example: "We're releasing the shark. March 19th." got 40 likes / 700 impressions (5.7% like rate - very high)
- The writing WORKED - people who saw it engaged hard
- But LinkedIn suppressed distribution because:
  - "Coming soon" / launch teaser posts read as ads to the algorithm
  - No concrete value or educational content = less algorithmic push
  - No engagement hook (question, poll, "comment below") = fewer comments = less distribution
  - Mystery/hype without substance doesn't get reshared
- **Lesson:** If writing a teaser, pair it with ONE concrete insight or data point so LinkedIn sees it as valuable content, not just promo. Or add a genuine question to drive comments

## Anti-Patterns (Learned from underperforming posts)

### Don't: Hook with a flex
- "Someone else just answered a full magazine interview as me" = brag disguised as story
- Reader immediately senses "this is about how great I am" and scrolls past
- **Fix:** Hook with tension, surprise, or something the reader relates to

### Don't: Write a post that serves your ego, not your audience
- 90% "look what I did" + 10% generic takeaways = low engagement
- The best posts make the reader feel smarter, not the writer
- **Fix:** Lead with value. What can the reader USE today?

### Don't: Give generic AI advice as takeaways
- "AI is a team member", "context is ROI", "build a system" - said thousands of times
- **Fix:** Give one specific, actionable insight they haven't heard before

### Don't: Make claims without proof
- "It answered exactly like me" with zero evidence creates a credibility gap
- **Fix:** Show a screenshot, a before/after, or one concrete Q&A example

### Don't: Switch from story to listicle mid-post
- Building a narrative then dropping into a numbered list kills momentum
- **Fix:** Either commit to story format or list format, don't hybrid

### Don't: End with a prediction instead of engagement
- "A year from now..." invites no action from the reader
- **Fix:** End with a question, a CTA, or a link

### Don't: Close the loop completely on viral topics
- When a post gives hook + story + problem + full conclusion, the reader has nothing to add
- Agreeable statements ("Humans aren't going anywhere") get nods, not comments
- **Fix:** Leave one gap open. End with a question or a sharper take that invites pushback. High impressions + low engagement = the content worked but the ending sealed the conversation shut

### Don't: Write too long with uniform blocks
- If the post is a wall of same-sized paragraphs, it reads heavy
- **Fix:** Cut by 40%. Apply short-long-short rhythm. Every line earns its place

## Reply/Comment Writing Rules (X, LinkedIn, Bluesky)

### Golden Rule: Every comment MUST include a real story from Lior's life
- Never write a generic opinion comment. Always anchor it in something Lior actually lived.
- Include at least ONE of these in every reply:
  - **A specific number he tracked** ("We tested this - conversion went from 2.1% to 4.7%")
  - **A client/customer story** ("Had a seller on our platform who did exactly this...")
  - **A concrete personal experience** ("When we scaled from 50 to 200 people, this broke first")
  - **A real decision he made** ("I stopped doing X at AutoDS after seeing Y")
  - **A timestamp** ("3 years ago we tried this. Here's what happened.")
- The story doesn't need to be long - even one line of real experience beats three lines of opinion
- This is what separates Lior's comments from every other founder commenting generic takes
- Pull from: 9 years building AutoDS, exit to Fiverr, hiring 300+ people, eCommerce/dropshipping, scaling teams, pricing experiments, delegation frameworks
