**Where the company is, culturally:**
- **Growing up.** It started as a bootstrapped founder-led company and now has about 200 people. Leadership's message:
  **"mid-management is the driver of this company"**. C-level is there for vision, decisions and hiring.
  Managers are asked to act "like founders" and bring initiatives, not just execute.
- **What leadership admits is broken:** innovation dried up, no cross-team brainstorming, "too
  corporate", processes that cost speed. Employees say yes too much and fear giving feedback upward.
  Ideas come from the top, not bottom-up.
- **The speed DNA is still the ideal.** Lior prefers fast and messy over a month of waiting, and he
  benchmarks against "two guys in their parents' apartment".
- **The honest tension:** managers fear "a ship without a captain", especially with Lior in Miami in
  December 2026. Decisions get stuck in endless meetings with no owner or action items. Tomer, head of
  Product, said it out loud in front of the founders and Lior agreed publicly. People here push back
  on the founder in the room, and that is normal.
- **Leadership standards being adopted:** manage up with data, partner with peers under pressure,
  manager = teacher who shows the standard once and gives feedback in real time. Trust = authentic +
  empathic, meaning you care about their success, "not soft", + convincing with data and being open to
  being convinced. Ask "What am I missing?". Every meeting owner closes with agreed / not agreed / who decides.
- **AI-era leadership:** leaders set the standard and audit the quality while AI does the middle.
  Agents count on the org chart. "Show me something you built with AI in two weeks" beat trainings.
- **Business self-image:** "we are half an affiliate company". Shopify is the #1 partner, and
  diversifying affiliate offers is a 2027 lever.
- **Warm, family texture:** weddings, babies, family trips, coaching kids. Remote people value in-person time.
