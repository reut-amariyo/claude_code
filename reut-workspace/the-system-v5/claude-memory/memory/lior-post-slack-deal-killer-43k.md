# Lior's Top LinkedIn Post — Slack-as-PM Deal Killer

**Topic:** During M&A due diligence, a founder told Lior he runs his company on Slack pings. That single line killed the deal. Lior turns it into a verdict on what makes a company investable.
**Performance:** 43K impressions (confirmed by Reut 2026-05-04 as a winning reference).

## Full text (canonical reference)

```
He said: 'I just Slack my team to get stuff done.'
That's the moment I knew we'd never acquire his company.

Last year, I was looking at buying a company.

As part of my due diligence, I asked the founder how he managed tasks and goals.

👉 "I just ping people in Slack."

That sounds like hustle.
But to an operator or investor, it sounds like chaos.

At AutoDS - Automatic Dropshipping Tools, even from early on, we had:

- OKRs across departments
- Feedback loops with customers
- SOPs and playbooks for everything from onboarding to retention
- A management system to track and improve all of it

A business that runs on DMs isn't scalable.
It's not investable.
And it sure as hell isn't sellable.

Your systems are part of your product.
Because the way you run your company is 50% of its valuation.

If you want an exit one day…
Get your house in order now.

Your systems are part of your product.
Treat them that way.

Also, founder or no founder - if your idea of project management is Slack, you're a walking business red flag...

Agree or disagree? 👇
```

## Why it works — the 8-beat architecture

1. **Quote-as-hook + verdict in line 2.** "He said: 'I just Slack my team to get stuff done.' / That's the moment I knew we'd never acquire his company." A real quote (in quotes) followed by an irreversible decision. The reader has to find out who, why, what.
2. **One-line scene-setter.** "Last year, I was looking at buying a company." Instantly establishes Lior is the buyer in the room — operator standing.
3. **The triggering question + the killer line (with arrow emoji).** "I asked the founder how he managed tasks and goals. 👉 'I just ping people in Slack.'" The arrow makes the quote scannable. The question is mundane on purpose — the answer is what kills him.
4. **Steelman → reframe.** "That sounds like hustle. But to an operator or investor, it sounds like chaos." Two short parallel lines. Validates why the founder thinks this is fine, then flips it.
5. **Operator proof: 4-bullet "what we built instead."** OKRs / feedback loops / SOPs / management system. Shows Lior didn't just judge — he built the alternative. This is the only place AutoDS gets named, and it's framed as evidence, not promotion.
6. **Triple-parallel verdict (rule of three).** "Not scalable. Not investable. And it sure as hell isn't sellable." Each line shorter and harder than the last. "Sure as hell" is the voice break.
7. **Quotable thesis (repeated twice for emphasis).** "Your systems are part of your product." Said once mid-post, repeated at the end. The repetition is the move — it's the takeaway he wants screenshotted.
8. **Voice-break sting + binary CTA.** "if your idea of project management is Slack, you're a walking business red flag…" + "Agree or disagree? 👇" — the sting gives detractors something to push back on, the binary question forces a side.

## What makes this post different from Shopify and Nike

- **Mini-narrative form.** Not a crisis post (Shopify) and not a case study (Nike). It's a 2-character scene with a verdict.
- **Direct AutoDS name-drop is allowed here** because it's framed as proof of the alternative, not as a sales pitch. The bullets describe operating practice, not product features.
- **Repeats the thesis line twice.** Most posts say it once. This one repeats "Your systems are part of your product" — the repetition signals "this is the line, take it."
- **Closes with a sting, not a story.** The "walking business red flag" line is provocative on purpose. Senior founders who run on Slack will defend themselves in comments = engagement.
- **Uses one quote as a hook.** A line in actual quote marks is rare in Lior's posts and very effective.
- **Numbers are absent — judgment is present.** No revenue stats, no impressions data. The credibility comes from "I was the buyer in this deal" + 4 specific operating practices.

## What this post does NOT do (still applies)

- Does NOT name the target company, the founder, or the deal — protects relationships.
- Does NOT pitch AutoDS the product — names AutoDS only as the "what we did instead" backdrop.
- Does NOT use em dashes (uses regular hyphens).
- Does NOT use arrow listicles (the single 👉 is a quote pointer, not a list).
- Does NOT include a hiring CTA or "200 employees" stat.

## Confirmed winning archetype: "Deal-Killer Moment + Operator Verdict"

**Pattern:**
> Quote-as-hook + irreversible verdict in line 2 → one-line scene set ("Last year, I was…") → triggering question + the killer quote (with 👉) → steelman the founder's view, then flip it → 4-bullet "what we built instead" (operating practices, not product) → triple-parallel verdict (rule of three: not X, not Y, not Z) → quotable thesis line, repeated twice → voice-break sting + binary CTA.

**Why it works for senior SaaS founders:**
Every founder in the audience has either had a deal die in due diligence or knows one that did. The post puts them in the room with the buyer. The lesson — "your systems are 50% of your valuation" — is the kind of thing they'd pay an investment banker to tell them, dropped for free in a 200-word post. And the sting at the end ("walking business red flag") is calibrated to make the half of the audience who actually does run on Slack defend themselves in comments.

**Required ingredients:**
1. Real quote in quote marks as the hook — must be short and self-incriminating
2. Irreversible-decision second line ("That's the moment I knew…")
3. Lior in a position of authority in the scene (buyer, investor, hiring manager, board member)
4. The triggering question is mundane; the answer is what does the damage
5. Steelman → flip in two short parallel lines
6. 3-5 bullets of "what we built instead" — operating practices, not product features
7. Triple-parallel verdict — three short clauses, escalating
8. One quotable thesis line, repeated twice (once mid-post, once at the close)
9. Voice-break sting line with a polarizing label ("walking red flag", "amateur hour", "category killer")
10. Binary CTA — "Agree or disagree?" or similar

**Do NOT use this archetype if:**
- The scene is fabricated or composite — senior audience smells fake
- The "what we built instead" bullets describe product features instead of operating practices — turns into AutoDS marketing
- The sting line punches at a protected group (employees, customers, partners like Shopify) — must punch at a behavior, not a person
- Lior's role in the scene is passive (he was just told about it) — must be in the room making the call
- The thesis doesn't survive being repeated — if it sounds repetitive instead of emphatic, the line isn't strong enough
