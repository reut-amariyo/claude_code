# LinkedIn Posting Mechanics — The 5 Rules

**These are process rules that apply to every LinkedIn post Lior publishes. They are independent of content voice, hook, or structure — they're the mechanics that multiply reach on ANY post.**

**Source:** Learned from a top creator (per Reut, 2026-04-23). Not all Lior has necessarily been doing these — we start now.

---

## Rule 1 — ALT description on every image (SEO + LLM reach)

When Lior adds any photo to a LinkedIn post, click "edit" on the image and fill in "describe it for Google" (ALT text).

**Why:** Google indexes it. LLMs (Claude, ChatGPT, Perplexity) read it when citing LinkedIn content. Means posts can end up in Google search and AI answers — reach far beyond LinkedIn itself.

**How to apply:** Every image gets 1-2 sentences of descriptive ALT text that includes the topic/keyword of the post.

## Rule 2 — Self-repost 6-8 hours after publishing

Repost the post from Lior's own account 6-8 hours after the original. After 1-2 days, delete the repost.

**Why:** Reaches new time zones and fresh audiences who missed the original. Delete after 1-2 days to avoid LinkedIn algorithm penalizing duplicate content.

**How to apply:** Set a reminder 6-8h after each post. Repost without adding new commentary. Delete 1-2 days later.

## Rule 3 — Stay active for 1 full hour to comment

For the first hour after posting, Lior responds to every comment.

**Why:** This is the single strongest signal to the LinkedIn algorithm. It also builds the community — people comment again because they know Lior will reply. "Never post and ghost."

**How to apply:** Lior schedules 1 hour of commenting after every post goes live. No meetings, no deep work — just comments.

## Rule 4 — Pinned comment to nurture

Add a self-comment under the post and pin it. Usually: bonus insight, related link, or a follow-up thought.

**Why:** Drives more engagement (pinned comment is high-visibility). More importantly: creates a sense of "belonging" — the post becomes a conversation hub with Lior as the host, not a broadcast.

**How to apply:** Write the pinned comment as part of drafting the post. Could be a related framework, a data point that didn't fit, or a question to the audience.

## Rule 5 — "Dear son / Love, Dad" method for natural tone

When writing a post, start the draft with "Dear son," and end with "Love, Dad." Write the body as if talking to a child. Delete the opening and closing before publishing.

**Why:** Forces casual, plain, no-jargon tone. The result reads easily because it was written easily. Removes "LinkedIn voice" (corporate, formal, over-hedged) without losing substance.

**How to apply:** When drafting for Lior, add "Dear son," at the top. Write. Add "Love, Dad." Delete both before publishing. Re-read — if it still sounds formal, rewrite.

## Rule 6 — Length: 750-1,200 characters is the sweet spot

The LinkedIn ideal post length for opinion/operator posts is **750-1,200 characters**. Under 750 = thin. Over 1,200 = drift, attention loss, "tl;dr" reaction.

**Why:** Long enough to deliver substance and earn the read, short enough to keep mobile scrollers engaged through to the CTA. Confirmed by Reut 2026-05-04.

**How to apply:** Before publishing, paste the full post into [charactercalculator.com/he/](https://charactercalculator.com/he/) to verify the count. If over 1,200 — cut. If under 750 — usually means the post is missing a payoff beat (more proof, sharper closer, etc.).

**Caveats:**
- Very rare exceptions for story-scene posts that need narrative breathing room (e.g., the Slack 43K post is ~1,400 chars). When narrative arc demands more, the 1,200 ceiling can stretch.
- For research-explainer or hot-take posts (like the cart abandonment post), stay strict at 750-1,200.

## Rule 7 — Statistical hook for opinion posts

When a post carries a single strong statistic, **lead with it**. Don't bury the number under a narrative opener.

**Why:** The brain locks onto numbers. A 70% / 11% / $6M number in line 1 stops the scroll harder than any "You know that feeling..." opener. Confirmed by Reut 2026-05-04 on cart abandonment post.

**How to apply:**
- Identify the strongest stat in the post
- Verify it's accurate (no fabrication)
- Lead with it as a 1-line punch
- Build empathy/scene in the next paragraph, not before

**Example (cart abandonment post):**
- ❌ Buried hook: "You know that feeling. You add product after product to your cart..." (stat lands at line 4)
- ✅ Stat hook: "70% of online shoppers do the exact same thing right before checkout." (stat lands at line 1)

**When NOT to lead with the stat:**
- The stat is generic or known (e.g., "90% of startups fail" — too tired)
- The post is a personal scene story where stats would break voice
- The stat is a side-fact, not the post's central claim

## Rule 8 — The breath test (paragraph length)

Read every paragraph out loud. If you can't say it in one breath, break it.

**Why:** Mobile readers process LinkedIn paragraphs the way podcast listeners process speech — in breath-units. A paragraph that runs longer than one breath causes the eye to skip ahead. Confirmed by Reut 2026-05-04.

**How to apply:**
- After drafting, read each paragraph aloud
- If you run out of air mid-paragraph, insert a line break at the natural pause point
- A single long sentence is fine if it can be said in one breath; a 3-sentence paragraph is too long if any of the three sentences are themselves long

**Example fix from cart abandonment post:**
- ❌ Pre-fix: *"And every ecommerce team is convinced they know why: shipping fees, slow checkout, wrong price, button placement. So they spend a fortune A/B testing free shipping and chasing a pathetic 1% lift."* (one breath fails)
- ✅ Post-fix:
  *"Every ecommerce team is convinced they know why.*
  *They blame shipping fees, slow checkout, or button placement.*
  *So they spend a fortune A/B testing free shipping, chasing a pathetic 1% lift."*

## Rule 9 — Two topical hashtags to control the post URL

LinkedIn builds each post's permanent URL slug from the FIRST LINE of the post, unless the post
has hashtags, then it uses the hashtags. Add exactly 2 hashtags naming what the post is about.

**Why:** LinkedIn posts rank in Google and AI answer engines read Google. The slug is a machine
signal about what the page covers, and it is free to control. Stacks with Rule 1 (ALT text).
Source: Tom Orbach newsletter, 2026-08-24.

**How to apply:** ❌ #mondaymotivation #hustle → ✅ #ecommerceautomation #aiagents. On-pillar,
lowercase, two only, at the end of the post. Never hashtag-stuff the body.

## Rule 10 — Check post analytics for an AI-slop warning after publishing

LinkedIn has a "seems like AI slop" report button (3-dot menu), pressed 1M+ times, and posts it
classifies as slop get ~40% fewer views. Post analytics now shows a warning when readers report
the post. Source: Tom Orbach newsletter, 2026-08-24.

**Why:** The anti-AI writing rules are now enforced by the algorithm, not only by reader taste.
"It's not X, it's Y" is reportedly auto-suppressed.

**How to apply:** During Lior's 1-hour comment window (Rule 3), check analytics for the AI-report
warning. If it appears, log the post and its patterns in the performance log and retire that
draft shape. See [[linkedin-ai-slop-penalty]].

- [ ] Reminder set to delete the repost 1-2 days later

- [ ] Statistical hook leads if a strong stat exists
- [ ] Breath test passed on every paragraph
- [ ] Two on-pillar hashtags added (controls the post URL slug)
- [ ] After publish: post analytics checked for an AI-slop report warning
