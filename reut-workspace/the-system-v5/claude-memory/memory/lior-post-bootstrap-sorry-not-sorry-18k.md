Beats: quoted objection → 3 mock apologies → "But I will NOT say sorry that I..." → 3 convictions with receipts → one-line close → question.

**Final shape:**
- Hook: `"Nobody bootstraps anymore."` + `Founders keep telling me it's outdated. Well...`
- Apologies: no salary 5 yrs / cheapest first developer / huge bug at 3 AM + "I was also our finance, legal and marketing team"
- Not sorry: hired while everyone cut in COVID "(No board told us otherwise.)" / charged from day one, $1 trial "(Made customers our only investors.)" / co-founder like a spouse, Michael "(Our only commitment was to each other.)"
- Close: "The slow playbook still works." + question.

**Image:** Lior + Michael Royf in tuxedos, elevator mirror selfie, 4:5. Reut chose it and thinks it helped a lot. I had ranked the 2016/2026 then-vs-now split first, so my visual pick lagged again. See [[feedback-visual-is-a-hook]].

**Why:** these are the lessons Reut forced during the drafting, and they are what made it work.
1. Reut's test: every list item must be something ONLY a bootstrapper could say. "Don't count hours" and "home for dinner" were cut because a VC-backed founder could say them too.
2. Reut's short parenthetical lines after each point carried the bootstrapping link, like the record post's "(I didn't come from money.)".
3. No exit, no $92M and no $38M, a week after the record post. Reut refused to repeat the exit.
4. Lior gave Reut numbers he didn't want public, the 1-in-4 vs 1-in-20 trial conversion. Use the principle, never the figures.

**How to apply:** for any themed list post, run each item through "could a founder without this trait say it?" before drafting. For Lior, a warm real photo with a person he names in the post can beat a concept image. Offer it as a strong first option, not as a fallback.
