The 4 positioning questions Reut wants used as the foundation for Lior's personal brand strategy. Source: creative director framework Reut shared 2026-05-03 (the process used to grow Dan Martell from 100K → 10M followers).

## The 4 questions

### 1. What do you want to be positioned as?
- What is the **promise** Lior is offering the world?
- What is the **outcome** he helps people attain through that promise?
- Most creators are mispositioned — talking about things that don't establish them as the expert in their niche. If a post doesn't reinforce the promise, it dilutes the brand.

### 2. What do you want to position *against*? (Often more important than #1)
- What **wrong** does Lior want to right?
- What **hill** is he willing to die on?
- What are his **contrarian takes** in the niche?
- In a world where everyone parrots each other, the contrarian stance is the moat. Without it, Lior is interchangeable.

### 3. What action do you want followers to take after each piece of content?
- Take action and get a result before they ever buy?
- Buy a product / book a call / join a list?
- Be intentional — the answer dictates the entire content strategy. A post with no intended next action is wasted.

### 4. What's the one thing Lior can say that no one else in his space can say?
- Doesn't have to be a giant outcome. Could be:
  - "I've scaled AutoDS from 0 to [X]M ARR"
  - "I've hired and fired hundreds of people"
  - "I've run OKRs across 200 employees for [X] years"
  - A specific number or experience he tracked
- Creators blowing up fastest **lead with proof and experience**. Most people make content with no proof — that's the gap.

## Lior's grounded answers (approved 2026-05-03)

### 1. Positioned AS
**Promise:** "I'll show you the operator's playbook for scaling a SaaS to 250+ employees — live, with the actual OKRs, dashboards, hiring decisions, and mistakes from inside AutoDS."

**Outcome for the audience:** Founders and operators stop guessing. They get implementable systems (the 70% hire rule, OKR cadence, retention/feedback calls, macro management) they can install in their own company this quarter — before they ever buy anything from him.

**Why this and not "ecommerce guru" or "AI founder":** He started dropshipping at 14, built AutoDS, sold it to Fiverr, runs 250 people across 30+ countries. The position is **the operator who built it from zero and runs it now** — not the advisor who's never sat in the chair.

### 2. Positioned AGAINST
**The wrongs Lior wants to right:**
- Polished LinkedIn-guru takes on scaling from people who've never run payroll for 250 people.
- "Inspirational" founder content with no numbers, no real stakes, no proof.
- The myth that you need to be perfect publicly (Lior went live on Facebook to apologize the same day his bug repriced every product to $1).
- The "hire the most experienced person" instinct (two senior product hires failed; the 3-year-experience product owner is still there 3+ years later).

**Hills Lior will die on (contrarian takes):**
- "Motivation beats skills 100% of the time." Hire for hunger, not résumé.
- "If I need to check what you're doing, we won't work together." Macro management isn't laziness — it's a hiring filter.
- "OKRs are not a measurement of success or failure — they're a road."
- "For the first 5 years I took zero salary home." Reinvest into the team or you'll never compound.
- "Without data you're just another person with an opinion." Kill feelings-based decisions.
- "While competitors fired in COVID, I hired HR and doubled the company." Counter-cyclical beats cost-cutting.
- "1% of a billion-dollar company > 100% of something that fails." Take the partner.
- "Advisors are the shortcut. It's not a shame to say you're struggling." Anti-lone-wolf founder.

### 3. Desired follower ACTION
**Primary:** Implement *one specific framework* before they ever buy anything — install the 70% hire rule this week, run a retention call this month, write 2-3 OKRs for the quarter, do one price optimization. Get a result in advance.

**By platform:**
- **LinkedIn:** Save the post + leave a comment with their own number/story (open-ended close, gap for the reader to fill).
- **X / TikTok / Reels:** One-word DM-opening comment ("OKR", "hire", "macro") → DM conversation.
- **Long-form (podcast / article):** Apply one framework + report back.

**Never:** "Buy my course." "Book a call." "Sign up for my newsletter." (Lior has no newsletter; product pitches break the personal-brand-≠-AutoDS-marketing rule.)

### 4. The one thing ONLY Lior can say (proof stack)
**The headline claim:**
> "I scaled AutoDS from a teenage dropshipping side-hustle to 200 employees across 30+ countries, sold it to Fiverr, and I'm publishing the actual systems — OKRs, dashboards, Slack messages, hiring filters — that got us there."

**Stackable proof points (most operators have 1; Lior has all):**
- Started dropshipping at **14 years old**, no family money, no connections, no entrepreneurs in the family.
- Ran **4 companies in parallel** (dropshipping, fulfillment, AI store builder, AutoDS).
- **Sold AutoDS to Fiverr** — real exit, not a "vision."
- **<1% monthly turnover** across 200+ employees.
- Took **zero salary home for 5 years** to reinvest in the team.
- Used **30+ advisors/consultants** — turned "asking for help" into a system.
- Hired HR and **doubled the company during COVID** while competitors fired.
- Public production failure: [dollar-bug story](lior-story-dollar-bug.md) — went live on Facebook to apologize, customer who caught it became first CS manager for 6+ years.
- Spent **millions** building data infrastructure to kill opinion-based decisions.
- **20%+ revenue lift** from a single pricing optimization.

## How to apply
- **Before drafting any post**, mentally check it against the 4 questions:
  1. Does this reinforce the promise (operator's playbook)?
  2. Is there a contrarian edge, or is it parroting consensus?
  3. What's the intended next action for the reader?
  4. Is there a piece of proof / specific number / lived experience anchoring it?
- If a draft fails any of the 4, fix it before sending. The most common failure is #2 (no contrarian edge — generic advice) and #4 (no proof — opinions floating in air).
- Use this as the **first filter** during content planning, BEFORE applying platform rules (LinkedIn structure, X data rules, short-form 10-beat structure).
- When auditing a post that feels weak but you can't articulate why, run it through the 4 — usually the failure is one of these.
