**No aggressive/violent language in replies.** "Kill the fastest" was rejected, replaced with "prioritize." Lior's voice is strategic and confident, not aggressive.
**Why:** Aggressive framing sounds like a guru pronouncement, not eye-level conversation.
**How to apply:** Avoid words like "kill," "destroy," "crush" when describing business decisions. Use strategic alternatives: "prioritize," "decide faster," "move on."

**Never show product weakness publicly.** A reply about testing AI video tools that showed 80% usage drop was rejected because it makes AutoDS look bad.
**Why:** Public replies are visible to customers, investors, competitors. Showing product failures undermines credibility.
**How to apply:** When using AutoDS stories in replies, only share outcomes that show strength - growth, efficiency gains, smart pivots. If referencing a challenge, frame it as a decision Lior made (not a product failing).

**When the insight landed, stop.** Removed "Sora had downloads. It never had retention. That's the whole story." from a reply where the point was already made.
**Why:** Adding a summary after the insight dilutes the punch. Confirmed pattern from voice-dna.md ending rules.
**How to apply:** After writing a reply, check if the last line just restates what was already said. If yes, cut it.

**Write "e-commerce" not "eCommerce".** Lior prefers the hyphenated lowercase form.
**How to apply:** Always use "e-commerce" in all content.

**Don't over-qualify insights with specific context.** "Explain what they built" beats "explain what they built to a non-technical CEO." Removing the qualifier makes the line universal - every reader sees themselves in it.
**Why:** Over-specifying narrows the insight and makes it self-referential (about Lior) instead of relatable to the audience.
**How to apply:** After writing a reply, check if any insight has an unnecessary "to whom" or "in what context" qualifier. If the line is stronger without it, cut it.
