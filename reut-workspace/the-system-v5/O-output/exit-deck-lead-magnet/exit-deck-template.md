# The Exit Deck Template
### The slide-by-slide structure of the deck that sold AutoDS

**Internal header for the social team — do not publish this block.**

## What this lead magnet is

A fill-in-the-blank version of the 9-section presentation Lior's team used to sell AutoDS to Fiverr.
The promise to the audience: "This is the exact structure that got my company acquired.
Fill in your numbers and you have a buyer-ready deck."

Suggested delivery format: designed carousel or PDF, 30-35 slides, one template slide per page,
each with a short "why this slide exists" note in Lior's voice.

---

## The 9-section architecture

This is the spine of the whole deck. Buyers read in this order:

1. Executive Summary — the whole story in 8 slides
2. Industry — why the market makes this inevitable
3. Solutions and Technology — what the product actually does
4. Architecture — proof it scales
5. Competitive Landscape — why you win
6. Customers and Marketing — how growth happens, with unit economics
7. Future Growth Drivers — what the buyer's money buys
8. Operations and Transaction Information — the company behind the product
9. Financials — the numbers, last on purpose

Note for the audience: financials come LAST. The first 60 pages earn the right to show them.
A buyer who reaches the numbers already convinced reads them differently.

## Section 1 — Executive Summary

### Slide 1.1 — Company at-a-Glance
One slide, four quadrants. A buyer should understand the whole business in 60 seconds.
- Quadrant 1, Company Overview: 4-5 bullets. What you do, who you serve, your unfair
  positioning with the biggest platform in your space, how recognized your brand is.
- Quadrant 2, Key Stats table: [TEAM SIZE], [OWNERSHIP STRUCTURE, e.g. founder-owned and
  bootstrapped], [REVENUE MODEL + % breakdown], [ACTIVE PAYING CUSTOMERS], [TAM TODAY / TAM IN 5 YEARS],
  [REVIEW RATING + COUNT], [HQ + remote footprint].
- Quadrant 3, Growth chart: revenue and profit bars, last 2 actual years plus current-year estimate.
- Quadrant 4, Unit economics teaser: your single best repeatable-economics chart.

### Slide 1.2 — Investment Highlights
7 numbered reasons to buy, one line each. The formula per line: claim + the proof baked into
the sentence. Cover: market position, product depth, unit economics, scalable tech, financial
profile with [GROWTH %] and [MARGIN %], team, and go-to-market efficiency.

### Slide 1.3 — The Ecosystem
One diagram: suppliers on the left, platforms on the right, your product in the middle,
your customer and their end customer below. Two benefit columns: what your customer gets,
what THEIR customer gets. Buyers love businesses that improve everyone's outcome.

### Slide 1.4 — Customer Evolution
Show that you serve customers across their whole lifecycle: [BEGINNER SEGMENT] →
[PART-TIME SEGMENT] → [FULL-TIME / PRO SEGMENT] → [ADJACENT SEGMENT]. For each: profile
and your value proposition. This slide proves your TAM is not one niche.

### Slide 1.5 — The Company Journey
Timeline with 6-7 milestones from founding year to today, ending with "the future" as the
final milestone. The trick from the real deck: the story starts before the company existed,
with the founder falling in love with the problem. Make the buyer feel the arc.

### Slide 1.6 — Financial Profile Summary
Revenue and EBITDA bars including next year's projection, plus your 4-6 growth strategies
as labeled boxes. This slide bridges the story to the money.

### Slide 1.7 — Management Team
Founders only, or founders plus 1-2 key executives. 3-4 bullets each: years of relevant
experience, prior roles, anything elite or credibility-building. One line stating the full
team is committed to the next chapter.

## Section 2 — Industry

### Slide 2.1 — The Market Shift
Tell the market's story in two eras: "then" with 3 bullets on why it was hard, "today" with
4 checkmarks on why it exploded. Anchor with third-party numbers: [MARKET SIZE THEN] vs
[MARKET SIZE NOW], each with a named source. End with 1-2 key themes that make your
category inevitable.

### Slide 2.2 — Market Size
One big chart: market value by year for the next 5 years with [CAGR %]. Below it, 2-3
supporting stats with sources. Use only third-party research firms as sources, never
your own estimates on this slide.

### Slide 2.3 — Your TAM, Two Ways
This is the credibility move: calculate TAM with TWO independent methods and show they land
in the same place.
- Top-down: [TOTAL MARKET VALUE] × [YOUR CATEGORY'S SHARE %] = [TAM]
- Bottom-up: [NUMBER OF POTENTIAL CUSTOMERS] × [CONSERVATIVE REVENUE PER CUSTOMER] = [TAM]
Label your assumptions conservative and footnote where each input comes from.

## Section 3 — Solutions and Technology

### Slide 3.1 — Platform Overview
One slide listing your 5-7 core modules, each with a one-line description of the job it does.
Frame: "end-to-end solution" on one side, "enabling customer success" on the other.

### Slide 3.2 — Key Integrations / Partnerships
Show your integrations timeline: which platform, which year added. Include a revenue-mix
pie by channel with [PLACEHOLDER %] slices. Three checkmark bullets on why integrations
compound growth.

### Slide 3.3 — Supply / Inventory Side
Whatever your version of supply is: partners, data sources, content. Show breadth
[TOTAL COUNT] and depth [EXCLUSIVE COUNT], with an advantages box per supply type.

### Slides 3.4-3.10 — Product Walkthrough (one slide per module)
Screenshot on the left, 3-6 checkmark bullets on the right. One module per slide.
Each bullet describes a job done for the customer, not a feature name. If a module has a
differentiated feature no competitor has, give it a call-out box.

### Slide 3.11 — Product Demo Link
One slide, one click-to-watch link. Buyers share this internally.

### Slide 3.12 — Product Roadmap
Four quarters, 4-6 items each. Mark shipped items with checkmarks to show you deliver
what you plan. Keep 1-2 ambitious unlabeled items to signal upside.

### Slide 3.13 — Brand Recognition
Logos and badges only: review-site rating [X.X stars, N+ reviews], platform partner status,
awards, top-10 lists. No text needed. Social proof compresses diligence.

## Section 4 — Architecture

### Slide 4.1 — Scale Stats
8 big numbers on one slide proving the tech works at scale: [ACTIVE CUSTOMERS],
[PLATFORMS SUPPORTED], [TRANSACTION VOLUME PROCESSED], [DATA POINTS MONITORED],
[COUNTRIES SERVED], [CATALOG SIZE], [MONTHLY VISITS], [UPTIME %].

### Slide 4.2 — Architecture Diagram
A simplified block diagram of your system: frontend, APIs, databases, workers, queues.
Enough to show a real engineering org built this. Do NOT include security-sensitive
details, exact service names, or anything an attacker could map.

---

## Section 5 — Competitive Landscape

### Slide 5.1 — Market Positioning
Place competitors into 3-4 point-solution buckets around you, with you in the center as
the only all-in-one. Add a "what makes us unique" box with 4 moats: [UNIQUE TECH],
[BRAND], [RELATIONSHIPS], [BREADTH].

### Slide 5.2 — Feature Comparison Matrix
Rows: 8-10 capabilities. Columns: you plus 6-10 named competitors. You get a full column
of checkmarks. Be honest on partial checkmarks for competitors, it reads more credible.

---

## Section 6 — Customers and Marketing

### Slide 6.1 — Marketing Strategy
Your funnel drawn top to bottom with every channel labeled. The winning frame from the
real deck: "no-touch" or low-touch acquisition. Buyers pay premiums for growth engines
that do not depend on a sales army.

### Slide 6.2 — Lead Generation Numbers
The funnel with real conversion math: [TOTAL SESSIONS] → [NEW VISITORS] → [SIGNUPS] →
[TRIALS] → [ACTIVATIONS], with [% CONVERSION] at each step. Plus a lead-mix pie:
[ORGANIC %] / [PARTNERSHIP %] / [PAID %]. The higher your organic share, the better
this slide reads.

### Slide 6.3 — Your #1 Channel Deep-Dive
One slide on whatever channel drives most growth. Strategy overview, 4 highlight stats
[ACTIVE PARTNERS], [% OF SPEND], [NEW TRIALS PER QUARTER], [VIEWS DRIVEN], and the KPIs
you track. Showing you measure a channel rigorously is the point.

### Slide 6.4 — Example Customer Profiles
3-4 anonymized or permissioned customer archetypes. For each: who they are, what they use
you for, 3 checkmarks of outcomes. Tie each archetype back to an addressable-population
number from Section 2.

### Slides 6.5-6.7 — Unit Economics (the deal-makers)
Three slides. If your numbers are good, this is what sells the company:
- CAC payback by monthly cohort: bar chart, one bar per cohort, with the median called out.
  [MEDIAN X.X MONTHS]. Consistency matters more than the absolute number.
- Realized LTV to CAC by cohort age: line chart, one line per quarterly cohort. Shows newer
  cohorts tracking at or above older ones. [MEDIAN X.Xx] for matured cohorts.
- Revenue retention: [XX %] retention of last year's cohorts this year, with methodology
  explained in plain language in a commentary column.
Rule from the real deck: every chart gets a commentary column explaining exactly how the
metric is calculated. Buyers distrust charts with hidden methodology.

## Section 7 — Future Growth Drivers

### Slide 7.1 — Growth Strategy Overview
6 labeled boxes: [PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT], [DEEPEN EXISTING CHANNELS], [NEW CUSTOMER SEGMENT],
[MARKETING EXPANSION], [CONVERSION + RETENTION], [ADJACENT OFFERINGS]. 3-4 checkmarks each.

### Slides 7.2-7.6 — One Slide Per Growth Lever
Expand each box into its own slide. The rule: every lever must be something the BUYER'S
resources accelerate. You are selling them the map of what their money buys.

---

## Section 8 — Operations and Transaction Information

### Slide 8.1 — Team Breakdown
[HEADCOUNT] across [COUNTRIES], split by department in a small table, geography pie,
and 3-4 commentary bullets on how the org actually runs.

### Slide 8.2 — Org Chart
Boxes by function with headcounts. No names below founder level.

### Slide 8.3 — Systems and Tools
The business software stack by category: ERP, HRIS, communication, invoicing. Signals a
company that runs on systems, not on the founder's memory.

### Slide 8.4 — Corporate Details
Legal structure, jurisdiction, tax status, and ownership summary at whatever level of
detail your process stage requires. Early conversations: "founder-owned, bootstrapped"
is enough.

---

## Section 9 — Financials

### Slide 9.1 — Financial Highlights
9 big numbers, no charts: [REVENUE LAST YEAR], [EBITDA LAST YEAR], [REVENUE THIS YEAR E],
[EBITDA THIS YEAR E], [GROSS MARGIN %], [EBITDA MARGIN %], [CAC PAYBACK], [LTV/CAC],
[RETENTION %].

### Slide 9.2 — Revenue Model
Every revenue stream as a column: how it works, how it's priced, and a revenue-mix pie
showing [% PER STREAM]. Show the upsell path from base plan to add-ons with an arrow.

### Slide 9.3 — Revenue Recognition Table
Rows per revenue type: contract terms, pricing, cash collection, recognition timing.
This slide exists to make the CFO on the buy side relax.

### Slide 9.4 — Revenue Detail by Channel
Table: revenue per channel per year, actuals plus 2 forecast years, with % of revenue
and growth rates. Numbered footnotes explaining every strategic shift in the mix.

### Slide 9.5 — Cost Detail
Cost of revenue and operating expenses tables with the same year columns. Footnote
anything unusual before the buyer asks.

### Slide 9.6 — 5-Year Forecast
Revenue, gross profit, EBITDA with margins, 5 years out. Above the table: 3 key forecast
assumptions in plain sentences. Keep assumptions conservative enough to defend line by line.

### Slide 9.7 — Balance Sheet Snapshot
Current assets and liabilities, last 3 year-ends. Note clearly if unaudited.

## 🛑 Hard rules for the social team

These may NEVER appear in the published lead magnet, in the promo content, or on camera:

1. The original PDF, any screenshot of it, or its visual design. Template only.
2. Any real AutoDS financial figure from the sale process: revenue, EBITDA, margins,
   forecasts, cost lines, balance sheet items, revenue mix percentages.
3. Cohort economics values: LTV/CAC multiples, CAC payback months, retention percentages,
   funnel conversion numbers.
4. The cap table, ownership percentages, share counts, or Israeli tax structure.
5. The investment bank's name, any banker's name or contact details.
6. Internal org data: employee vs contractor split, org chart, salaries, tools stack.
7. Customer or store names from the deck, named suppliers, payment processor fee terms,
   and any platform revenue-share terms.
8. The real software architecture diagram or service names.
9. The internal headcount figure from the deck. If team size comes up in promo copy,
   the approved public number is 250 employees.

Approved public wrapper facts for promo copy: sold to Fiverr, the $92M exit, bootstrapped,
founder-owned, two co-founders, founded from Lior's own eBay dropshipping automation.

## Sanitization log (for Reut, not for the social team)

Everything replaced with placeholders, by category:
- All financials: 2022-2025 revenue/EBITDA actuals and projections, the 5-year forecast
  through 2028, gross margin and EBITDA margin figures, full cost tables, balance sheet.
- Unit economics: median CAC payback, median LTV/CAC for 2022 cohorts, revenue retention
  figure, full funnel conversion numbers, lead mix percentages, influencer spend share.
- Corporate: cap table with both founders' exact percentages and share counts, the 12%
  preferred enterprise tax rate, employee/contractor split and org chart.
- Deal apparatus: all CIBC references, four bankers' names, emails, and phone numbers,
  the confidentiality disclaimer.
- Commercial terms: Payoneer / PayPal / BlueSnap take rates, Shopify Appstore revenue
  share detail, private supplier counts.
- Named third parties kept OUT of the template: customer stores and profiles, specific
  suppliers, internal tools by name.

Judgment calls I made, flag if you disagree:
- I kept the 9-section order and per-slide anatomy fully intact. That structure is generic
  M&A practice, and it IS the promised value of the magnet.
- I kept qualitative "tricks" from the real deck as teaching notes, like financials-last,
  dual TAM methodology, and commentary columns, with no real numbers attached.
- Deck says 134 team members including contractors, which contradicts the public 250
  figure. The template contains neither; rule 9 above locks promo copy to 250.
- One legal flag worth a 5-minute check with Lior: the CIP was produced by the bank under
  a confidentiality process, and the Fiverr deal likely has NDA terms around deal materials.
  A structure-only template with zero real data should be safe, but Lior should confirm
  nothing in the purchase agreement restricts referencing the deck's existence.
