## What we took, and what we left

The guide has six teachable sections: the five model abilities, the Claude Code cockpit,
leader-and-workers orchestration, custom agent files, light CLAUDE.md, goals and loops.
Five of the six are tool tips and fail the CEO relevance filter.

We took ONE line from "secret 1: do not prescribe the route":

> "Make the checkout work" invites a plausible claim. "Complete a test purchase in the
> sandbox and show the resulting order row" gives the model a finish line it cannot talk
> its way around.

That one idea is not about AI at all. It is about how a CEO writes a brief. Big promise:
rework drops and "done" stops being an opinion. Small action: add one sentence to the
bottom of a brief. Eleven seconds, no tool, no process, no meeting.

Left out on purpose: agent files, subagent orchestration, /goal, /loop, effort levels,
model pricing, CLAUDE.md line limits. All of it is engineer content and none of it
survives "would a SaaS CEO with 200 employees credibly say this?"

## Structure donor

Lior's own proven pattern, the 93K MD-to-HTML post: insider intel from a live launch,
operator confession in the middle, strategic close that generalises past the tool.
Hook rule from Tom Orbach: shrink the action, inflate the result.

Pillar: focus. Archetype: 9 operator confession, with a day-1 news anchor for reach.

--- POST STARTS ---

Anthropic's new model found the bug in my briefs.
It was one missing sentence.

Monday it came back with "done" on a job that was not finished. The work was plausible. That is worse than wrong, because plausible gets approved.

The difference sits between two versions of the same instruction.

"Make the checkout work."

versus

"Complete a test purchase in the sandbox and show me the order row it created."

Same task. The first invites a confident answer. The second sets a finish line nobody can talk their way around.

Then it hit me that I have been accepting the first version from humans for years.

A company of 200 people runs on briefs. Every one of them ends with what to do. Almost none of them end with what proof will exist when it is finished.

So here is the entire change, and it takes eleven seconds:

Add one line to the bottom of every brief you send this week.

"This is finished when ___ exists and I can see it."

No new tool. No new process. No meeting.

You will feel it in the first round of work that comes back, because "done" stops being an opinion and starts being a thing you can look at.

The agents made this obvious. They did not create it.

What does "done" point to on your team right now?

#aiagents #delegation

--- POST ENDS ---
