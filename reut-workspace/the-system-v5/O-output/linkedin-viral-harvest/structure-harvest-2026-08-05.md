## Post #1 — "I don't want to sound like I'm bragging" (LinkedIn coach, author + metrics unknown)

**Source archetype:** Coach/creator, permission-giving post. Metrics not provided.

### Structural skeleton (10 beats)
1. **Quote-hook.** Opens with a sentence the reader says to themselves, in quotes. Under 10 words, instant self-recognition. Reader is IN the post from word one.
2. **Diagnosis line.** Names it a shared disease: "This sentence is a LinkedIn epidemic." Elevates one person's worry into a phenomenon.
3. **Credibility beat.** "I've coached thousands of you" — one line of authority, then moves on.
4. **Belief interrogation.** "Who convinced you that...?" Attacks the hidden assumption instead of giving advice. The reader has never asked themselves this.
5. **Rapid OR-chain.** "what you do OR what you've accomplished OR who you've helped" — enumerates the reader's own innocent behaviors to show how absurd the guilt is.
6. **Objection pre-kill.** Carves out the one real exception (money screenshots) so the claim survives scrutiny.
7. **Odd-numbered proof list.** 3 outcomes, escalating, third one is emotional ("You'll wish you bragged earlier").
8. **Identity reframe with a vs.** Influencer vs. influencing invisible decision makers. The actual quotable idea of the post.
9. **Verdict close.** "I rest my case." Confidence signal, zero hedging.
10. **Double engagement gap.** Repost CTA + P.S. question ("Have you ever thought, said or heard the same?") — a yes/no question with a near-100% "yes" rate for the target audience.

### Psychological engine
**Absolution.** The post removes a guilt the reader already carries and gives permission to do what they secretly want to do. Identical engine to Reut's own winning Hebrew formula: pain hook → absolution → hidden mechanism → checklist → CTA. This is the transferable core.

### Transfers to Lior ✅
- Quote-hook: open with a sentence founders say to themselves or hear from others.
- Belief interrogation: "who convinced you that X?" fits Contrarian/Operator verdict posts.
- Objection pre-kill in one line (must be rewritten without parentheses).
- 3-item escalating proof list, third item emotional.
- P.S.-style yes/no question with a guaranteed-yes answer as the engagement gap.
- Absolution engine itself: give founders permission for something they feel guilty about (shipping ugly, saying no, staying small-team, living on 200).

### Dies at the door ❌
- Parentheses ×2 — hard rule.
- "You're not on LinkedIn to become an influencer. You're here to..." — this is the "not X, it's Y" contrast template in disguise. The vs. must be rebuilt as a real comparison, not a negation pair.
- "I've coached thousands of you" — coach credibility. Lior's version is operator proof: a number from AutoDS production.
- Repost ♻️ CTA — never for Lior.
- Topic itself (LinkedIn bragging meta-content) fails the CEO relevance filter. Engine transfers, topic does not.

### Pattern candidate (needs 2 more confirmations)
- **Quote-hook + absolution + belief interrogation = permission post.** Watch for recurrence in the rest of the batch.

## Post #2 — "The biggest threats to your team" (leadership creator, author + metrics unknown)

**Source archetype:** Leadership/management creator, invisible-threat post. Metrics not provided.

### Structural skeleton (9 beats)
1. **Invisible-threat hook.** "The biggest threats to your team are the ones you never see." Fear of the unseen. Note: 12 words, zero concrete anchor — works for leadership creators with warm audiences, violates our hook discipline.
2. **Stakes line.** "By the time they show up in your numbers, the damage is already done." Makes inaction expensive.
3. **Role reframe.** "A leader's real job is to catch them early." Tells the reader what their job actually is.
4. **Hidden mechanism.** Solid outside vs. trust eroding in silence underneath. Surface/underneath contrast.
5. **Delayed villain reveal.** Narrows from "many threats" to one, then names it alone on its own line: "Blame culture." Two-word payoff after a 5-line buildup. This is the scroll-stopper.
6. **Witnessed micro-story.** Team lived with it → mechanism of decay in 2 lines → ONE change by the leader → result with a timeframe ("Within months"). Classic before/turn/after in 6 short lines.
7. **Save-worthy checklist.** "That is the real work:" + 5 arrow items, each a behavior not a platitude. Odd number. This is the bookmark engine.
8. **Aphoristic vs. close.** "Talent and effort get a team started. Safety is what lets them last." The quotable principle.
9. **No engagement gap.** Fully closed ending, no question. Confidence over conversation.

### Psychological engine
**Invisible enemy + named villain + replicable fix.** Reader fears something silent is killing their team, gets it named, gets a checklist to act on tomorrow. The checklist makes it a SAVE post, and saves drive distribution.

### Transfers to Lior ✅
- Delayed villain reveal: buildup lines narrowing to a one-or-two-word name alone on its own line. Strong fit for Operator verdict posts.
- One-change turnaround with a timeframe. Lior's version uses a real AutoDS moment with a number.
- Behavior checklist as the save engine — rebuilt as plain lines or numbered list, never arrows.
- Surface vs. underneath mechanism ("looks solid outside, eroding inside") — natural vs. for one sentence.
- Quotable two-sentence close.

### Dies at the door ❌
- ↳ arrow list — PROVEN killer on Lior's account, three posts died at ~800 imp. Hard no.
- Hook: 12 words, no anchor. Lior's version needs a number, name, or year in line 1.
- Witnessed anonymous story ("I watched a team", "their leader") — too vague for Lior. His stories carry his own numbers or they don't run.
- Closed ending with zero question — for us this is a ⚠️ note, not a blocker, but the audit flags it.
- 9 beats, ~1,600+ chars — over our 750–1,200 sweet spot. Compress.

### Pattern candidate
- **Delayed one-word villain reveal on its own line.** Watch for recurrence.
- **Checklist-as-save-engine** (post #1 used a proof list, #2 an action list — both odd-numbered lists as the body's spine). 2/2 so far.

## Post #4 — "People don't quit because it's hard" (Nathan Hirsch, 2,226+ reactions / 287 comments / 188 reposts)

**Source archetype:** Founder/creator, grievance-validation leadership post. Typographic quote card visual with the key word in red italic.

### Structural skeleton (10 beats)
1. **Negation-pair hook.** "People don't quit because it's hard. They quit because it's unfair." Textbook "not X, it's Y" template — and it did 2,226 reactions. The template works; it's still banned for us (AI tell). The reframe VALUE transfers, the wording pattern doesn't.
2. **Concession beat.** "Hard rarely runs people off. Most people will happily do the difficult work if they believe the game is straight." Disarms the obvious objection before it forms.
3. **Grievance enumeration.** 3 arrow items, each a specific injustice the reader has personally lived: wrong promotion, stolen credit, double standard. Odd number, escalating intimacy. This is the resonance engine — every reader has one of these.
4. **Diagnosis rename.** "That's not a hard-work problem. That's a fairness problem." Second negation pair; renames the phenomenon.
5. **Aphorism checkpoint.** "No perk covers a rigged deal." Quotable mid-post, 6 words.
6. **Leader empowerment pivot.** "If you lead a team, you have the power to change this." Flips the audience from victim to hero mid-post — dual-audience design.
7. **Save-worthy checklist.** 5 numbered items, emoji numerals, each with a ↳ explainer line. The save/repost engine: 188 reposts.
8. **Two-sentence aphoristic close.** "Hard work bonds a team. Unfairness quietly poisons it."
9. **Venting question.** "What's the most unfair thing that ever made you want to leave a job?" Guaranteed-story question — everyone has an answer and wants to tell it. 287 comments is this line working.
10. **P.S. self-promo funnel.** Portfolio brag + newsletter link.

### Psychological engine
**Grievance validation, dual-audience.** Employees get told their resentment was justified all along; leaders get a hero script and a checklist. Both halves of LinkedIn see themselves. The venting question converts validated grievance directly into comments.

### Transfers to Lior ✅
- Dual-audience pivot: open on the employee's lived pain, flip to the operator's fix. Lior's natural altitude is the second half; borrowing the first half doubles the audience.
- Concession beat right after the hook — disarm the objection early. Clean fit for Contrarian posts.
- Grievance enumeration as resonance engine, rebuilt as plain lines. Specific lived injustices beat abstract claims.
- Guaranteed-story question as the engagement gap. Lior's version asks it without inviting pure negativity toward employers.
- Mid-post 6-word aphorism checkpoint, not just at the close.
- Visual: single key word highlighted in red italic inside a typographic card. Simple, brandable emphasis trick.
- Topic passes the CEO filter: retention and fairness is exactly what a 250-employee CEO has standing on. And we hold a canonical receipt no creator has: walking away from the PE deal a day before signing because the buyer planned to cut loyal staff.

### Dies at the door ❌
- Both negation-pair reframes ("don't quit because hard / quit because unfair", "not a hard-work problem / a fairness problem") — hard rule, must be rebuilt as a "vs." comparison.
- → and ↳ arrows throughout — proven killer on Lior's account.
- Emoji numerals (1️⃣) — marketing-graphic energy, not his voice.
- P.S. newsletter funnel + follow-for-more in the visual.
- The pure-venting question. Ours needs a constructive frame or it curates a comment section of employer-bashing under a CEO's name.
- ~1,900 chars. Compress hard.

### Pattern scoreboard (4 posts in)
- **Checklist-as-save-engine: 3/4** (#1 proof list, #2 action list, #4 numbered checklist; #3 externalized it into the visual). Third confirmation reached → RECOMMEND PROMOTING to the Top 13 playbook: odd-or-5-item plain-text list as the body spine of save-type posts.
- **Externalized quotable in the visual: 2/2** (#3 handwritten note with number, #4 typographic card with red key word). One more promotes it.
- **Delayed reveal on its own line: 2/4** — absent here (hook reveals instantly). Still a candidate.

## Post #6 — "Working ON the business, not IN it" (Codie Sanchez, metrics not provided)

**Source archetype:** Investor/creator, how-to-delegate post with an event funnel. Visual: screenshot of HER OWN X post on black background.

### The visual trick (Reut flagged it specifically)
Self-quotation dressed as third-party authority. The tweet card carries verified-badge + handle + platform chrome, so it borrows the credibility infrastructure of a "quoted tweet" while quoting herself. The tweet holds the quotable (90% / 3 things / "delusional in your optimism"); the LinkedIn text delivers the how. Division of labor: image = the claim, text = the method.

**Direct application for Lior:** the agency posts daily on his X. Screenshot his best X posts as LinkedIn visuals. One asset, two under-target platforms, and the X chrome adds authority LinkedIn text alone doesn't carry. Cheapest new visual family candidate we've found — and it feeds the X account discovery loop too.

### Structural skeleton (7 beats)
1. **Cliché acknowledgment hook.** "You've heard SO MANY times you should be working ON the business, not IN the business" — names the advice the reader is tired of hearing. Instant agreement without claiming novelty.
2. **Two-word pivot.** "But how?" on its own line. The post's promise: everyone sells the WHAT, this delivers the HOW. Tension in 2 words.
3. **Vivid testable dream state.** "2 weeks off with your phone on silent and no internet." Converts an abstract goal (delegation) into a concrete pass/fail test the reader can picture and self-administer. This is the line that gets saved.
4. **3 command sections (odd).** Each: imperative header ("Fire yourself", "Kill meetings", "Find a mentor") + method + quantified sting.
5. **Quantified meeting math.** "A useless 1 hour meeting with 16 employees is like losing 2 work days." Arithmetic as argument — numbers doing the persuading.
6. **The funnel seam.** Tip 3 (find a mentor) morphs mid-section into the ad: "it can be hard to find people on the same journey... you just have to know where to look..." → event registration. The pitch masquerades as the answer to the third tip. The seam is invisible — this is the masterclass beat of the post.
7. **Incentivized CTA.** $1M in cash and prizes for live attendees + "See you there."

### Psychological engine
**The withheld HOW.** Reader has heard the advice a hundred times and never once the method. Naming the fatigue buys trust; the testable dream state (2 weeks unreachable) creates desire; the how-list satisfies. Funnel converts the trust at peak satisfaction.

### Transfers to Lior ✅
- **The X-screenshot visual. Promote-track candidate** (see above — actionable this week, no design work needed).
- Cliché acknowledgment + "But how?" pivot. Lior version: name the advice founders are sick of hearing, then deliver the operator's actual mechanics.
- Vivid testable dream state. He has the real one: the company ran while he negotiated the Fiverr deal for 23 days, and runs today under a macro-management cadence.
- Quantified meeting math — CEO-credible arithmetic. His canon has the 70% delegation rule, weekly status calls, bi-weekly OKR rhythm.
- Delegation topic passes the CEO filter at full marks; it's literally his "I stopped editing decks, reviewing minor expenses, and scheduling my own meetings" confession territory.

### Dies at the door ❌
- The entire funnel half: event, prizes, registration arrow, "See you there." No channels, no newsletter, no events for Lior.
- The ellipsis cliffhanger into an ad — reads as bait once you see it.
- "(even better)" parenthetical — hard rule.
- 3 sections is HIS Rule of 3 ✓ but the sections balloon past our char window with the funnel attached. His version is the 3 tips, compressed, funnel amputated.

- **Externalized quotable in the visual: 3/4 among visual posts** (#3 handwritten note, #4 typographic card, #6 tweet screenshot). THIRD CONFIRMATION → RECOMMEND PROMOTING: the visual carries the quotable claim, the text carries the story/method.
