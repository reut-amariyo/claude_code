## Post 1 — The $1 bug at 3 AM
**Donor:** Christine Carrillo, "Working smarter, not harder is a beautiful lie." 1,141 reactions / 292 comments.
**Why this donor:** a contrarian quote-hook, a cost story in 3 beats, a fork with two choices, a 3-step fix, one result number, a principle, a question. The $1 bug has every one of those beats natively.

| Donor beat | Carrillo | Lior fill |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Contrarian quote hook | "Working smarter, not harder is a beautiful lie." | "Never let customers see you sweat" is a beautiful lie. |
| 2. Two-line stance | Hard work first / never burn out (parenthetical confession) | Owning the mistake comes first. Line 2 counter-hook: I learned it at 3 AM. |
| 3. The cost story, 3 arrow beats | Built at breakneck speed → burnt out → concussion → wake-up call | Ran a test on one product → every product in the system went to $1 → hundreds sold in hours → a customer messaged at 3 AM |
| 4. The misread (new, Lior-only beat) | none | I said congratulations. She said they sold for a dollar. |
| 5. The fork | slow down or keep risking health | Blame eBay, or go live and own it |
| 6. The 3-step fix | document / automate / delegate | Sat with her to message her buyers so eBay would not block her / went live on Facebook the next day / hired her |
| 7. Result number | "20 hours a week" | Nobody left. A competitor offered 20% to poach us. Nobody moved. She ran a 100-person support team. |
| 8. Principle | "Smart work comes from experience." | People forgive the mistake. They never forgive the hiding. |
| 9. Question close | "What's one thing you learned that saved you hours?" | What did you do in the 6 hours after your worst bug? |

**Hook options (copy shape verbatim):**
- "Never let customers see you sweat" is a beautiful lie.
- "Move fast and break things" is a beautiful lie.
**Visual:** one number. Photo card, candid Lior, headline "EVERY PRODUCT WENT TO $1 AT 3 AM", highlighted "$1". Never a diagram of the post.
**Strip from donor:** the ♻️ repost line, "Follow X for more", the playbook link, arrow glyphs (plain lines instead), "I wish it didn't take a concussion" (Lior does not wish, he states).
**Guardrails:** never "no options, no equity" for the hire; don't blame eBay in any beat; "$1" and "3 AM" verbatim.

## Post 2 — Dad's password

## Post 3 — Judo third place, "if he can, so can I"
**Donor:** The Wise Mind Group, Branson chalkboard "say yes, then learn how to do it later". 825 reactions / 215 comments.
**Why this donor:** "You've done this your whole life" names a lifelong pattern, credits it for the career, then shows the same pattern's cost. Lior's comparison engine has exactly that two-sided shape, and the host's own episode title proves the mom beat lands.

| Donor beat | Wise Mind | Lior fill |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook, pattern named | "You've done this your whole life." | You've heard this your whole life. |
| 2. The pattern in one line | "Said yes before you were ready and figured it out." | "Look how good HE is." |
| 3. Proof it built you | career, trust, title, results | Third place in judo at 10, and the whole ride home was about the kid in first. Basketball, same. It turned into one sentence: if he can, so can I. |
| 4. Where it kept working | "You are wired to leap." | Founding AutoDS against competitors 10 years older, with funding and connections. If they can, so can I. |
| 5. "Except when..." pivot | mental health | Except the voice never turns off. |
| 6. The cost, specific | scans, providers, research before one step | Thousands of paying customers, and my mother still asked if I could get a programmer job. Last Saturday she asked it about my CTO. |
| 7. The reframe | "the same pattern that made you exceptional is the one keeping you locked" | The comparison that built the company is the same one that says it is never enough. |
| 8. What we do about it | the 10-week group | I keep the engine and drop the scoreboard: compare to who I was, not to who is in first. |
| 9. Close | registration link | Mom, he's not supposed to find a job as a programmer. (flat punchline, then open question: who was the kid in first place for you?) |

**Hook options:**
- You've heard this your whole life.
- Third place in judo. The ride home was about first.
**Visual:** one quote artifact, like the chalkboard. Quote card: "If he can, so can I." Or the mom line as the card, since the host chose it as the episode title.
**Strip from donor:** the program pitch (beats 8-9 of the original), "nervous system", "modalities", the date and link, the poem-style line breaks (Lior writes in sentences).
**Guardrails:** no imposter syndrome, no fear confession. The cost beat is a pattern with a fix, not a wound. "Never enough" appears once, as the host's phrase he agreed with, not as his self-description.
