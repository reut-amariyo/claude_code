## The number, so it holds up in comments

Standard two-sided A/B sizing, 95% confidence, 80% power, 2% baseline conversion,
detecting a 20% relative lift (2.0% → 2.4%): n ≈ 16·p(1−p)/δ² = 16(0.0196)/(0.004)²
≈ 19,600 visitors **per variant**. Hence "roughly 20,000 visits per version" and
"40,000 before the number means anything" for a two-arm test.
Keep the word "roughly". Do not quote a Lior/AutoDS-specific conversion rate; we don't have one logged.

1,000 store versions in one click. Zero buyers.

That was the demo in a reel my team sent me this week. An AI builds the store, writes 100 product pages from one photo, spins up a thousand variants, and drops a reviews wall on top.

Here is the part the demo skips.

To prove one version beats another by 20%, at a 2% conversion rate, you need roughly 20,000 visits per version. Two versions, 40,000 visits, before the number means anything.

A thousand versions is not a test. It is a slot machine with a progress bar.

So the scarce thing moved. It used to be the store. Now it is traffic, and the patience to point it at three ideas instead of a thousand.

Three rules we run by:

Test two things at a time, never more.

Give a version 20,000 visits or do not call it.

Kill anything you cannot explain to a supplier in one sentence.

Generating options is free now. Choosing between them is the whole job.

What got scarce in your business the day building got free?
