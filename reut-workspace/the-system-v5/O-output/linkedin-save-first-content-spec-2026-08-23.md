# Save-first content spec for Lior's LinkedIn

Reut's decision, 2026-08-23: **the main axis becomes content built to be saved.** Roughly
every fourth post is personal. References she named: Chris Donnelly, and Jasmin Alić for the
way he writes rather than what he writes about.

I read both accounts directly rather than describing them from memory. What follows is what
their posts actually look like, with the numbers, and what survives contact with Lior's rules.

---

## 1. What I found, with the numbers

### Chris Donnelly — 1,254,275 followers

Five most recent posts. Every single one carried media. Length ran 1,252 to 2,501 characters,
which is well above our 750-1,200 rule.

| Reactions | Comments | Reposts |
|---|---|---|
| 219 | 156 | 7 |
| 479 | 293 | 16 |
| 294 | 168 | 7 |
| **1,647** | **2,136** | 75 |
| 656 | 403 | 29 |

His standout post has **more comments than reactions**, and the structure explains why:

1. Hook, 8 words, superlative plus a named tool. "This Claude x LinkedIn lead gen prompt is insane."
2. Credential beat carrying a number. "It's one of 12 I used to generate 20k leads in 60 days."
3. The giveaway, stated immediately. "I'm giving the full set away for free."
4. A permission line that lowers the stakes. "Growing on LinkedIn actually isn't that hard."
5. A shared observation. "You've seen it, I've seen it: creators with 100,000s of followers making zero money."
6. The manifest. Seven-plus checklist items, each one concrete and specific.
7. Comment-to-receive.

**The save trigger is the manifest.** Not the writing. A reader scans seven specific,
enumerable items and thinks "I will need this later." That is what a save is.

  Then, it's beautiful."

## 2. The honest problem with copying Donnelly

His save engine runs on a **lead-magnet giveaway aimed at creators and marketers**. A prompt
pack, comment-to-receive, "turn attention into revenue." Every part of that fails a rule we
already hold:

- Prompt packs are productivity and tool tips. Banned.
- "Turn your following into revenue" is creator-economy content. Fails the CEO relevance filter.
- Comment-to-receive and repost-to-win are engagement bait. Never for Lior.
- ✅ and 👇 emoji manifests plus designed graphic cards are marketing-team visuals. Banned by
  the four-visual-families rule.
- 2,500 characters is double our cap.

Copy his format and Lior becomes a LinkedIn-growth creator. That is the wrong person.

**What transfers is the trigger underneath it, not the format on top.** People save what they
expect to *need again*. A save is a bet on future utility, never a reaction to enjoyment.
Donnelly manufactures that bet with a concrete, enumerable, specific artifact. So can Lior,
with a payload only he can produce.

## 3. The five save-first formats for Lior

All operator-seat, none are tool tips, all enumerable.

### A. The decision checklist
"The 7 questions we ask before an AI agent touches a customer order."
The saveable object is the question list. Only someone running the company can write it.

### B. The threshold post
The internal numbers he actually manages by. What he kills a test at, what he doubles down at,
what margin makes him walk. People save numbers to benchmark themselves against.

### C. The teardown scorecard
Take a public commerce or AI event and break out the criteria he judges it by. The event ages.
**The criteria are the artifact**, and that is what gets saved.

### D. The briefing with a "so what" list
The weekly operator's read on AI commerce. The saveable half is the short list of what to
actually do about it.

### E. The anti-checklist
"5 things we stopped doing after the exit." Negative lists are rarer, so they get saved more.

## 4. The line craft, taken from Jasmin

- One idea per line. Blank line between nearly every line. No paragraph blocks.
- Odd-numbered lists, 3 / 5 / 7, matching our existing rule.
- Every list item concrete and specific enough to be useful alone. Vague items kill saves.
- A parallel pair with a turn is allowed, sparingly. It goes formulaic fast, and it sits close
  to the banned "not X, it's Y" template. One per post maximum, and never as the closing line.
- Close on a real question, not a manufactured one.

**Dies at the door, from both references:** ♻️ repost CTAs · comment-to-receive lead magnets ·
✅ and 👇 emoji manifests · parentheses, which both of them use constantly · heart emoji sign-offs ·
coach voice · designed Canva-style checklist graphics.

## 5. Mechanics that change

| | Current rule | Save-first |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 750-1,200 chars | **up to 1,500 for save-first posts.** A manifest needs room. Deliberate exception, same as the Archetype 2 exception |
| Visual | 4 families, no marketing graphics | **unchanged, and it matters more here.** The artifact must be the real thing photographed or screenshotted, a genuine internal checklist or dashboard, never a designed card. More credible, and it fits the access thesis |
| Ending | engagement gap open | **the manifest IS the reason to save.** Keep the open question, but the list carries the post |
| Mix | — | **3 save-first : 1 personal**, per Reut |

---

## 6. What to measure

Saves are visible per post in Creator Analytics. Start logging them in the daily scan alongside
impressions and the follower delta. The hypothesis worth testing in September:

> A save-first post produces more net followers per impression than a reach post.

If saves rise and followers do not, the format is winning the wrong prize and we change again.

---

## 7. Caveats

- Five recent posts per account is a snapshot, not a study. Donnelly's older library may skew
  more toward carousels and infographics than what he happens to be posting this week.
- Jasmin's comment ratio is exceptional and consistent across all five, so that finding is solid.
- Follower counts read live from their profiles on 2026-08-23.
