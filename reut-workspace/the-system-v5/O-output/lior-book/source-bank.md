# Book Source Bank — collected material in Lior's voice

Running collection for the book. Every entry: source, the material itself, and
which book theme it feeds. New recordings from Reut get mined and logged here.
Format for new entries: date added, source, verbatim quotes first, paraphrase second.

## How to add to this file (for future sessions)

1. Verbatim beats paraphrase. His actual sentences are the book's voice.
2. Log the source precisely (recording filename, podcast episode, date).
3. Tag each entry: [NO] dealing-with-no, [BOOT] bootstrap mechanics, [FRAME]
   frameworks and mental models, [STORY] narrative scenes, [QUOTE] chapter-ready lines.
4. Never invent or embellish. Garbled transcription gets flagged, not fixed.

## From Real Life Superpowers E97 (added 2026-08-12, transcript in O-output/podcast-transcripts/)

Full analysis: M-memory/lior-spoken-voice-e97-podcast-analysis.md — read it before
drafting any chapter. Highlights mapped to book themes:

### [FRAME] The "and then what?" chain — candidate spine of the whole book
"What's the worst case? I will waste time. Okay, and then what? I will become
older and need to raise a family and not have money. Okay, and then what?"
He chains it until the fear collapses. Appears 4+ times in the episode. This is
the book's teachable mechanism.

### [NO] The doubters, in triple repetition
"So many people said that AutoDS will fail. So many people didn't believe in it.
So many people said that there are already strong competitors."
Payoff, stated flat: "my competitors in the beginning, that we also acquired them
in the end."

### [NO][STORY] The parents who never said yes
Conservative family. Until the exit they kept asking when he'd get a degree, why
he isn't a developer like his friends. Even today they can't grasp the exit is
real; they ask him to stop publishing about it. Strongest humility material in
the canon — a "no" that never converted to yes, and he built anyway.

### [NO][STORY] The suspension that became AutoDS
eBay suspended the stores that were his main income at 21. Answer: open 100
stores. Management broke, so he built software for himself, conferences turned it
into a product, users became accidental beta testers. He didn't know what
fundraising was — which is WHY AutoDS was bootstrapped. The founding "no" of the
entire company.

### [BOOT][STORY] The e-cigarette business, age 14
No budget for stock, so: collect payment upfront, order from China, deliver at
school ~30 days later. Back-to-back cash flow before he knew the term. Bootstrap
mechanics as a school kid. School threatened expulsion; he stopped — knowing
which risks not to take is part of the story.

### [BOOT][STORY] De-risked, not reckless
Built AutoDS while working as a freelance developer. "I'm not encouraging people
to leave their job and start a business." The worst case must stay survivable.
This is the book's anti-guru differentiator.

### [FRAME] The advice filter
"I don't take advice from people that are not in the place where I want to be."
Paired with: "I asked what's the difference between them and myself? Why can I
not do that better than them?"

### [QUOTE] Chapter-ready lines (cleaned, from his mouth)
- "I look at life as a game."
- "It's always about what's the downside, and how big is the downside really."
- "Okay, and then what?"
- "I prefer to do a mistake and then clean it."
- "If I elevate myself, the business will also elevate."
- "Believe that it will be okay... just believe in yourself more."
- "Ignore what people say and really believe in myself... because I want to do it my way."

### [FRAME] Third-party framings from the episode article (ideas only, rewrite the sentences)
- The game mindset "changes the emotional cost of entrepreneurship. Failure
  becomes feedback. Risk becomes calculated."
- The advice filter as PERMISSION to stop assuming someone else belongs where you
  want to be more than you do.
- "Most of the limits they believed in were rules they never had to accept in the
  first place."

## From the Tony Robbins Business Mastery debrief (added 2026-08-23)

Sources: "New Recording 22.m4a" (5:12, Hebrew), "New Recording 23.m4a" (20:38, Hebrew),
"קו פאונדר 2.m4a" (1:45, English), all recorded 20-23 Aug 2026.
Transcripts: O-output/lior-transcripts/tony-robbins-business-mastery-2026-08-20-en.md
and cofounder-personal-development-2026-08-23-en.md.
Hebrew material below is translated, so treat it as close paraphrase, not verbatim.
The co-founder quotes ARE verbatim, recorded in English.

### [FRAME] The business is a child
Baby, toddler at year three, teenager, adult, old age, death. His own words on the
teenager stage: "the business is running, everyone is running, everyone is making a
ton of mistakes, but that's what a teenager is. He'll drive 160 on the highway, and
maybe he crashes, maybe he doesn't, but that's life."
Book value: gives the AutoDS timeline a spine that isn't a founder-genius arc.

### [STORY] His own teenage years, named as mistakes
"We got blocked on Shopify because we abused the policy. On eBay we got blocked too
because they shut down our app. Because we didn't meet the regulation. Because we
were a teenager running." Two new named failures, both his own fault, both survivable.
Guardrail: this is self-criticism, not criticism of Shopify. Keep it that way.

### [FRAME] Recognize, use, create
Three patterns critical to a business owner: pattern recognition (see the trend),
pattern utilization (ride it), pattern creation (make it). His verdict on himself:
"We had both the recognition and the utilization, and then we sold the company. We
never created a trend. We never created a niche. We never created anything."
Book value: the most honest self-assessment in the canon. Refuses the visionary story.

### [QUOTE] The bucket list
Everything that excited him was either an experience or something for another person.
Buy his parents a house. Pay off his wife's parents' mortgage. Fly both families
business class. Watches, Ferrari and Ducati were on the list and did nothing for him.
Book value: the anti-materialism beat after an exit, in specifics rather than platitude.

### [FRAME] The ice bath is not about the ice
"The real training isn't the dopamine it releases, or the health. It's about training
your brain that when you said something, it happens." Discipline reframed as a promise
you keep to yourself. Pairs with the Wim Hof material.

### [BOOT] Compounding as the whole plan
He refuses long business plans but runs on a fixed improvement rate: "I don't build a
10-year business plan, but my plan will always improve the business by a certain
percentage every quarter, every half year, every year." Plus the $100 to $110 to
$10M-in-ten-years illustration. Book value: the bootstrap alternative to fundraising math.

### [STORY] The song anchor
Shazams the song playing right after every conference breakthrough and keeps them in a
Spotify playlist. Wim Hof songs, Tony Robbins songs. "Nobody can really understand what
each one connects to." Small, specific, humanizing scene.

### [QUOTE][NO] The co-founder, verbatim in English
"If it's with co-founders, if it's with the guild, like a couple, it's important both
sides do the personal development together. Because otherwise it creates gaps between
them, which is hard to bridge."
"You're coming back home, you think that you are another new person, and actually
nothing really changed in these five, seven days in the world."
"Now we will go back home and nobody will really understand what we experienced. And
that it's okay, and we need to let it go, and just to know what we experienced and that
we changed. It doesn't mean that the world changed."
Book value: the loneliness of the founder who grows faster than the people around him.
Strongest new emotional material in this batch, and it is in his own English.

### Flagged, do not use yet
The Jar of Hope story (block 16). The audio was unclear and Lior himself questions on
tape whether it should become content. Verify at jarofhope.org before touching it.

## Source: IG team meeting, 2026-09-02 (Plaud, 2h44m)

Full analysis: `the-system-v5/M-memory/ig-team-meeting-2026-09-02-analysis.md`.
The Hebrew auto-transcript is rough; every line below is paraphrase unless marked verbatim.

### [BOOT] How he would actually start today, in six steps
Asked point blank how to take a product to the next level, he answered without hedging:
go to design partners first, people close to you with a real interest in using the thing,
and work with them free because the outcome is their payment. Check the product is sticky
with them before anything else. Then start charging. Distribute through communities,
organic, social, and conferences, talking to people directly. Read competitors' reviews to
find where they are weak and market against exactly those points. And solve your own
problem, because if it is your problem the people around you have it too, and they are the
route into their friends and their communities.
Book value: this is the bootstrap go-to-market chapter, already sequenced, in his voice.
No fundraising step appears anywhere in it.

### [FRAME] Why he buys companies
Three questions: what value does this bring to my company, can I get inside and grow it,
and what is the ceiling of this acquisition. The method is real due diligence, knowing both
the target's superpowers and where it falls down. He buys what would take too long or cost
too much to build.

### [STORY] Yaballe, the acquisition that was really a market
Yaballe had built a community, which AutoDS never had and which takes time and money to
build. They also sold to agencies, while AutoDS only sold to end customers. So buying them
did not add revenue, it opened a market. Cleanest illustration in the book of buying a gap
rather than buying a number.

### [STORY] Forbes said no three times
Rejected three times before the exit, because he did not have the stamp yet. Accepted after
it. Includes the scene of reading the email with his partners, and then having to explain to
his mother what Forbes 30 Under 30 even was. Pairs directly with the "no" spine of the book.

### [STORY] Calling his father after the deal was signed
He called his father to tell him. Told on tape as a moment he wanted to keep. Emotional
anchor for the exit chapter; the details are thin on the recording and need a second pass
with him.

### [FLAG] The detention story
Referred to on tape as the prison story. He describes being placed under arrest / house
detention after an argument with someone. He wants it out through PR first; Reut argues a
big article burns the card. Unresolved, and the audio is unclear. Do not write from this
until he tells it properly on a clean recording.

### [FLAG][DO NOT USE] The reviews tactic
He offered, as a content idea, asking friends and contacts to leave reviews for a product
they never used so it looks like social proof. It is a real thing he says people do, and it
is unusable as advice under his name. Noted here only so nobody mines it later thinking it
was missed.
