# Book Source Bank — collected material in Lior's voice

Running collection for the book. Every entry: source, the material itself, and
which book theme it feeds. New recordings from Reut get mined and logged here.
Format for new entries: date added, source, verbatim quotes first, paraphrase second.

## How to add to this file (for future sessions)

1. Verbatim beats paraphrase. His actual sentences are the book's voice.
2. Log the source precisely (recording filename, podcast episode, date).
3. Tag each entry: [NO] dealing-with-no, [BOOT] bootstrap mechanics, [FRAME]
   frameworks and mental models, [STORY] narrative scenes, [QUOTE] chapter-ready lines.
4. Never invent or embellish. Garbled transcription gets flagged, not fixed.

## From Real Life Superpowers E97 (added 2026-08-12, transcript in O-output/podcast-transcripts/)

Full analysis: M-memory/lior-spoken-voice-e97-podcast-analysis.md — read it before
drafting any chapter. Highlights mapped to book themes:

### [FRAME] The "and then what?" chain — candidate spine of the whole book
"What's the worst case? I will waste time. Okay, and then what? I will become
older and need to raise a family and not have money. Okay, and then what?"
He chains it until the fear collapses. Appears 4+ times in the episode. This is
the book's teachable mechanism.

### [NO] The doubters, in triple repetition
"So many people said that AutoDS will fail. So many people didn't believe in it.
So many people said that there are already strong competitors."
Payoff, stated flat: "my competitors in the beginning, that we also acquired them
in the end."

### [NO][STORY] The parents who never said yes
Conservative family. Until the exit they kept asking when he'd get a degree, why
he isn't a developer like his friends. Even today they can't grasp the exit is
real; they ask him to stop publishing about it. Strongest humility material in
the canon — a "no" that never converted to yes, and he built anyway.

### [NO][STORY] The suspension that became AutoDS
eBay suspended the stores that were his main income at 21. Answer: open 100
stores. Management broke, so he built software for himself, conferences turned it
into a product, users became accidental beta testers. He didn't know what
fundraising was — which is WHY AutoDS was bootstrapped. The founding "no" of the
entire company.

### [BOOT][STORY] The e-cigarette business, age 14
No budget for stock, so: collect payment upfront, order from China, deliver at
school ~30 days later. Back-to-back cash flow before he knew the term. Bootstrap
mechanics as a school kid. School threatened expulsion; he stopped — knowing
which risks not to take is part of the story.

### [BOOT][STORY] De-risked, not reckless
Built AutoDS while working as a freelance developer. "I'm not encouraging people
to leave their job and start a business." The worst case must stay survivable.
This is the book's anti-guru differentiator.

### [FRAME] The advice filter
"I don't take advice from people that are not in the place where I want to be."
Paired with: "I asked what's the difference between them and myself? Why can I
not do that better than them?"

### [QUOTE] Chapter-ready lines (cleaned, from his mouth)
- "I look at life as a game."
- "It's always about what's the downside, and how big is the downside really."
- "Okay, and then what?"
- "I prefer to do a mistake and then clean it."
- "If I elevate myself, the business will also elevate."
- "Believe that it will be okay... just believe in yourself more."
- "Ignore what people say and really believe in myself... because I want to do it my way."

### [FRAME] Third-party framings from the episode article (ideas only, rewrite the sentences)
- The game mindset "changes the emotional cost of entrepreneurship. Failure
  becomes feedback. Risk becomes calculated."
- The advice filter as PERMISSION to stop assuming someone else belongs where you
  want to be more than you do.
- "Most of the limits they believed in were rules they never had to accept in the
  first place."

## From the Tony Robbins Business Mastery debrief (added 2026-08-23)

Sources: "New Recording 22.m4a" (5:12, Hebrew), "New Recording 23.m4a" (20:38, Hebrew),
"קו פאונדר 2.m4a" (1:45, English), all recorded 20-23 Aug 2026.
Transcripts: O-output/lior-transcripts/tony-robbins-business-mastery-2026-08-20-en.md
and cofounder-personal-development-2026-08-23-en.md.
Hebrew material below is translated, so treat it as close paraphrase, not verbatim.
The co-founder quotes ARE verbatim, recorded in English.

### [FRAME] The business is a child
Baby, toddler at year three, teenager, adult, old age, death. His own words on the
teenager stage: "the business is running, everyone is running, everyone is making a
ton of mistakes, but that's what a teenager is. He'll drive 160 on the highway, and
maybe he crashes, maybe he doesn't, but that's life."
Book value: gives the AutoDS timeline a spine that isn't a founder-genius arc.

### [STORY] His own teenage years, named as mistakes
"We got blocked on Shopify because we abused the policy. On eBay we got blocked too
because they shut down our app. Because we didn't meet the regulation. Because we
were a teenager running." Two new named failures, both his own fault, both survivable.
Guardrail: this is self-criticism, not criticism of Shopify. Keep it that way.

### [FRAME] Recognize, use, create
Three patterns critical to a business owner: pattern recognition (see the trend),
pattern utilization (ride it), pattern creation (make it). His verdict on himself:
"We had both the recognition and the utilization, and then we sold the company. We
never created a trend. We never created a niche. We never created anything."
Book value: the most honest self-assessment in the canon. Refuses the visionary story.

### [QUOTE] The bucket list
Everything that excited him was either an experience or something for another person.
Buy his parents a house. Pay off his wife's parents' mortgage. Fly both families
business class. Watches, Ferrari and Ducati were on the list and did nothing for him.
Book value: the anti-materialism beat after an exit, in specifics rather than platitude.

### [FRAME] The ice bath is not about the ice
"The real training isn't the dopamine it releases, or the health. It's about training
your brain that when you said something, it happens." Discipline reframed as a promise
you keep to yourself. Pairs with the Wim Hof material.

### [BOOT] Compounding as the whole plan
He refuses long business plans but runs on a fixed improvement rate: "I don't build a
10-year business plan, but my plan will always improve the business by a certain
percentage every quarter, every half year, every year." Plus the $100 to $110 to
$10M-in-ten-years illustration. Book value: the bootstrap alternative to fundraising math.

### [STORY] The song anchor
Shazams the song playing right after every conference breakthrough and keeps them in a
Spotify playlist. Wim Hof songs, Tony Robbins songs. "Nobody can really understand what
each one connects to." Small, specific, humanizing scene.

### [QUOTE][NO] The co-founder, verbatim in English
"If it's with co-founders, if it's with the guild, like a couple, it's important both
sides do the personal development together. Because otherwise it creates gaps between
them, which is hard to bridge."
"You're coming back home, you think that you are another new person, and actually
nothing really changed in these five, seven days in the world."
"Now we will go back home and nobody will really understand what we experienced. And
that it's okay, and we need to let it go, and just to know what we experienced and that
we changed. It doesn't mean that the world changed."
Book value: the loneliness of the founder who grows faster than the people around him.
Strongest new emotional material in this batch, and it is in his own English.

### Flagged, do not use yet
The Jar of Hope story (block 16). The audio was unclear and Lior himself questions on
tape whether it should become content. Verify at jarofhope.org before touching it.

## Source: IG team meeting, 2026-09-02 (Plaud, 2h44m)

Full analysis: `the-system-v5/M-memory/ig-team-meeting-2026-09-02-analysis.md`.
The Hebrew auto-transcript is rough; every line below is paraphrase unless marked verbatim.

### [BOOT] How he would actually start today, in six steps
Asked point blank how to take a product to the next level, he answered without hedging:
go to design partners first, people close to you with a real interest in using the thing,
and work with them free because the outcome is their payment. Check the product is sticky
with them before anything else. Then start charging. Distribute through communities,
organic, social, and conferences, talking to people directly. Read competitors' reviews to
find where they are weak and market against exactly those points. And solve your own
problem, because if it is your problem the people around you have it too, and they are the
route into their friends and their communities.
Book value: this is the bootstrap go-to-market chapter, already sequenced, in his voice.
No fundraising step appears anywhere in it.

### [FRAME] Why he buys companies
Three questions: what value does this bring to my company, can I get inside and grow it,
and what is the ceiling of this acquisition. The method is real due diligence, knowing both
the target's superpowers and where it falls down. He buys what would take too long or cost
too much to build.

### [STORY] Yaballe, the acquisition that was really a market
Yaballe had built a community, which AutoDS never had and which takes time and money to
build. They also sold to agencies, while AutoDS only sold to end customers. So buying them
did not add revenue, it opened a market. Cleanest illustration in the book of buying a gap
rather than buying a number.

### [STORY] Forbes said no three times
Rejected three times before the exit, because he did not have the stamp yet. Accepted after
it. Includes the scene of reading the email with his partners, and then having to explain to
his mother what Forbes 30 Under 30 even was. Pairs directly with the "no" spine of the book.

### [STORY] Calling his father after the deal was signed
He called his father to tell him. Told on tape as a moment he wanted to keep. Emotional
anchor for the exit chapter; the details are thin on the recording and need a second pass
with him.

### [FLAG] The detention story
Referred to on tape as the prison story. He describes being placed under arrest / house
detention after an argument with someone. He wants it out through PR first; Reut argues a
big article burns the card. Unresolved, and the audio is unclear. Do not write from this
until he tells it properly on a clean recording.

### [FLAG][DO NOT USE] The reviews tactic
He offered, as a content idea, asking friends and contacts to leave reviews for a product
they never used so it looks like social proof. It is a real thing he says people do, and it
is unusable as advice under his name. Noted here only so nobody mines it later thinking it
was missed.

## Source: "99 Is Not 100" Hebrew podcast, host Alex Rif, episode "כן אמא, אני עדיין יכול להיות מתכנת" (added 2026-09-15, https://open.spotify.com/episode/5rFy8iBmw6P3N6N28IhlvW, transcripts in O-output/podcast-transcripts/99-lo-100-*)

Full analysis: M-memory/lior-spoken-voice-99-lo-100-podcast-analysis-2026-09-15.md.
Everything below is translated from Hebrew: close paraphrase, not verbatim English.
Lior is the show's first Israel-born guest; 38 minutes, unscripted.

### [NO][QUOTE] The thesis, unprompted, in one sentence
"I really believe in no. Our lives are made of many no's that we receive, and
it's our choice: do I take that no, or do I make it a yes."
Book value: this is the title-page line. He said it without being asked about
the book, about rejection, or about anything. Candidate epigraph.

### [NO][STORY] Judo, third place
Won third in a judo competition; the parents' focus went to the kid in first.
Basketball team, same: "how beautifully HE plays." The no that became the engine:
"if he can, so can I", which he then aimed at older, funded AutoDS competitors.
Book value: the origin of the whole "dealing with no" reflex, in one scene with a
podium on it.

### [NO][STORY] The wrong army team
Landed in an IT technician team, not development. "I'm not doing anything until
I'm moved to the team I want." Opened the books, taught himself the course,
demanded to be tested. "Nobody came to me and said prove yourself. I said it."
Then: "Anyone can demand that chance."
Book value: the no you convert yourself, no rescuer. Pairs with the eBay
suspension story from E97 as the two founding no's.

### [NO][STORY] Mom, last Saturday
With thousands of paying customers she still asked if he could go be a
programmer at another company. This week she asked whether his co-founder, the
CTO, "can still find a job as a programmer". Punchline, flat: "Mom, he's not
supposed to find a job as a programmer."
Book value: the no that never converts, retold with a date and a laugh. Use with
the E97 version (they asked him to stop posting about the exit).

### [BOOT][STORY] Dad's password
Dad bribed him: one more year of Russian evening school = his own computer.
Punishment = a password on the PC. He learned to hack it. Dad escalated to
removing the power cable. He kept a spare cable. "That was part of my game."
Book value: the first system he ever worked around was his father. Chapter-
opener material for the hacker-to-builder arc.

### [BOOT][STORY] Bootstrapped by ignorance, then by choice
"When I started I didn't know at all that raising money was an option." First
developer hired in Armenia because he was cheap; then a high-schooler working
24/7. Investors came years later: "not my style, I didn't want to waste time on
decks." AI era verdict: less money to build, more creativity to win; deep tech,
medical and hardware need capital, SaaS does not.
Book value: the anti-fundraising chapter, with the honest admission that the
first reason was not knowing, not principle.

### [BOOT][STORY] The $1 bug, fullest telling
Self-test on one product repriced tens of thousands of products (avg $40-50) to
$1. Hundreds sold in hours. 3 AM message ("a normal working hour for me"); he
said "congratulations"; she said they sold for a dollar. Sat with her, did not
blame eBay. She was an Israeli in Finland with no job; he had wrecked her
business; hired her with no money, $200 a month then $400, rising with growth;
she became Head of Customer Support with ~100 people. Facebook Live next day.
Nobody left. A competitor offered 20% to poach AutoDS customers. Nobody moved.
Book value: the transparency chapter is now complete. Lead with the 3 AM
"congratulations" misread. Guardrail: he says "not options, not a percentage";
never write that line in any draft.

### [FRAME] Unreasonable goals, two rules
From The Science of Scaling, which he listened to: set an unreasonable size goal
because the step size follows ("if I want billions I won't take $100K-a-month
steps"), and an unreasonable deadline because a month and a day produce the same
deck. Applied to himself: billionaire by 45, "a million lives may not be enough,
I need 100 million."
Book value: the goal-setting chapter, and the public record of the 45 target.

### [FRAME] Mindset yes, skillset no
Told his coach the day before recording: "I'm already there in mindset. In
skillset, not yet." Took on too many projects to reach the goal, found himself
drowning. His fix: jump in first, organise second.
Book value: the honest late chapter, where the exit is done and the next
mountain is bigger than his current tools.

### [FRAME] Advisors as a line item
A high share of expenses was always advisors, from day one. They rotate because
you outgrow them. Domain-specific down to "paid ads in one channel". Many are
coffee, not retainers: "especially in Israel, people are happy to help. It's very
rare that someone says no to a coffee."
Book value: the how-a-bootstrapper-buys-experience chapter.

### [FRAME] Openness as a management system
"I'm the most open person in the world, for better and worse." Set in the first
interview: "This is how I work, sorry. You can take it or not, but I'll always be
the most honest there is." Then: "I give you the space. Run, fall, get up, fail,
I'm for it. But I always tell the truth."
Book value: the people chapter; the dollar bug is its origin story.

### [QUOTE] Chapter-ready lines (translated, close paraphrase)
- "Nobody is born a CEO."
- "If you don't become a CEO, you won't be a good CEO."
- "The company advances as far as the CEO advances as a person."
- "Don't have it? I'll have it. He's better? No, I'm better."
- "Anyone can demand that chance."
- "Freedom is the value that leads my life."
- "If you do something, do it to the maximum."

## Source: two voice recordings with Reut, 2026-09-22
Transcript: `O-output/agency-briefs/lior-recordings-2026-09-22-transcript-he.md`. Hebrew,
unscripted, him thinking out loud about post ideas. Two usable frames, one image.

### [FRAME] The cheapest channel left is being a person
"In the end, with all the AI, we are losing some of our humanity. And the cheapest
way is personal brands. It is organic content. People connect to people. And in the
end a personal brand is also a brand people connect to, and from there you sell
anything."
Book value: the distribution chapter. A bootstrapper's argument for why attention
you own beats attention you buy, made from the AI era rather than from 2015
growth-hacking. Pairs with the no-investors spine, he never had budget to buy reach.

### [FRAME] Record everything, so the machine knows you better than you remember
Every meeting recorded, every thought sent as a voice note to a WhatsApp group. A
year of it now sits in one brain: everything he thinks, everything he does, his whole
background, including his coaching sessions and his sessions with a psychologist. His
reason, flat: "so that Claude knows me as well as anything can." His advice to anyone
starting: "just record yourself, without stopping. More and more and more, and at
some point it already knows, and you yourself have forgotten it."
Book value: the how-this-book-exists chapter. A dyslexic, audio-first founder
building an external memory is the literal method behind the manuscript. Also the
honest answer to "you have a team, I don't" — his answer was that the AI is the
leverage, not the headcount.

### [FRAME] Two or three big things, and nothing else is measured
After a session with his mentor he named the problem precisely: a huge amount was
happening and it was not a feeling, things really were getting done, but the big
items that would actually move the business kept getting pushed aside because they
were big and needed more thinking. His fix: an agent on Claude, set up as an
assistant, that asks him every morning what the two or three things are that he
commits to today, follows up three times a day, and gives him a report on Friday and
at month end of what he committed to versus what he completed. He still works the
twelve hours because he likes it. But "in the end what I measure is only the two or
three big things." His verdict on the alternative: "far more measurable than a
shopping list of small tasks that do not really help."
Book value: the systems chapter, and the late-stage counterpart to the early
bootstrapping grind. The interesting beat is that the fix was not doing less, it was
measuring less.

### [QUOTE] The compound-lift image (his, unprompted)
"In the gym there are compound exercises. They are not isolation, they are compound.
Someone doing a deadlift or a bench press, that is one exercise that works around 70
percent of you, and in one exercise you get the effect you would get from three or
four different ones. Because of how the exercise is built. It is the same, because in
the end one big task can be worth many small tasks, even if it takes less time."
Reut said "big". He corrected her: "Big in substance."
Book value: the best original metaphor from either tape, and the correction at the
end is pure Lior. Chapter-opening material for the systems chapter.

- "People connect to people."

- "Just record yourself, without stopping."

## From Built to Sell Radio Ep 568 (2026-10-16, English, verbatim where quoted)
Transcript: O-output/podcast-transcripts/built-to-sell-ep568-lior-pozin-transcript.md

### [NO] The PE no, his own words
$20M offer, raised to almost $38M. 5-6 months of diligence, every question "who we can fire,
what service we can decrease". The plan was to "acquire my business, and then optimize it, and
then milk it." Early employees had worked for far below market because "they believe in me."
One day before signing: "hey, I'm out. Thank you very much. But it's not for me."
The final straw: he asked to be paid MORE if he beat their own revenue targets and they said no.
The PE CEO was furious and closed the door. Book value: power-of-no chapter, the no from the
seller's chair.

### [STORY] "One day yes, one day no"
Five or six months of telling his girlfriend, now his wife, every night "we are going to exit
soon. We're going to have money," then "no, you know what, I don't want this." She backed him.
Lesson he took: run the business as if you'll run it forever, never dress the slides for a buyer.

### [NO] The competitor who said no for 2.5 years
Two older, funded, business-school brothers, the market leader when he was "just a kid."
They said no, then maybe, then almost, then sold with ~10% of the business left, at a fraction of
the first offer. Why didn't you sell earlier? "Because he didn't have trust in me." Trust was
built with beers and 2.5 years of proof. Book value: a no that cost the other side, timing.

### [STORY] The bank team that vanished on bid day
24 meetings in Boston. On bid day an email: the whole 4-person banker team moved to another bank,
emails blocked, momentum gone. "People buy founders, people buy people." Second time he made the
new banker promise he wasn't leaving, then presented 50-60 times himself.

### [STORY] Whiskey at 9pm
Fiverr was not the highest offer. It won on trust, reputation and the founders, closed over
whiskey at their office nearby at 8-9pm. "They're not buying the company to milk it."

### [STORY] The exit watch
