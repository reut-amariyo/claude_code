# The Diary of a CEO: what Steven actually books, and the angle for Lior

Built 2026-09-23 for Reut. Sources at the bottom. Everything here is evidence, not feel.

---

## 1. The finding that changes the whole pitch

**DOAC does not book founders to tell business stories anymore. Business is 1% of episodes.**

An independent audit of the channel from Oct 2020 to Sep 2026 found three distinct eras,
detected statistically across 18 measures:

| | Era 1 (Oct 2020 - Jan 2023) | Era 2 (Jan 2023 - Mar 2025) | Era 3 (Apr 2025 - Sep 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Episodes | 158 | 222 | 152 |
| Founders / execs as guests | **28-33%** | dropped to **13%** by 2024 | still low |
| Business as topic | peaked at **42%** mid-2022 | receded | **1% by 2026** |
| Health | low | **41%** | 30% |
| Scientists / academics | 2% | rising | **31% by 2026** |
| Celebrities / athletes | 35-39% | falling | **5%** |
| Median length | 94 min | 110 min | **116 min** |
| Median views | - | 2.5M | **3.4M** |
| New in this era | - | credentials in titles, warning format | **multi-guest panels and debates** |

What expanded in Era 3 instead of business: **money 13%, AI 13%, geopolitics 7%.**

**So the pitch in the 17 September brief is aimed at the 1%.** "Bootstrapped founder, 200
employees, sold to a public company" is an Era 1 pitch delivered in Era 3. It is all true and
it will get a polite no, because there is no slot shaped like it on the calendar.

We do not change Lior. We change which door we walk through.

## 2. What they actually published in the last 6 months

Straight from the podcast feed, Apr to Sep 2026, every full episode:

- Rick Rubin on Jay-Z
- The Great AI Debate: is AI an extinction threat
- Professor Jiang: three predictions on the collapse of the world order
- Vanessa Van Edwards, Dr Leanne ten Brinke, Andrew Huberman
- David Friedberg: the US empire is declining
- Ed Zitron: AI is the world's greatest scam
- The financial crash expert who predicted 2008
- Seth Godin: quit now before AI makes the choice for you
- Michael Saylor, Pete Buttigieg, Ray Dalio
- Alex Hormozi: the man who made $100M before 32
- OpenAI whistleblower, Mo Gawdat, Scott Galloway
- Death of the Middle Class: Billionaire vs Entrepreneur DEBATE
- Emergency debate: the 2026 AI crisis nobody sees coming
- JD Vance, Neil deGrasse Tyson, Michio Kaku, Graham Hancock
- plus roughly 30% health

**Zero bootstrapped software founders. Zero company-journey episodes.**

The only commercially-shaped guests got in through a non-business door: Hormozi through money
and control, Priestley through class collapse, Saylor through AI and bitcoin, Godin through the
job market. Every one of them was booked as a witness to something happening to the audience,
never as a case study of their own company.

### Their title grammar, measured across 199 full episodes from 2025-26

| Pattern | Share |
|---|---|
| `Label: Claim` with a colon | **93%** |
| Ends in or contains `!` | **81%** |
| A credential word in the title: expert, scientist, professor, Dr, whistleblower, billionaire, ex- | **50%** |
| Speaks to the reader as "you" / "your" | **43%** |
| Contains a number | 34% |
| Secrecy framing: nobody, no one, hiding, secret, they don't want you to | 10% |
| Explicit warning or urgency words | 6% |
| Debate or versus | 6% |
| Guest name in the title | only **50%** |

Half of episodes do not put the guest's name in the title. **They are not selling the person.
They are selling the claim.** Our pitch has to arrive as a claim.

### Two more mechanics worth designing for

1. **Every Friday they publish a "Most Replayed Moment" episode.** 71 of them since the start of
   2025. They measure which 90 seconds people rewind to, and they re-publish it as its own
   product. A guest who arrives with pre-identified replayable moments is doing their job for
   them.
2. **The closing tradition.** Every guest answers a handwritten question left by the previous
   guest, then leaves one for the next. It is the show's signature and its merchandise. A guest
   who arrives with their question already written is speaking their language.

### Who decides

Winnie Dunbar Nelson is Guest and Research Director at FlightStory, the company behind the show.
Stated philosophy from the company: guests who "don't just fill slots, they shape culture," chosen
with "storycraft, rigorous research and data science," using their own tools to map audience
interest and spot trends before they peak. Steven has said publicly that selection follows
curiosity: his own, his team's, or a topic visibly rising.

Practical reading: **they want proof of a rising topic, and evidence that the guest is the
primary source on it.** Not a CV.

## 3. The angle

### The door: AI, told by the only person in the room with a P&L

Their AI lane is 13% of episodes and it is currently split between two voices: the doomer
(extinction risk, 70% chance it goes wrong, AI is a scam) and the prophet (Saylor, Gawdat).
Both are forecasting.

Lior is neither. He runs a commerce company with 200 employees and 1.8M sellers, and AI has
already changed what he does, who he hires, and how much of himself he needs to show up for.
He is not predicting the effect. He is reporting it, with numbers, from inside.

And he has the demonstration that makes it television: **he has built an AI version of himself
that makes his content, and it works.** That is the Avatar Factory scoop. It turns an abstract
debate into a thing Steven can watch on a screen, next to the real guest, and say out loud
whether he can tell the difference.

That is the Friday Most Replayed Moment. It writes itself.

### The second act: the ladder everyone says is gone

Their money lane is the other 13%, and its current thesis is collapse: the death of the middle
class, the American dream is dead, buying a house is a mistake, the rich do not need you anymore.

Lior is the live counter-example, and he is not a smug one. Kicked out of school at 14. One day
in a police station and one week he was not allowed to leave the house. No degree, no investors,
no network, and a language he still fights. By 28 a public company bought the thing he built.
He does not claim it is easy. He claims the ladder moved, and he can say exactly where it moved to.

That is a society episode, not a business episode. It is also the honest answer to the show's
own current thesis, which is the kind of tension Steven books.

### The wrapper: "do not book me"

Open the whole pitch on the five reasons not to have him. It disarms the obvious objections by
saying them first, in his voice, and it is the only pitch in their inbox that opens by arguing
against itself.

1. You have never heard of me.
2. I have never finished a book. I am dyslexic.
3. No investor ever turned me down, because I never asked one.
4. I will not warn your audience about AI.
5. I am 31 and I still run the company I already sold.

Each one turns. The turn is the pitch.

## 4. The three asks, in order of how easy they are to say yes to

1. **A debate or panel slot on AI and work.** Era 3 invented this format and uses it constantly.
   Lior opposite a doomer is a two-hour booking that costs them one chair. Lowest bar, highest
   chance, and it gets him on tape with them.
2. **A solo episode** on the AI-clone reveal plus the ladder story. This is the real target.
3. **BuildYourStore integrations** in one or two episodes with a guest in commerce, Shopify,
   starting an online business, or AI for small business. Ask for upcoming guests in that space,
   the formats available, and their rates. Do not name a budget.

Lead with 2, name 1 as an easier alternative in the same breath, close on 3 as a separate line
of business so the sponsorship never contaminates the editorial ask.

Offer all three and let their team pick. Every one uses the colon, the exclamation, the claim

## 6. Language rules for everything that leaves our hands

- The category word is **e-commerce automation**. The other word never appears. Not once.
- The exit is **"a reported $92 million"** in anything DOAC's research team will fact-check.
  Their researchers will find Fiverr's 20-F, which shows $55.658M plus up to $36M in earn-out.
  "Reported" survives that. A flat $92M does not.
- Team size is **200 employees**.
- **"I did not know raising money was an option."** He was never rejected by an investor.
- He **listens**. No favourite book, no "I read".
- The private equity walk-away was **about the team**, never about holding out for a bigger number.
- The PE offer was **$30M**. Do not use any multiple against $92M in writing, the two figures do
  not reconcile cleanly.

## 7. Two things to clear with Lior before this link goes anywhere

1. **The arrest.** One day held, one week he could not leave the house, after a fight at school.
   This is a criminal-adjacent claim about a real person and their research team will ask about
   it on tape. He needs to approve the exact wording, the age, and how far he wants to go on air.
2. **Avatar Factory.** The standing rule in the system is that the product is never named in a
   public draft. The pitch page names it, behind a seal, marked under embargo. That is a
   deliberate exception and it needs his explicit yes before the link is sent.

Neither of these is a reason to drop them. They are the two strongest assets in the pitch. They
just need his signature on them first.
