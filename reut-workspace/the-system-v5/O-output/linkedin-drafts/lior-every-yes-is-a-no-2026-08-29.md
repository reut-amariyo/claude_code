↳ Every yes is a no you never say out loud. Protect the one thing this quarter is for.

None of that requires being special. It requires being willing to look boring for a few years while it compounds.

What are you waiting for that you probably never needed?

I killed three partnerships this year to protect one roadmap.

Every yes is a no you never say out loud.

After the Fiverr deal my calendar filled up with good things.
Another intro.
Another partnership.
Another founder who wants 15 minutes.

None of it looked like a mistake. That is what made it expensive.

For years I took almost all of it. A full week felt like progress.

Then I looked at what the yeses were buying. Less time on the product, less time with the people who built AutoDS with me, and a team waiting on decisions nobody else could make.

So I changed the question I ask before agreeing to anything.
Old question: is this a good opportunity.
New question: what am I giving up to say yes to this.

Three rules every request goes through now:

1. Sleep on it.
↳ Nobody needs the answer in the room. Tomorrow is still a fast answer.

2. Check it against the one thing this quarter is for.
↳ A good opportunity and the right opportunity are rarely the same thing.

3. Say the cost out loud.
↳ Name the ship date, the meeting or the dinner this yes takes from.

Protecting your calendar is how you show up fully for what you already committed to.

What did you get more selective about as your company grew?
