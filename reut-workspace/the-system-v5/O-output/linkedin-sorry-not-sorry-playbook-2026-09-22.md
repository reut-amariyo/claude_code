## Draft

--- POST STARTS ---

Founders keep telling me my playbook is outdated.

Because "nobody bootstraps anymore." Fine.

1. Sorry I never took an investor
2. Sorry we ran for two years with no website
3. Sorry I walked away from $30M the day before signing
4. Sorry I bought four competitors instead of outspending them
5. Sorry I still go home for dinner

But I will never apologize for these:

1. No business plan. Not once.

Every decision came from watching what sellers actually did. A plan written in a quiet room is still a guess.

2. Picking a co-founder like a spouse.

Michael joined in 2018 and rewrote the whole platform. Eight years later we have still never had a conversation about who owns what.

3. Giving away 70% of my job.

Founders hold decisions because holding feels like control. Holding decisions is how you become the bottleneck in your own company.

4. Hiring HR before it looked necessary.

Everyone calls it a luxury at 20 people. We crossed 200 without the year of chaos most companies pay for.

5. Saying no to life-changing money.

$30M, a day before signing. The buyer wanted to squeeze the company and cut the people who had worked below market since day one.

Two years later Fiverr closed in 23 days at three times the price.

Look.

The playbook is fine. It is just slower than the one being sold to you.

P.S. What founder advice are you tired of hearing?
