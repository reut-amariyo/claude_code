# LinkedIn post: "You're not the CEO. You're the janitor."

Built 2026-08-30 on a **borrowed proven structure**: Eric Partaker, "7 Truths New CEOs Learn
Too Late" (1,399 reactions / 208 comments / 120 reposts).
New standing rule from Reut, same date: Lior LinkedIn posts get built on structures that
already worked for someone else, not invented from scratch.

---

## 1. The donor structure, beat by beat

| # | Beat | Partaker's version | Ours |
|---|------|--------------------|------|
| 1 | Hook, 6-8 words, direct address | "I thought I was ready to be a CEO." | "You're not the CEO. You're the fucking janitor." |
| 2 | Parenthetical counter-hook, under 6 words | "(I wasn't even close.)" | "(Employee 30 told me.)" |
| 3 | Reframe in 2 short lines | title = arrival vs beginning | title = finish line vs start line |
| 4 | Authority slot, 2 lines | Egon Zehnder, 402 CEOs, 68% | the ego-title vs plumbing line |
| 5 | Personal credential, one line | "learned more in year 1 than the previous decade" | 250 people, learned it expensive |
| 6 | "7 things I wish someone told me earlier:" | same | "6 rules that sound lazy and run a 200-person company:" |
| 7 | 7 numbered items: bold claim line + 2-3 sentence proof | same | Lior's actual management practice |

**Beat 4 changed on 2026-08-30, at Reut's direction.** Partaker fills this slot with a survey
stat. Ours read as filler and the numbers needed verifying, so the slot now does the same job
with a metaphor: the title inflates you, the work is plumbing. It buys the same permission to
list 7 rules, and it sets up the janitor payoff at the close instead of interrupting it.

Word choice: **plumbing**, not a second job title. Plumber and janitor would compete as
identities. Plumbing is what he does with his day, which lets the janitor line stay the only
noun in the post. Alternates if she wants a different flavor, same slot, same length:
- "The actual job is drainage. Everything the company cannot process ends up at your desk."
- "The actual job is cleanup. You spend the day on messes you did not make."
- "The actual job is maintenance. Nobody notices it until you stop doing it."

**List lead-in and item 7 changed on 2026-08-30, Reut's call.** The flat "7 rules I run the
company on now" became **"6 simple rules that changed how I manage people"**, which promises a
transformation instead of announcing a list. To make the count honest, the listening item came
out of the numbered list and became the closing conclusion, which is where Reut wanted it
anyway. So the list is 6 and the payoff is the 7th thing, unnumbered. Nothing was lost.
"Completely" was dropped from her phrasing, the adverb was propping the verb up.

**Item 7 and the close changed on 2026-08-30, Reut's call.** The mirror item was Partaker's,
and Lior's real conclusion is listening, so #7 became that and the closing line now lands on
it. Also added to the opening block: the title is exciting for about a month and then stops
meaning anything. It gives the ego setup a time limit, which is what makes the janitor hook
read as experience instead of a pose.

**Items 5 and 6 rewritten on 2026-08-30, Reut's call.**
- The old #5, "your mood is a company announcement", was cut. Reut: boring. It was the last
  item still carrying Partaker's content instead of Lior's.
- The dyslexia item was reframed from "I write badly" into **expectation setting**, which is
  the actual practice. He puts the whole truth on the table in the first conversation, his own
  weak spots included, then gives the same honest read back to the other person, with respect.
  The dyslexia stays inside it as the concrete proof, and the no-yelling line rides along.
- **The fun rule** is his own principle and it is now #6. If it stops being fun for either
  side, it ends. Framed as an early-warning instrument so it does not read as a perk.
- Count stays at 7, one out and one in. Odd-number rule holds.

**Item 4 rewritten on 2026-08-30, Reut's correction.** The first version only had the trust
half and it was inaccurate. The real practice is a two-sided deal: total freedom from week one,
and a very fast separation when it is not working, on results, on personality or on vibe. It
comes from the bootstrap years when there was no money to carry a bad fit, and it stayed after
the Fiverr money removed the pressure. The tone has to hold both halves at once, generous and
decisive, so the firing half is framed as respect for the other person's time. Never write this
one as a flex about being ruthless.

**Item 6 replaced "clarity beats charisma" on 2026-08-30**, at Reut's direction. Partaker's #6
is "your weaknesses are public property, own them", and Lior has a real, specific, first-party
version of it: he is dyslexic, his written messages to employees land far harsher than he
means them, and he says so to every new hire on day one. It is the strongest item in the post.
The generic clarity line was the weakest and the most obviously borrowed, so it went.
Dyslexia is an approved angle when the post is genuinely about it. Reading claims stay banned.
| 8 | Closing reframe, the unlearning | "unlearning what got you there" | same idea, janitor payoff |
| 9 | Question CTA | "Which of these hit home for you?" | "Which of the 7 would be hardest for you?" |
| 10 | P.S. offer + repost line | Accelerator cohort | no offer, Lior has no funnel. Save-prompt instead |

**What we changed on purpose:** Partaker's list runs on the "X, not Y" template in almost every
item. That construction is the exact shape LinkedIn's AI-slop report flags, so every one of our
7 items is rewritten to carry the same meaning without the contrast frame.

## 2. The draft

--- POST STARTS ---
You're not the CEO.
You're the fucking janitor.

Nobody told me that at the start.
(Employee 30 did.)

The title felt like a finish line. It turned out to be the day my actual job started.

CEO is a title built to inflate the person holding it. Three letters that make a room
go quiet. It is exciting for about a month. After that it stops meaning anything to
anyone, including you.

The actual job is plumbing. You spend your day unclogging whatever your team is stuck
behind.

I built AutoDS to 200 people and learned most of this the expensive way.

6 rules that sound lazy and run a 200-person company:

1. Nobody clocks in.
I have never asked an employee what time they logged on. I ask what moved. Hours measure
presence, and presence is the cheapest thing a person can hand you.

2. The 70% rule.
If someone can do it 70% as well as you, it stops being your job. That missing 30% is the
price of scale, and most people close the gap inside a month.

3. You will have to give away the work you love.
Mine was product. Handing it over felt like losing an arm. Two quarters later it was better
than anything I would have shipped alone. That is how it goes almost every time.

4. Full trust on day one, and a fast goodbye when it does not work.
Nobody here gets a proving period. Real decisions from the first week. The other half of that
deal is that we separate quickly when the fit is wrong, and wrong covers results, personality
and the way someone lands in a room. We bootstrapped for years with nothing to waste, so we
learned to call it early, and we kept doing it after Fiverr when we could have afforded to
wait. Waiting is the cruel option. Both people are better off six months later.

5. Everything on the table in the first conversation.
Including the parts where I am weak. I am dyslexic, so what I write comes out short and blunt
and it can read like anger. I say that on day one, so nobody spends a year decoding my Slack
messages. I have never once raised my voice at someone who works for me. Then I do the same in
the other direction, early and with respect. Most managers save the honest version for a
review, which is where good people find out too late.

6. The fun rule.
If working together stops being fun for either of us, we stop working together. It sounds soft.
It is the earliest signal I have. Every bad hire and every bad partnership I have been through
announced itself as a heavy feeling months before it showed up in any number.

The hardest part of this job is unlearning what got you here.

You were the best person in the room. Now you get paid to build a room that runs without you
in it.

And most of that work is listening. Every real problem here reached me as something a person
was trying to say, weeks before it showed up as a number. Being in the room often enough to
hear it, and quiet enough that the sentence gets finished.

That is the janitor part. You clear the floor so everyone else can move.

Which of the 6 would be hardest for you?

Save this for the day you get your first direct report.

#leadership #founders
--- POST ENDS ---

~2,100 characters. Longer than our 750-1,200 rule, and that is deliberate: numbered
list posts scan instead of read, and the donor post is the proof. Do not apply the length
rule to this archetype.

## 3. Three hook options

Reut's hook is #1 and it stays verbatim unless she says otherwise.

1. You're not the CEO. You're the fucking janitor.
2. You're not the CEO. You're the janitor. (clean version, same beat)
3. 250 employees and I have never checked what time anyone starts.
   Use #3 if the profanity reads wrong on a founder profile that also pitches conferences.

Note on #1 and #2: "You're not X, you're Y" is technically the contrast frame the anti-slop
rule bans. It survives here because it is direct address to the reader with a shock noun,
not a definitional reframe of a concept, and because Reut chose it. Every other instance of
that frame was stripped from the body to keep the total count at one.

## 4. Visual

Partaker's post won on a photographed physical artifact: a real book page with real highlighter
on a real wooden table. Analog, first-party, un-fakeable. That is what earns the dwell time and
the save.

Our version: **a photo of Lior's phone**, screen on, held in his hand or lying on his desk,
showing the 7 rules as a note or as his lock screen. Shot on a phone, not designed. Slight
angle, real desk, visible reflection. It has to look photographed, never rendered.

Fallback if that photo does not exist: the same 7 rules handwritten on a page of his notebook,
photographed the same way.

ALT text:
"A phone lying on a desk showing a note titled 7 rules I run the company on, with the first
rule, nobody clocks in, visible at the top."

## 5. Pre-publish flags

- **No third-party stat in this version.** Nothing in the post now needs fact-checking except
  the team size. Every claim is first-party.
- **"200 employees"** is the approved public number, corrected by Reut 2026-08-30. Not "100 engineers".
- **The dyslexia line is the save trigger.** It is the only sentence in the post nobody else
  can write. If anything gets cut for length, it is not this. It also carries the reason the
  janitor hook is not just an insult: the job is clearing your own noise out of other people's
  way. Never pair it with a reading claim anywhere on any platform.
- **No yelling line:** stated as fact, in his voice, no flex. It reads as a standard he holds
  himself to, not a boast about being a nice boss.
- **First-person count** is high for a Lior post, around 12. That is inherent to the donor
  structure, which trades on lived authority. The claim lines are all second person, which keeps
  the reader inside the post. Accepted.
- **Follower KPI:** the save prompt and the question CTA are the two follow drivers here.
  There is no gate, no "comment X", no link. That is on purpose after the exit-deck A/B.
- Self-repost 6-8 hours after publishing.

## 6. Tom Orbach hook layer, added 2026-08-30

Reut's rule: Tom shrinks the effort and inflates the result. Small, almost embarrassing action,
oversized specific outcome, colon, deadpan. Full breakdown lives in memory,
`reference-tom-orbach-humor-playbook`.

Lior's version of the "small action" is an operator decision that sounds like negligence.
His version of the "big result" is a real number, never inflated adjectives on top of it.

### Tom-shaped list lead-in, APPLIED to the draft 2026-08-30
Was: "6 simple rules that changed how I manage people:"
Now: **"6 rules that sound lazy and run a 200-person company:"**
Same slot, same length, and the effort/result gap now does the work. "Sound lazy" is the
Tom self-deprecation move, sized for Lior. It also pre-empts the "must be nice" comment.

### Tom-shaped hook alternates, if the janitor line ever gets retired
1. I stopped checking what time anyone starts. We got to 200 people anyway.
2. I gave away the only part of the job I was actually good at:
3. I know it sounds lazy. Nobody at my company has ever clocked in.
4. The dumbest rule I run my company on is the one that fires people fastest:
5. One rule about fun decides who works here. 200 employees, no exceptions.

All five: small action first, real number second, no adjective doing the lifting.
The janitor hook stays the pick while Reut wants it. These are the bench.
