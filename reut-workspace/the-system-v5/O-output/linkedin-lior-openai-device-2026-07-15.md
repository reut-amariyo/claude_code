OpenAI's first device just leaked.

A movable speaker with no screen, built by the engineers who made the iPhone. It talks while it listens, moves around on its own so it feels alive, and reads your emails until it starts anticipating you. Around $300, launching in 2027.

You already know how this movie ends. Every smart speaker ever sold became a kitchen timer.

I know because years ago I filled my house with Alexa devices, convinced an assistant should simply always be there. Always around, always recognizing, always knowing. The intelligence wasn't ready. Every one of them stayed a box waiting for commands.

This time the bet goes the other way, and the reason is a small experiment. An agent tracks my mood these days, 1 to 10, through the day. A Tony Robbins method. The same numbers could sit in Excel. The difference: skip a check-in and it pings and asks why. That's how it caught that evenings between 8 and 10 are the weakest hours, exactly the time reserved for personal growth. Evenings now open with meditation and breathing.

That change never came from anything passive.

A box that waits for commands is a tool. The one that starts the conversation is a companion.
