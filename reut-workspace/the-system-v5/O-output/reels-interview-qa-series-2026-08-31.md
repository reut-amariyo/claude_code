# Reels: "Ask Lior" interview Q&A series — 7 scripts
Created 2026-08-31. Format: a woman asks one question on camera, Lior answers in one word, then unpacks it in 3 lines.
Goal: net new followers, not views. English. Drafts only — Reut publishes.

---

## Why this format converts to follows

- The credential lives in HER question, not in his answer. He never brags, she does it for him. Solves the "never arrogant" rule and still front-loads the hook.
- The one-word answer is the pattern interrupt. Question ends around 0:04, answer lands at 0:05, and the viewer stays for the "why".
- It is repeatable forever. Same set, same chair, same interviewer, one question per video. Repeatable format is what makes a stranger hit follow instead of just liking.
- Series name suggestion, pick one and lock it: **One Question** / **Ask the Founder** / **28 Seconds With Lior**.

## Production notes (same every episode)

- 9:16, one camera on Lior, interviewer stays off-frame or a shoulder in the corner. Her voice is enough.
- On-screen text at 0:00 = her question typed out, so it works on mute.
- The one-word answer appears as a full-screen word card at 0:05. Same font every episode. That card IS the branding.
- Hard cuts only on his lines. No music under the question.
- Length: 20-30s for scripts 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7. Script 4 runs 45s.
- End card: [ life is beta ]

## Two things to verify before shooting

1. **Age at exit.** The brief says 28. Our canon is: started AutoDS at 21, bootstrapped 8 years, then Fiverr. That reads as 29. Confirm with Lior, or cut the age and say "in my twenties".
2. **The $92M.** That figure is media-sourced. Fiverr's SEC filing states $55.658M plus up to $36M earn-out. He uses $92M publicly, so it is your call, but a reel is screenshottable. Two safe swaps that keep the hook: "You sold your company to a public company with zero investors" or "You bootstrapped to $20M ARR and sold it".

# SCRIPT 1 — Focus (v2, built from the LinkedIn "saying no" post)

**Main version, 30s**

--- SCRIPT STARTS ---

HER: You sold your company at 28. No investors. One tip for someone starting today?

LIOR: Focus.

LIOR: Saying yes is easy. We all want to be liked. No is the one that costs you something.

LIOR: I said yes for years. Another intro, another partnership, another 5 minutes. Then I noticed the product was getting my leftovers.

LIOR: Now every request goes through 3 questions. Can it wait until tomorrow. Does it move the thing I set out to build. And what am I saying no to by saying yes to this.

LIOR: Every yes costs a ship date somewhere.

LIOR: Say yes to everyone and the person you end up disappointing is yourself.

--- SCRIPT ENDS ---

On-screen word card at 0:05: FOCUS
On-screen list while he says the 3 questions:
1. Answer tomorrow, never in the room
2. Does it move what I set out to build
3. Name what this yes is costing me
Caption: Say yes to everyone and the person you end up disappointing is yourself.
CTA on screen: How do you decide if something is worth your time?

# SCRIPT 4 — The most expensive mistake (45s)

--- SCRIPT STARTS ---

HER: What's the most expensive mistake you ever made?

LIOR: One line of code.

LIOR: 3 in the morning. A customer messages me. Every product in her store is priced at one dollar.

LIOR: Orders are coming in. Hundreds of dollars of product, sold for a dollar. My bug, in code I wrote myself.

LIOR: On eBay you can't cancel dozens of orders without losing the account. So it was her business on the line, not mine.

LIOR: I stayed on the phone with her until morning. Then I went live on Facebook the same day and told every customer exactly what I broke.

LIOR: She's the one who caught it at 3AM. She ran our customer service for the next 6 years.

--- SCRIPT ENDS ---

On-screen word card: $1
Caption: The bug that almost ended us, and the customer who became employee number one in support.
CTA: Would you have gone live?
Note: keep "3AM" and "$1" verbatim, they are the anchors.
