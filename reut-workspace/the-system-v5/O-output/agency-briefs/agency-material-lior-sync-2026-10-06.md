### 1. How Lior decides to buy a company

AutoDS has bought 4 companies. Reut asked what he actually looks at, and he answered with a list, in this order:

1. People. A connection to people he trusts.
2. We can add value. Either we make it more profitable or we make the product better.
3. What it gives our ecosystem. A complementary product that fills a gap.
4. Payback within a year. His line: "If I can get the investment back within a year, I'm buying." After all the optimization, the deal has to pay itself back in 12 months. That's how every acquisition so far was done.
5. We understand the product. "We don't buy something we don't know." Usually it's something we planned to build anyway, so buying saves time and focus.

The seller's side, in his words: our deals run on targets. So the seller has to trust that together we'll hit them, and that they'll make more with us than running alone. And they understand we're coming into their space either way.

The regret: his biggest mistakes in M&A are the deals he didn't do. Same playbook, he knew exactly what to do, but they didn't take a loan, or didn't have the time to do it properly. He called it "a great story". We're getting the deal and the number from him, don't publish a number before we confirm it.

Angles: "Payback in 12 months or I don't buy" is the hook. The regret is a second post: the deal you skip never shows up as a loss.
