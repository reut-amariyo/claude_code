Hi guys,

### Story 1: The CEO who runs his own personal brand, and the AI brain behind it

Lior keeps getting asked the same question lately, by a lot of people, about personal branding. His own framing for the post: I run a company of 200 people, and I still reach [X] impressions and [X] followers a month on social.

His argument for why personal brand is worth a CEO's time right now: with all the AI around us, we are losing some of our humanity. The cheapest channel left is a personal brand. It is organic content, and people connect to people. In the end a personal brand is also a brand people connect to, and from there you can sell anything.

Then the part almost nobody shows, how it actually runs:

Every meeting he has gets recorded. Every thought he has gets recorded, and when one hits he sends a voice note to a WhatsApp group. Out of that they built a brain that now holds a full year of everything he thinks, everything he does, his whole background. It includes his coaching sessions and his sessions with a psychologist. His own words on why: so that Claude knows me as well as anything can. From that brain they produce the posts, the videos, and the writing off a brief.

Reut pushed back on this in the recording, and the answer is the best part of the story. She asked: you have resources, you have a team, how does someone earlier in their journey do this? His answer is that the resources are not the point, the AI is. The AI produces the content because of that huge brain that knows everything about him. The advice he wants to give people is one line: just record yourself, without stopping. Keep adding to it, and at some point the thing knows you better than you remember yourself.

He sees two separate posts here:
1. Why a CEO does this at all. A lot of people have asked him why he bothers, so that question is the hook.
2. How he got to [X] impressions and [X] followers without putting in more than [X] hours a week.

He also asked for a saveable asset to go with it, a file or an illustration of the loop: meeting, record, Claude, learn, meeting. Something people bookmark rather than just like.

Two things to hold on this one. The AI-brain detail is the whole differentiator, so do not dilute it into generic "use AI for content" advice. And the coaching and psychologist detail is his, he volunteered it, but treat it as a passing proof of how complete the brain is, never as the subject of the post.

### Story 2: Two or three big things a day

This is a working system he built for himself in the last stretch, and it is unusually concrete.

The trigger: he talked it through with his mentor and realised he was doing a huge amount and still not moving at the pace he wanted. Not a vague feeling, he was clear about that. A lot really was happening. But the big things, the ones that would actually move the business forward, kept getting pushed aside, precisely because they were big and needed more thinking.

What he built: an agent, his regular one, currently running on Claude, set up as an assistant. Every morning it sends him one simple question. What are the two or three things you commit to doing today? It follows up three times a day. He tells it what happened. On Friday he gives it a report of what he finished and what he did not, and it comes back around on the next two or three.

What changed: things started happening. His word for them was simple, the results were simple. The small work did not disappear, he is clear that he still puts in his twelve hours because he likes it and wants to keep moving. But what he measures is only the two or three big things.

The payoff he named, and this is the quotable part: at the end of the week and the end of the month he gets a report of the big things he completed, and he can see where he stands against the commitments he made to himself each day. How many got done, how many did not. His verdict on the alternative: far more measurable than a shopping list of small tasks that do not really help.

Then he reached for a gym analogy, unprompted, and it is the best line in either recording. In the gym there are compound exercises, not isolation ones. A deadlift or a bench press works around 70 percent of you, and in one exercise you get the effect you would get from three or four separate ones, because of how the exercise is built. Same with work. One big task can be worth many small ones, even when it takes less time. Reut said "big", he corrected her: big in substance.

Reut asked him in the recording whether he can give people the thing itself, the prompt or the assistant instructions, so they can run it too. He said yes. So a lead-magnet version of this post is available if you want it, and Reut can get the actual instructions from him.

One more angle from the same recording, Reut's observation rather than his, but he agreed with it and it fits his voice: you plant seeds and work on them for a long time, then you let go a little and things start landing, the way the LinkedIn post about him and the Twitter post that blew up both did. His answer was that it is the result of work that has been happening the whole time.

Let me know what you want to run first. If you need more depth on either one, I can get Lior to expand.

Reut
