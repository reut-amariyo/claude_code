# Management Rules Worth Breaking, Lior version (2026-09-17, v2)

Rewrite of the X agency's 6-rule card. Same card, same frame: the heading is the common rule, the text under it is what Lior does instead. v2 takes the five rules from the approved "You're not the CEO, you're the janitor" post, so every line is already in his voice.

## What was wrong with the agency card

1. Zero numbers and zero scenes. Six rules of pure opinion.
2. Cynical motive-attribution ("that rule is there to make firing easy"). Lior never positions himself above other managers.
3. "Don't answer. Let them get it wrong." He withholds tasks, never answers. Openness is his stated management identity.
4. Six rules. Odd wins.

## The card

--- POST STARTS ---

Management Rules Worth Breaking
FIVE RULES · @LIOR_POZIN

1. Track hours
I have never asked an employee what time they logged on. I ask what moved. Hours measure presence, nothing more.

2. Nobody does it as well as you
If someone can do it 70% as well as you, it stops being your job. Even if that means giving away the work you love.

3. Trust is earned
Nobody here gets a proving period. Real decisions from the first week. The other half of that deal: when the fit is wrong, we separate quickly.

4. Never show your team where you're weak
Everything goes on the table in the first conversation. I am dyslexic, so what I write comes out short and blunt, and it can read like anger. I say that on day one.

5. Keep it professional
If working together stops being fun for either of us, we stop working together. It is the earliest signal I have.

Every rule here was written for a team you have to watch. Your job is to clear the floor so everyone else can move.

--- POST ENDS ---

All five rules and their wording: the approved "You're not the CEO. You're the janitor." post (200-person company, 5 rules learned by getting them wrong). Headings are the common rules each one breaks. Do not add: equity or options, 250 employees, "100 engineers".
