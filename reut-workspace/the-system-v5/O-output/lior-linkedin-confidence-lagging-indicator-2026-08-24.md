## Beat map borrowed from the winner
1. Scene with a concrete anchor instead of a lesson
2. Triple of specific actions that used to terrify, now routine
3. Reversal pivot: the activity did not change, the reps did
4. Named mechanism: fear as a prediction running on evidence
5. Lowered-bar prescription: smallest repeatable version
6. Short exit quotable, separate from the card line
7. Self-disclosure prompt instead of a CTA ask

## Draft v3 - the emotional fix (Reut rejected v1 and v2, 2026-08-24)

Reut: "not good, does not create identification and emotion like the post I sent."
Diagnosis: v2 kept the skeleton but swapped universal fears for CEO fears
(acquisition meeting, firing someone, shipping a bug). Those earn admiration,
and admiration blocks identification. The original works because all three items
are small, universal, and slightly embarrassing, and because it is written in body
language, not operator language. v3 restores both and buys authenticity with one
unimpressive detail instead of a war story.

--- POST STARTS ---
There are things I do every week now that used to keep me up the night before.

Hitting publish on an opinion.
Messaging someone far out of my league.
Standing in front of a room full of strangers.

Now they are ordinary parts of my week.

Which is strange, because none of it actually got easier.

My body just stopped treating it as danger.

Before my first stage in Dubai I rewrote the opening line 40 times, then forgot it anyway. Nobody walked out. My brain quietly filed that away.

Fear is a prediction. Your brain sees something unfamiliar and guesses: this could hurt.

Repetition is how you argue with it. One rep at a time, you hand it evidence, until the alarm gets quieter.

So when something scares you, do not aim for confidence first.

Find the smallest version you can do again next week. Then do it again.

Give your brain something new to work with.

What is the thing you keep not doing?

## Draft v2 - near 1:1 transplant of the original (Reut's call, 2026-08-24)

Same skeleton, same line breaks, same connective tissue. Only the three actions,
the internal quote, and the proof are Lior's.

--- POST STARTS ---
There are things I do all the time now that used to wreck me for days.

Going live to explain a bug I shipped.
Saying a number out loud in an acquisition meeting.
Telling a good person the role no longer fits.

Now, they are pretty normal parts of my month.

Which is funny, because the actual situations have not changed.

My brain has.

It collected enough evidence over time to realize: "Oh, this is survivable."

That is one of the reasons this quote stuck with me.

Fear is often your brain making a prediction: this could be dangerous.

It is why repetition matters so much.

You are teaching your nervous system, one rep at a time, that the situation can be handled. And eventually the alarm stops going off quite so loudly.

So when something scares you, do not make "feeling confident" the first goal.

Instead, figure out the smallest version you can practice over and over.

Give your brain some new data.

## Draft v1 - looser adaptation

--- POST STARTS ---
At 3AM my own bug repriced every connected store to $1.

Same day, live on Facebook, in front of every customer we had: this is what I broke, this is how we fix it. Hands shaking the whole time.

Now it is a Tuesday.

Same with saying a number out loud in an acquisition meeting.
Same with telling a good person the role no longer fits.

None of it got easier. The reps piled up and my body followed.

Fear is your brain making a prediction. It runs on the evidence you fed it, and every rep is new data.

So when something scares you, stop waiting to feel ready. Find the smallest version you can repeat this week, then repeat it until your body stops filing it as an emergency.

Confidence is a lagging indicator.

What is the rep you keep postponing?
