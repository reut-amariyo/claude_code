# The exit-deck post is a format, not a hit. Turning it into the follower engine.

Written 2026-08-29 after Reut confirmed the exit-deck post is the best follower post
Lior has ever had on LinkedIn.

**Final numbers:** 66,682 impressions, 317 reactions, 60 comments, 1 repost.
Published 2026-08-25. Grew from 23,111 at first read, so it kept distributing for four days.
Reut's read: the follower volume is the thing we have never seen before.

---

## 1. Why it converted, separated from why it reached

Two different mechanisms fired here and they must not be confused. Reach came from one set of
choices, follows came from another. Only the second set is the thing to repeat.

### What bought the reach
- **Native document carousel.** LinkedIn's highest-dwell format. A reader who swipes 8 pages
  hands the algorithm a much stronger signal than a reader who scrolls past an image.
- **No gate.** The A/B two days earlier is the cleanest experiment in the log. Same copy,
  comment-gate CTA, 55 impressions. Ungated, 66,682. The gate is not a small tax on this
  account, it is a kill switch.
- **A number and a named company in the first 8 words.** $92M and Fiverr.
- **Four-day tail.** Saves and downloads keep re-surfacing a post. Narrative posts die in 24h.

### What bought the follows
This is the part worth copying.

1. **It is not a post about an asset. It is the asset.** The reader ends the interaction owning
   something. Everything else in the feed asks them to agree, react, or feel something.
2. **Nobody else can give this away.** A prompt pack is a commodity, twenty accounts posted one
   this week. An actual deck that moved an actual company to an actual buyer exists once. The
   scarcity is the credential.
3. **A follow is a bet on the next one.** A stranger who downloads a 47-slide deck for free is
   not thanking Lior, they are calculating: this person gives away real internal documents, so
   there will be more. That calculation is exactly the subscription decision the mega-accounts
   run on. It is the only reliable follower lever we have found.
4. **The body earned the download before it asked for it.** One counterintuitive tactic, not a
   list of ten: pitch buyers you would never sell to, and watch which slide loses the room.
   That is insider information, unavailable anywhere else, and it proves the deck is worth having.
5. **Generosity with no ask attached.** No "comment DECK". No "follow for more". ~80 connection
   requests arrived unasked in the first read. A LinkedIn connection auto-follows.
6. **The parenthetical counter-hook.** "(Now I'm open-sourcing it.)" Line 1 says he sold a
   company. Line 2 immediately gives it away. That turn is the whole emotional payload.

### The one-line version
The post works because a stranger can answer "why would I press Follow?" without thinking.
No other Lior format has passed that test.

## 2. The template, beat by beat

Copy this shape exactly. Seven beats, ~950 characters.

1. **Hook.** Outcome with a hard number + the artifact, in one line. "Sold my business to
   Fiverr for $92M with this 47-slide deck."
2. **Counter-hook in parentheses, under 6 words.** The giveaway turn. "(Now I'm open-sourcing it.)"
3. **Why this document exists.** One sentence of standing. "Founders keep reaching out to
   consult on their sale processes."
4. **One tactic, never a list of ten.** The most counterintuitive thing Lior knows about this
   document. Three short imperative lines maximum.
5. **The proof line.** Why the artifact survived something real.
6. **The handoff.** "Copy the structure. Fill in your numbers."
7. **The link, plainly, with no ask.** No comment gate, no follow ask, no like ask.

**Visual:** native document carousel, portrait 1080x1350, one idea per page, from the artifact
itself. `build_carousel_sections.py` in `O-output/exit-deck-lead-magnet/` renders it from any PDF.

**Non-negotiables:** ungated. Free. First-party. A document Lior actually used, never a
document written to be a lead magnet. The moment we manufacture the artifact for the post, the
scarcity evaporates and it becomes a prompt pack.

## 3. The strategic move: this is the signature series

The follower KPI work in August concluded that the follower engine is a signature series on a
fixed rhythm, and that personal posts are the affinity layer around it. We now have a series
format with a proven 66K, high-follow result instead of a hypothesis.

Recommended shape:
- **One artifact giveaway every two weeks.** Preparing a real document takes real time. Weekly
  will force manufactured artifacts, which kills the format.
- **Same closing line every time**, so the series is recognisable: free, no comment needed, link below.
- **Headline change on the profile.** Every mega-account states the promise in the headline,
  because the follow decision happens on the profile, not the post. Current headline sells the
  title. Proposed: CEO & Co-founder AutoDS | Sold to Fiverr | I give away the real documents
  behind it. Needs Lior's approval.
- **Measurement.** Follower delta at 48h per post, and monthly profile visits, which have still
  never been pulled and are blocking the whole conversion model.

## 5. What kills this format

- **Any gate.** Comment-to-receive, follow-to-receive, connect-to-receive. Proven lethal on this
  account at 55 impressions. Do not retest, and do not let anyone talk us into "just this once".
- **A manufactured artifact.** A document written for the post is a lead magnet. The whole value
  is that these are internal documents that already did a job.
- **A list of ten tips in the body.** The winner gave one tactic. A list reads as content, one
  tactic reads as access.
- **A single image instead of a document carousel.** The carousel is doing real distribution work.
- **Asking anyone to comment via a direct link.** Off-feed engagement patterns as a pod and
  suppressed the A version.
- **$92M in fact-checkable contexts.** The Fiverr 20-F says $55.658M plus up to $36M earn-out.
  It is fine in a LinkedIn post as the media-reported figure and it is already public at 66K
  impressions, but keep it out of anything a journalist or a regulator reads.
