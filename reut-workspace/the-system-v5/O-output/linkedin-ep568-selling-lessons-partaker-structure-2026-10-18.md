I almost sold my company for $38M.
(I didn't come from money.)

5 months of due diligence with a PE fund. One day before signing, I walked.
Two years later we sold to Fiverr for more than double.

Nobody trains founders for this.
You sell once. The buyer's team does it every week.

7 things I wish someone told me before selling:

1. Get your house in order before anyone calls.
We were bootstrapped and messy. Cleaning up ate most of a 5-month process, weeks that should have gone to growth.

2. Listen to the questions in due diligence.
Every PE question was about who to cut and which service to trim. Their plan was in the questions from month one. It took me until month five to hear it.

3. Ask to be paid more for beating their targets.
Their targets were aggressive, so we asked for upside if we beat them. They said no. A buyer who won't pay for growth isn't planning any.

4. Never let the banker run it without you.
The first bank said they'd handle everything. 24 meetings in Boston, then on bid day the whole 4-person team moved to another bank. The momentum left with them. People buy founders.

5. See every offer before you decide to sell.
The second process meant 50+ presentations. Offers for 1%, for 51%, some higher than Fiverr's. Seeing all of them is what told me I wanted to sell at all.

6. Pick the buyer, then the price.
Fiverr's questions were about growth. The deal came together over whiskey at their office at 9pm. Trust closed it.

7. A good earn-out sets you free.
Ours paid on revenue and EBITDA every 6 months, and we hit the targets fast. Once it stopped being only my money, every decision went to growth and the team.

Run the company like you'll own it forever.
That's the version buyers compete for.

P.S. I went through all 7 with John Warrillow on Built to Sell Radio, link in the comments. Which one would have saved you the most?▮

## FINAL v2 — Lior's direction (2026-10-19): built as a profitable long-term business, never for a buyer

--- POST STARTS ---
Turned down $38M. Then sold to Fiverr for $92M.

(I didn't come from money.)

Since the exit, founders keep reaching out to consult on their sale processes.
One piece of advice helped us more than anything else.

So if you want to sell yours one day, start here:
Build it as if you'll never sell it.

Sounds backwards, I know.

- Make it profitable from day one.
- Make every decision for the next 10 years.
- Keep investing in growth, even when buyers call.

Our first buyer wanted to cut costs and milk it.
So we walked away one day before signing.

One of the hardest calls I've made.
But it was worth it.

Two years later, Fiverr saw a profitable company built for the long run.
They weren't buying it to milk it.

The right buyer will see what you built.
And pay for it.
