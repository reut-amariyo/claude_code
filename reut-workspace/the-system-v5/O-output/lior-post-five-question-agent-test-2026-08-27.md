Gartner says 40% of AI agent projects die by 2027.

The models are fine. The word is doing the lying.

Most of what lands on my desk as an "agent" answers a question and waits.

Hands, sometimes.
A trigger, sometimes.
Never a loop.

Gartner has a name for that gap.

Agent washing.

Tom Even built a five-question test for it. We run it before anything gets a seat inside a 250-person company.

1. Does it loop? When a step ends, does the system read what came back and choose the next move without me?

2. Does it have hands? Files, inbox, browser, code. Text in and text out is thinking. Hands are acting.

3. Does it have a trigger? Can it start at 07:00 tomorrow with nobody in the room?

4. Does it have memory? Does anything it learned today survive into next week?

5. Does it have a mandate? Does it know in writing what it may do alone and what it may never touch without a human?

The first two make it an agent.
The next two make it autonomous.
The fifth is the only reason it gets near a customer order.

Most of what I get pitched scores two out of five.

Two out of five is a workflow with a good name on it.

Run it on the last thing someone sold you. What did it score?
