## What we took from the seven steps

Step 7 only: screenshot-based verification across the full scroll.

Why that one and not the other six. Brand guidelines, pain/person/promise, scroll feedback,
inspiration sites, and component reuse are all design-craft advice that any designer could
give in 2024. None of them are new and none of them are his lane. Step 7 is the only one
that is actually unlocked by this release, because vision inside the verification loop is
one of the five new abilities in 5.1. New + usable in one sentence + generalises past
websites. That is the whole brief.

Dropped on purpose: the $12.83 vs $21.64 cost comparison, which is the banned cost framing,
and the YouTube-link-in-comments CTA, which is creator behaviour.

Pillar: focus. Archetype: 9 with a day-1 news anchor.
Hook rule: Tom Orbach, shrink the action and inflate the result.

--- POST STARTS ---

Fable 5.1 fixed the reason AI websites look cheap.
It can see the page.

Every AI-built site carries the same tell. Layers stacked wrong. An element half outside the frame. An animation that behaves on desktop and collapses at 390px.

That happens because the model wrote the page and never looked at it. What it shipped was a description of a website.

Fable 5.1 can screenshot its own work, inspect the image, and fix what it finds before it reports back.

The whole upgrade is one instruction at the end of your prompt:

"Screenshot the full scroll at desktop and at 390px wide. Look at every frame. Fix anything broken, cut off, or overlapping. Then show me the screenshots."

Same model. Same prompt you already use. One extra sentence.

A page that reads well versus a page that survives being looked at. Your visitors only ever meet the second one.

And this travels well past websites. Anything that can inspect its own output should be told to inspect it before it says done. Decks, dashboards, data pulls, code.

The cheapest quality control available to you is asking for the evidence.

Where would you point this first?
