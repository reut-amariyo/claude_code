## Long biography (reference, over the limit)
Lior Pozin is the co-founder and CEO of AutoDS, an e-commerce automation platform used by more than 1.8 million online sellers. He built it from his parents' apartment in Beer Sheva, Israel, with no outside investors, grew it to 200 employees, and sold it to Fiverr, a NYSE-listed company, at age 28, in a deal US media reported at $92 million. He still runs AutoDS as its CEO.

His career is a sequence of no's turned into products. At 10, his father locked the family computer as punishment and Lior hacked back in. At 14, he found a phone on a Chinese site that sold for $30 more on eBay and opened his first online store. At 21, eBay banned the account he had built for 7 years. Instead of appealing, he opened 100 new accounts and wrote software to run them all, because doing it by hand was impossible. Other sellers asked to use the software. That software became AutoDS. When a year-end VAT bill he did not know existed nearly closed the company, he took a $30,000 loan and turned what he knew into a mentorship program for 150 sellers, which funded the company's next stage. Years later, a private equity fund offered to buy the company and he walked away the day before signing, because their plan was to cut the team.

Lior was named to Forbes Israel's 30 Under 30 in 2025. AutoDS was Shopify's number one partner in both 2024 and 2025. He has spoken on stage at Affiliate World Dubai and Affiliate Summit East in New York, hosts AutoDS's own conferences, and personally recorded 100 free educational videos, one a day for 100 days. He is a member of the Forbes Business Council. The son of Ukrainian immigrants, a grocery store worker and an air-conditioning technician, he speaks English, Hebrew and Russian and lives in Tel Aviv.

## Unique idea (v2, 2026-09-20, game frame per Reut)
Lior treats business and life as a game with no finish line, only the next challenge. In that game, a no is the most useful move the other side can make, because it tells you exactly where the next level starts. He has been collecting no's since he was 10: a father who locked the family computer, an eBay ban at 21 that erased 7 years of work, a tax bill he could not pay. Each one became the next level: hacking back in, writing the software that grew into AutoDS, building a mentorship program that saved the company. He puts his rule simply: our lives are made of many no's, and it is our choice whether to take the no or turn it into a yes.

The talk shows the 3 moves he uses to turn one. Read the no literally, without the story you attach to it. Solve it for yourself first. Then look for who else got the same no, because a no from a system, a platform, a bank, a school, is never sent to one person only, and the players who quit at that point leave the level empty for the ones who stay. What is new is the frame: rejection as game design instead of a verdict. The no is where the game gets interesting, and Lior has 1.8 million sellers and a sale to a public company, with no investors, as proof that the game is worth playing past it.

## Key takeaway (one sentence, pick one)
1. Life is a game with no finish line, and every no is where the next level starts. (recommended)
2. A no is the game telling you where to play next, and it is your choice whether to take it or turn it into a yes.
3. Every no you accept ends the game, and every no you refuse opens the next level.
