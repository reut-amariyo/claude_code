## Part 1 — The News

**Article:** TechCrunch, July 14, 2026 — "OpenAI's first hardware device is reportedly a screenless speaker that can move"
https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/14/openais-first-hardware-device-is-reportedly-a-screenless-speaker-that-can-move/
(Original reporting: Bloomberg, July 14, 2026)

Key facts from the reporting:
- OpenAI's first consumer device is a mobile, screen-free smart speaker, positioned as a new type of home computer for the AI era and a humanlike AI companion that lives in the home.
- It controls smart-home appliances, plays media, answers questions, responds to messages, and taps ChatGPT's capabilities.
- It has mechanical elements that move on their own, designed to feel alive rather than like an object waiting for commands.
- It becomes increasingly personalized and proactive over time, drawing on personal information such as emails, with the goal of anticipating needs and surfacing information before being asked.
- Voice runs on GPT-Live, a model that listens and talks at the same time.
- Developed with many former Apple engineers who worked on the iPhone and Mac, inside OpenAI's hardware bet built around Jony Ive, whose startup io was folded into OpenAI.
- Expected price: $200 to $300. Reveal planned this year, launch in 2027.

## Part 2 — Lior's Voice Notes (translated from Hebrew)

### Recording 1 — His Alexa story (why he believes in ambient assistants)

"At home I had an Alexa integration, so it would always be around me in the house, listening, because I knew this was supposed to become an integral part of our lives. The goal was that it would be an integral part. Not that I walk over to it and press it, but that it's simply always there. Always in the environment, always recognizing, always knowing. Like a second brain. Not a tool, really with us, all the time. And the shift is that it becomes active instead of passive, instead of us turning to it."

### Recording 2 — His daily proof: a proactive mood-tracking agent (conversation)

Lior: "I do a check-in with my mood, and whether I ate, whether I kept the routine. I send it my mood score, 1 to 10, and it tracks me through the day. It's a Tony Robbins method. It tracks my mood, but across the whole day. Then you can deal with it, once we know it, once we monitor it."

Q: "Say you send it a 2?"

Lior: "It monitors it. It knows the 2, by hours, like data. In principle I could track all of this in Excel. But here it's active. Even if I didn't answer it, it pings me, it asks me."

Q: "And it doesn't just ask what happened?"

Lior: "It asks me why. Explain. And I explain."

Q: "So right now you're collecting the data?"

Lior: "Yes. But for example, I saw that in the evenings I'm always weaker, and evening is personal development time for me, time that really matters to me. I look at what I do in the evening, between 8 and 10."

Q: "And what do you want to do with it?"

Lior: "I do meditation in the evening when I get home. Breathing, sleep, sport."
