## Stories and lines, in Lior's words

1. **The first thing he bought after the exit was McDonald's.** It's a deadpan joke and a perfect hook.
2. **The morning after the exit.** "I woke up in the morning and I started to think, okay, what's next?" Millionaire became billionaire, and then "next what?" He took it to his therapist and realized "it was always not about the money but about the challenge of getting the money."
3. **"Please fix me."** He told his therapist he keeps waking up at night with ideas. The therapist said: "I don't need to fix you... That's your game. Some play tennis, some play football, and some just play business."
4. **The New York rooftop.** He looked at the skyline with partners and said: "What's driving me crazy is that still not all the buildings are mine."
5. **"They used to call me a robot."** He was a developer who didn't show feelings. Therapy helped him "be more open and achieve more."
6. **Wealth isn't money.** Wealth conferences taught him that wealth means health, friendships and relationships. Money is one part. Now he uses money for new hobbies and more time with his wife.
7. **Do it again: jump more.** "Just jump and start and not think about what some other random people will tell me." If it works, great. If not, pivot.
8. **Zero to $1M = problem → market → solution.** Find a problem you have yourself. Tell as many people as you can about it. Check whether others feel it too. Study competitors with SimilarWeb and their reviews, fix what they get wrong, copy what they get right. "Only then you start thinking about the solution." Today, build the MVP on Lovable without investors, sell to the first believers, run the feedback loop, then spend on marketing.
9. **The 70% rule + ROI hiring.** "If someone can do that at least 70% of how I would do that, I would just delegate it to them." Because they were bootstrapped, the first hire was whatever paid back fastest. A feature worth $10K/mo plus a developer at $5K/mo = "200% ROI, why wouldn't I hire?" *(Already in Lior OS. The $10K/$5K math is new.)*
10. **He automated his OKR dashboard, then turned it off.** "If I don't go through the process and check manually what my managers have done... I don't really know what's going on in the business." It's the strongest AI-era contrarian story in the episode: the best founders automate less in the places where judgment lives.
11. **"We didn't even have a strategy."** He told his organizational advisor, "We just did what they do but better," and put everything in one place. Her answer: "That sounds to me like a strategy. You created an all-in-one platform."
12. **The biggest no was an internal no.** The market leader was run by two older brothers with business-school degrees, money and connections. "How can I even go to this market?"
13. **Partner pitch = product first.** "Look at this competitor. You work with him. We are much better. Look why." Relationships come second, and they're real: clubs, trips, he knows their kids. "Some of them are better friends of my competitors, but they know we are just a better product." A bad product burns influencers after 2-3 campaigns.
14. **No salary for 5 years.** "It took me 5 years to start taking money back home... the business could even crash and I would stay with nothing. Why not take 5%, 10%?" It's a work-life-balance mistake he names openly. He traveled to 30+ countries during the build.
15. **AI = a team of copywriters per niche** with zero spend, plus marketing that learns from competitor campaigns and cuts A/B-test costs. If he started AutoDS today, the team would be smaller from day one because he'd optimize for efficiency. Today he optimizes for multiplying the people he already has.
16. **Quick hits:** cut from 6 espressos a day to 3 in three weeks. His most impactful book is *The Power of Habit*: "Habits is everything. That's what builds or breaks the business." His billboard message: **"Just jump to the water."**
