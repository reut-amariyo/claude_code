# Lior Pozin on the "99 Is Not 100" podcast — English translation

**For the X and Instagram agencies.** Full English translation of a ~38 minute Hebrew interview. Lior speaks in Hebrew, so every line below is a close translation, not verbatim English. Quote the ideas and numbers freely; do not present translated lines as his exact wording.

## Story bank from this episode (with his numbers)

1. **First shekel at 12.** Fixed neighbours' computers for about 100 shekels, and rolled from there.
2. **First eBay product at 14: a Chinese phone, $30 profit.** Dropshipped from 14 to 18, later also his own store. Kept electronic-cigarette stock at his parents' home and sold it all day; the parents "at some point simply gave up".
3. **Dad's password.** Dad bribed him with a computer of his own for one more year of Russian evening school. When Dad wanted to punish him he put a password on the computer. Lior learned to hack it. Dad started taking the power cable. Lior kept a spare cable. "That was part of my game." This is how he entered hacking, information security and software.
4. **"If he can, so can I."** Third place in a judo competition and all the focus was on the kid in first. Basketball, same. It became the engine for life: when AutoDS started, the competitors were older, had money and connections, and "if they can, so can I".
5. **The contrarian kid.** Told to sit somewhere, he sat elsewhere. Religious school in Beer Sheva, so a kippah was required, so anti. Told to learn music, no. "Don't have it? I will have it. He's better? No, I'm better."
6. **Mom still asks.** With thousands of paying customers she still asked whether he could go be a programmer at another company. Last Saturday she asked whether his co-founder, the company's CTO, "can still find a job as a programmer".
7. **Expelled in 10th grade** from one high school, somehow managed at the second. Built a school forum everyone talked on, built websites freelance, all before the army.
8. **The army: wrong team.** Landed in an IT team, computer technicians, not programming. Told them he would do nothing until he was moved to the team he wanted, opened the books, taught himself the course content and demanded to be tested. "Nobody came to me and said prove yourself. I said it." Later became a team leader.
9. **Why not cyber.** Everyone in his unit went to cyber. He went where his passion was: a system to run 100 eBay stores for himself. Other sellers asked how they could use it. That is how AutoDS was founded.
10. **Bootstrapped by accident.** He did not know raising money was an option. First developer hired in Armenia because he was cheap; then a high-schooler who worked 24/7. Investors came years later; "not my style, I didn't want to waste time on decks."
11. **The $1 bug, full version.** Testing on his own, he set one product to $1. The software repriced every product in the system to $1: tens of thousands of items averaging $40-50. Hundreds sold within a few hours. A customer messaged him at 3 AM, "a normal working hour for me": she woke up to a flood of sale notifications. He said "congratulations, what do you want from me?" She said the products sold for $1, 30-something sales. He sat with her, helped her message her buyers so her account would not get blocked, and admitted the mistake instead of blaming eBay. She was an Israeli who had moved to Finland and could not find work there; he had "ruined her business", so he offered her a job with no money to pay her, starting at $200 a month, then $400, rising with the company. Over time she became Head of Customer Support with about 100 people under her. The next day he went live on Facebook to apologise and explain the fix. Nobody left. A competitor offered 20% to anyone who brought AutoDS customers over. Nobody moved.
12. **Radical openness as a management system.** "I'm the most open person in the world, for better and worse." Expectations set in the first interview: "This is how I work, sorry. You can take it or not, but I'll always be the most honest there is." The strategy: "I give you the space. Run, fall, get up, fail, I'm for it. But I always tell the truth."
13. **10 hours a week on personal development.** Psychologists, coaches, workshops, before and after the exit. "The company advances as far as the CEO advances as a person." To a friend hesitating over a CEO role: "Nobody is born a CEO."
14. **The book he did not read.** Asked to bring a book, he explained he listens because he cannot sit and read; his coach tried "five pages, five minutes a day" and it did not take. The book: The Science of Scaling. His two takeaways: set an unreasonable size goal, because the steps scale with the goal, and set an unreasonable deadline, because a month or a day produce the same slide deck.
15. **Billionaire by 45.** Stated as his dream in the first minute. Reason one: impact. Reason two: the person he has to become to get there. "Changing a million lives may not be enough to be a billionaire. I need to change 100 million lives."
16. **Mindset yes, skillset no.** The day before recording he told his coach: "I'm already there in mindset. In skillset, not yet." He took on too many projects to reach the goal and found himself drowning in overload. His fix is to jump in and then learn to organise it.
17. **Advisors from day one.** A high share of expenses was always advisors. They rotate because you outgrow them. Domain-specific: not "marketing" but "paid ads in one channel", then the best advisor he can find and afford. Many are just coffee: "Especially in Israel, people are happy to help. It's very rare that someone says no to a coffee."
18. **"I believe in no."** "Our lives are made of many no's. It's our choice whether I take that no, or make it a yes."
19. **On bootstrapping in the AI era.** Less money needed to build, more creativity needed to win. Deep tech, medical and hardware need capital; SaaS does not. Solve your own pain, or the pain of someone you can talk to every day.
20. **Three languages, one accent.** Russian at home first, Hebrew at school, and he is now fighting his accent in English because English comes out as a mix of all three. Recently gave a talk to new immigrants translating himself into three languages.
21. **Freedom is the value.** He needed money for a skateboard and for trips, and the home could not give it. Money was the route to freedom, not the goal.
22. **Future kids.** He will not push them anywhere specific, "obviously entrepreneurship" as a joke, but whatever they choose, be the best at it. "If you do something, do it to the maximum."

**Lior:** Yes. And one of the things was that I'm overloaded, OK? I do everything: I write code, I work with the accountant, legal, marketing, everything. **Lior:** Hundreds of products were bought. It was relatively early days, but it happened really, really fast. Within a few hours hundreds of products were sold. **Host:** There was a heart-stopping moment there.

**Lior:** And since then I've also learned this thing of openness, towards employees too. I'm the most open person in the world about everything, for better and worse.

**Host:** Where is it worse?

**Lior:** For better and worse towards the employee. Good things they do, and less good things. Where does it hurt? It can hurt people who don't take it in the best way.
