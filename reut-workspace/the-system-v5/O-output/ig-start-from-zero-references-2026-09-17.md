## Your reference, decoded

1. Disclaimer-as-credential: "I speak from experience, I entered business at 15"
2. Refusal: "I would NOT go be an employee" + why (the ceiling is the floor)
3. Proof line with numbers: 12 businesses, 3 worked, 1 exit
4. Reframe of what the first business is for: not profit, sales + marketing reps
5. Elimination: not stock market, not a physical product, not tourism
6. The pick: a service business, "I am the product", 9 concrete options
7. The unfair advantage of the reader: at 16 you can fail 9 times for free
8. Close: business #13 is better because of 12 mistakes, not better thinking

That is the skeleton to reuse. Content must be Lior's own.

## What the winners share (for the Lior brief)

1. Credential in the first 5 seconds, stated as fact, then dropped. Sam: "I literally already did this." Codie: "we pay a college kid $3-5K." Lior: sold to Fiverr, reported $92M, then never mentioned again.
2. ONE move, not a list. Turner (1 move, 29K) and Codie 2023 (1 move, 3.6K on a 2023 account) beat Simon Beard's 13 rules (322) with a bigger exit behind them.
3. A number pair that makes the move concrete. $7.95 vs $3-5K. $10K down vs $1M property. Lior has these: at 21 on eBay, 200 employees, 10 hrs/week.
4. A refusal beat early: "I would NOT go be an employee" / "no rented Lambos" / "everyone skips to the selling part". Contrarian stance, matches the Operator archetype.
5. Close that flips the reader's excuse: "you can't go back and start earlier" / "broke doesn't mean stuck" / "the 13th business is better because of 12 mistakes".
6. Length: 35-50 sec for the one-idea versions, 2 min for Sam's full playbook. Lior's talking-head cap is 40 sec, so it is the one-idea version.
