## Draft

--- POST STARTS ---

Nine GPT-6 Astra prompts landed in my team's Slack.

Five of them are job descriptions.

Renegotiate our vendor bills and cancel what nobody uses.
Run signup and checkout on a real phone every night.
Sit inside the competitors' products and report what changed.
Audit the company for work we should stop hiring for.
Open the browser and finish the workflow end to end.

Read them again. That is a hiring plan.

Running 200 people teaches you where it breaks.

The work is the easy part. An agent does 80% of all five today.

The other 20% is who answers when it goes wrong.

A vendor call that goes badly costs a relationship. A checkout bug that ships costs a day of revenue. Someone owns that number at the end of the month, and software cannot be that someone.

So the rule before we hand anything over:

If a wrong answer costs less than the time a person needs to check it, the agent takes the job.

If it costs more, the person keeps the job and gets the agent as leverage.

One line, and it decides more of our roadmap than any model release.

The demos are real. Watching them, everyone asks what the model can do.

Ask the other one. Which role are you still paying a person to do slowly?

#aiagents #ecommerceautomation

--- POST ENDS ---

## One risk worth naming

"An agent does 80% of all five today" is a claim, not a measurement. If Lior has not actually
run any of these five against AutoDS, soften to "an agent gets most of the way through all five"
before publishing. A number he cannot defend in the comments is the only weak point in the draft.
