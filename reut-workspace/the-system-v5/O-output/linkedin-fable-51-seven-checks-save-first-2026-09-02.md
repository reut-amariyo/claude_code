## The angle, and why "למה הוא הכי טוב" was dropped

The video's own honest finding is that Fable 5 and 5.1 produced near-identical sites from
the same prompts, assets and brand guidelines. The only consistent difference was cost.
"Why it is the best" therefore has exactly one honest answer, and that answer is the banned
cost framing. So the post takes the stronger and truer angle: the model was not the lever,
these seven things are. That reversal is what makes it worth saving.

Save-first format used: decision checklist. Seven items, odd number, each useful alone.
Line craft: one idea per line, blank line between nearly every line, per the save-first spec.
Length runs to the 1,500 ceiling, which the spec allows for save-first posts.

Dropped: every dollar figure, the weekly-limit talk, the free-community CTA, the skill plug.

--- POST STARTS ---

Ran the same brief through Fable 5 and 5.1.
The sites came out nearly identical.

Same prompts. Same brand guidelines. Same assets. Two models a generation apart, and choosing between them was a coin flip.

Which means the model was never the lever.

Seven things decide whether an AI-built site looks premium or looks generated:

1. Brand guidelines go in first. Colors, type, logo. Consistency is most of what reads as expensive.

2. Name the pain, the person and the promise before any design starts. A page that does not know who it is for looks generic every time.

3. Give the visitor feedback as they scroll. Text fading in, elements moving at different speeds. Subtle beats flashy.

4. Bring specific inspiration. Name the site and the exact element you want. "Make it look premium" is the instruction that produces slop.

5. Ask for layering. Three or four planes moving at different speeds is the strongest premium tell there is.

6. Reuse components instead of having the model invent every button. 21st.dev, Godly, Awwwards and Motion Sites are where they live.

7. Demand screenshot verification across the full scroll, on desktop and on a phone. Without it the agent has no idea what broke.

Number seven is the one almost everyone skips, and it is the one that catches the broken layer sitting outside the frame.

All seven come down to the same thing. How specific you are willing to be before you press enter.

Keep this for your next build.

Which of the seven does your team skip?

## The one caveat

Web design sits at the edge of the CEO relevance filter. Items 1, 2 and 7 are operator
judgment and clear it easily. Items 3, 5 and 6 are craft advice a designer could give,
and a CEO of a 200-person commerce company giving scroll-animation tips is the drift risk.

Two ways to close it if Reut wants a 🟢:
- Add one line under the list tying it to his seat, for example that every store page his
  market builds now starts from a model, so the gap between premium and generated is a
  business problem before it is a design problem.
- Or cut items 3 and 5, run it as a five-item checklist, and let the post sit fully in
  brief-quality territory rather than design territory.

My recommendation is the first. It keeps the save value intact and buys back the altitude
in one sentence.
