## A — Judo (hook verbatim from the Branson chalkboard post)

--- POST STARTS ---
You've done this your whole life.

Measured yourself against the kid in first place.

Third place in judo. The ride home was about first.
Basketball. Same ride, different kid.

In a Soviet home there is one sentence: look how good HE is.

It could park a kid for life. It did the opposite.

If he can, so can I.

At AutoDS the competitors were older, funded and connected.
If they can, so can I.
Bootstrapped past them. Acquired 4 of them.

The sentence never retired.

Thousands of paying customers, and my mother still asked if I could get a programmer job somewhere.
Last Saturday she asked about my co-founder, the CTO.

Mom, he's not supposed to find a job as a programmer.

Who was the kid in first place for you?

## B — The $1 bug as a bootstrapped confession list (hook verbatim from the bootstrapped-founder post)

--- POST STARTS ---
Being a bootstrapped startup Founder sucks. Here's what nobody will tell you, from my own experience:

- The first developer was hired in Armenia because he was cheap. The second was a high-school kid working around the clock.

- One founder did the code, the support, the marketing and the accountant. So one founder ran a test on a single product, priced it at $1, and the software priced every product in the system at $1. Tens of thousands of listings. Hundreds sold in hours.

- The customer who caught it messaged at 3 AM. The reply was "congratulations". She was selling on eBay from Finland to get by, and the bug had wrecked her business.

- The fix was a Facebook live the next day, to every customer, with the bug and the plan. No blaming eBay.

- The head of customer support was hired that week at $200 a month, a salary nobody should offer today, because that was the budget. She grew to run 100 people.

- A competitor then offered 20% to anyone who moved AutoDS customers over. Nobody moved.

- Investors showed up years later. Passing on them meant no decks and no meetings, and also no cushion the night everything went to $1.

Overall, I highly recommend it.

#Bootstrapping #SaaS

## C — Dad's password (hook verbatim from Nir Eyal)

--- POST STARTS ---
"You just got lucky."

Things people say about a founder who sold his company:
"He was born a coder."
"He had a computer early."
"The exit fell in his lap."

Almost none of it holds up.

The whole thing started with a punishment.

My dad locked the family computer with a password.
I broke the password.
He took the power cable to work with him.
I had a second cable.

That was the first computer-science education. Beer Sheva, no courses, one locked machine.

Then the part nobody sees:
Neighbours' PCs fixed for 100 shekels.
A Chinese phone flipped on eBay for $30.
A school forum built because the kids needed one.

Years later a company sold. From the outside it looks like talent.

Every skill in this story started as something someone blocked.

What was the first thing you learned because someone said no?

#Entrepreneurship #Founders

## D — Manual years before AutoDS (hook verbatim from the Codie Sanchez / Christine Carrillo post; bonus, fits the episode exactly)

--- POST STARTS ---
Working smarter, not harder is a beautiful lie.

From 14 to 18 the work was manual.
Find a product. Upload the photos. Write the listing. Ship it from the supplier. Answer the buyer. Repeat, all day, one eBay listing at a time.

Four years of that.

At 21 the smart part arrived: a system to run 100 eBay stores automatically.
Other sellers saw it and asked for it. That system became AutoDS.

It was only possible because of the four manual years. Every rule it automated was a rule learned by hand.

You cannot automate what you have not done by hand. Not for a week. For years.

Hard first. Smart second. In that order, every time.

What did you do by hand for years before you could automate it?

#Automation #Entrepreneurship
