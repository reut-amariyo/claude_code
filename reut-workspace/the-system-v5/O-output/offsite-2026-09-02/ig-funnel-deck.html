The gate rule is opposite on the two platforms, and both are now proven. On Instagram the comment gate is the follower engine. Nobody should carry one platform's lesson to the other.

Profile visits barely moved between July and August, 65,463 against 64,241, while output went from 114 pieces to 161. The extra 47 pieces bought no additional attention at the top of the funnel. More posting is not the lever for September.

On the follower rate, July is the outlier and not the baseline. June converted 13.4% of profile visitors and August 14.0%. July's 37.2% was one childhood photo. The true rate is around 14%, measured twice, and it moves only when an exceptional asset appears.

This is the part worth understanding. Average views per video barely moved, 34,333 to 33,949, so at first glance August was pure volume. It was not. The median video went from 1,877 views to 4,318, and the top three fell from 82% of the month's views to 66%. August was a month where the typical video worked rather than a month carried by two outliers. Somebody changed something in the process and nobody has written down what.

TikTok published 41 videos against Instagram's 161 and averaged 64% more views on each one. It is the only platform where more output came with more reach per piece, it is at four times its September target, and it gets the least attention of any channel we run. That is the clearest resourcing question on the table.

These are views on videos published in each month, pulled from Metricool, not account-level monthly views. TikTok's own July figure on the same month was 787.5K, so treat the series as the shape rather than the decimal.

Three candidate causes, and what the data says

The profile restriction

Already active on 3 July, and July still delivered 3.18M views. On its own it does not explain August. It may have tightened, which we cannot see from outside.

The Instagram cross-post breaking

Partly supported. In July 96.9% of Facebook traffic came from Reels; in August reels are 63.3% of views and photo posts jumped to 34.4%. That is what a broken reel pipe looks like.

No viral hit

Facebook was never steady. May was carried by two reels at 9.8M and 4.6M, July had one at 860,319. August's biggest piece did 13,936.

What the publishing data says. Facebook posted heavily in the first half of August, five to nine pieces a day between the 5th and the 12th, and those days produced five to eight thousand views each against a July daily average near 103,000. Content was there and it did not travel. Publishing did fall to almost nothing from the 21st to the 29th, so supply dropped too, but later. Distribution is the primary cause and supply is secondary.

One number settles it. Facebook's post count for August against July, and views per post for each. If views per post fell around 95%, it is distribution. If the post count fell around 95%, it is the Instagram connection. Worth pulling before anyone spends on fixing the wrong thing. Until it is answered, the plan should assume Facebook contributes nothing, which means the 7M monthly target has to be met by Instagram and TikTok alone.

A follower target and a Build Your Store target can quietly diverge here. India, Brazil and Nigeria are 22% of the Instagram audience, and Facebook's audience is a different population again. If those followers convert to paying Shopify stores at a very different rate from the American ones, then growing followers and growing stores stop being the same job and the content plan has to choose. We cannot answer it today because signups carry no source. It is the first thing a tagged link will tell us.

Build Your Store

The one tension to resolve rather than work around

The chain is content, then a comment or DM, then the ManyChat flow, then a store created, then a Shopify customer. We can see the first two steps and nothing after them.

Option one

Build Your Store lives only on its own brand. Lior's channels stay clean.

Option two

Lior's channels carry it as founder story only, never as an offer.

Option three

Lior's channels carry the offer, capped at a fixed share of the monthly mix.

Lior's personal brand is a founder journey, not product marketing, and promoting the product pushes against that. If the room does not pick one of these three today, the agency keeps proposing product posts and they keep getting killed. The founder story is the strongest asset here and it is already documented: the 2023 AI wave, Shopify's own conference where they shared that around 30% of new customers never finish setting up, the same picture inside AutoDS, so use AI to remove the hardest barrier. That is journey content that happens to sell.

Wording that is not negotiable. Partner, never affiliate. The ~30% is an opportunity Shopify shared openly, never a criticism. E-commerce, never dropshipping.

The AI videos

Fix, pivot or kill

The target is six AI reels a month at 8,000+ views. Two rounds have failed. The Gemini watermark flagged in the July review was still in the 30 August render, which makes it a process failure rather than a creative one.

Kill

Move the hours to real footage. Defensible after two failed rounds.

Pivot

Point the lane at what only AI can do: generate what cannot be filmed. Fourteen years old on eBay, the first office, the night of the VAT crisis.

Fix

Three more attempts under a hard gate. Watermark fixed, one venue, two images. 8,000 views or the lane closes.

The pivot is the recommendation, and the reason is in the follower data. AI and graphics content wins reach while real, personal Lior wins follows. We have been spending the AI lane on a synthetic Lior giving generic advice, which competes with the real one and loses. Aiming it at his own history points it straight at the personal artifact, which is the only thing proven to convert on this account.

Questions for Lior

Ask for documents that exist, not for content ideas

Five artifact giveaways produced 45% of August's followers, and we are down to two artifacts with one already exhausted. Everything in Q4 depends on this list getting longer, so these are the questions worth the room's time.

What people already want from you

  • 01What do founders keep asking you to send them?
  • 02What is the question you get asked most, in DMs, at events, over dinner?
  • 03What do people assume you know that they want you to explain?
  • 04When someone finds out about the exit, what is the first thing they ask?
  • 05Who calls you for advice, and what about?

What only you have

  • 06What document would be embarrassing to show, and what would people learn from it?
  • 07What did you get wrong that you have written evidence of?
  • 08What did you decide not to do, and still think you were right?
  • 09If you had to give away something nobody else on earth could give, what is it?
  • 10What is in your drive that you would never post, and why?

Then go drawer by drawer, one minute each, and write names on the wall

The acquisition: the data room index, the diligence questions Fiverr sent, the first offer email, the board update from the month of the deal.
Zero to one: the first landing page, the first pricing page, the first-100-users spreadsheet, the eBay listing from age 14, the 2023 AI plan that became Build Your Store.
Operating: the hiring scorecard for the first 10 hires, the weekly dashboard, the dollar-bug postmortem, the org chart at 50 people against 200.
Money: the VAT paperwork, the first P&L, the month he first paid himself.
Judgment: ideas he rejected and why, deals he walked away from.
