# AutoDS vs Wix — Precedent Library & Strategy Layer

Date: 2026-07-09
Stage: strategy BEFORE ideas, per Reut. Company-vs-company guerrilla precedents, the lesson each one carries, and the distilled rules for our move.

---

## The anchor case: Wix vs WordPress, 2021 (casabona.org analysis)

What Wix did:
- Sent unrequested $400 Bose QC700 headphones to WordPress influencers. QR code inside linked to a video where an actor playing "WordPress" warned them about Wix's incoming campaign.
- Simultaneous paid Twitter + YouTube ads hitting WordPress pain points: updates, complexity.

What happened:
- Matt Mullenweg published a scathing blog post. The community raged. The ads using mental-health imagery for "WordPress frustration" were the specific tone failure.

Casabona's verdict, and ours:
- Strategically SOUND: targeted frustrated users, not loyalists. Gifting influencers guaranteed social chatter regardless of sentiment. Mullenweg's outrage itself amplified the campaign.
- Executionally BURNED: mental-health imagery was tone-deaf and gave the community a moral high ground.
- His prescription for the defender: respond with positivity, highlight your own strengths. Which tells the ATTACKER: leave no moral high ground to take.

**Lesson for us: the attack wasn't the mistake, the tone was. Wix proved the mechanics work. We run their play with the tone fixed, against them.**

## Precedent library by strategic pattern

### Pattern 1 — The Doorstep Stunt: stage it on their turf
- **Salesforce vs Siebel, 2000.** Benioff hired actors to picket Siebel's user conference with "No Software" signs, plus a fake TV news crew. Siebel panicked, parked semi-trucks to block the protest (blocking their own signage) and called the police. Real news crews showed up. Revenue grew 100%+ annually 2001-2003. Also: rented every taxi at Nice airport during a conference, drivers pitched Salesforce for the 45-minute ride.
  - *Lesson: the target's panic IS the story. Design the stunt so any defensive reaction by Wix becomes our second act.*
- **Samsung vs Apple, Sydney 2011.** Pop-up store next to the Apple store during an iPhone launch, selling the Galaxy SII for $1.50 to people standing in Apple's line.
  - *Lesson: convert the competitor's own crowd at their own moment. Price theater makes the offer the headline.*
- **AMD vs Intel.** Hijacked Intel's 40th-anniversary giveaway by offering its own processors to winners.
  - *Lesson: piggyback their calendar moments, not just their address.*

### Pattern 2 — The Duel Invitation: design for a reply
- **BMW vs Audi, Santa Monica 2009.** Audi billboard: "Your move, BMW." BMW rented a bigger board across the street: "Checkmate." Audi crowdsourced its comeback on Facebook. Every round was a free press cycle for both.
  - *Lesson: pick a jab the rival CAN answer with a smile. Each answer doubles reach. Wix is the most reply-capable brand in Israel — this pattern is built for them.*

### Pattern 3 — Condescending Kindness: the welcome letter
- **Slack vs Microsoft, 2016.** Full-page New York Times ad "welcoming" Microsoft Teams to the market and offering friendly advice.
  - *Lesson: kindness positions you as the incumbent even when smaller. Counter-lesson: it aged into hubris once Teams overtook Slack. Never write a line that looks stupid if the scoreboard flips.*

### Pattern 4 — The Gift Bomb: guaranteed coverage
- **Wix's own headphones move.** Coverage was guaranteed regardless of sentiment, because influencers can't not talk about a $400 surprise package.
  - *Lesson: a physical artifact sent to press/influencers guarantees conversation. The artifact's tone decides whose side the conversation takes.*

### Pattern 5 — Underdog Honesty: license to attack
- **Avis vs Hertz, 1962.** "We're No. 2. We try harder." Named its own weakness, which bought permission to attack the leader for 50 years.
- **Pepsi Challenge, 1975.** Turned the comparison into a participatory street event instead of a claim.
  - *Lesson: self-aware humility + let people experience the comparison themselves beats asserting it.*

### Pattern 6 — The Location Hijack: their real estate as your mechanic
- **Burger King "Whopper Detour", 2018.** Geofenced 14,000 McDonald's locations; the app unlocked a 1-cent Whopper only when ordered from inside a McDonald's parking lot. 1.5M app downloads in days.
  - *Lesson: the competitor's location shouldn't be a backdrop, it should be the TRIGGER of the offer. Directly applicable to anything we build at Wix HQ.*

### Pattern 7 — The Data Jab: attacks need an offer attached
- **T-Mobile Un-carrier vs AT&T/Verizon.** Real pricing data + a showman CEO, sustained for years. Worked because it was a persona, not a one-off.
- **Microsoft "Scroogled" vs Google.** Millions spent attacking Google's ad model with no reason given to choose Bing. Flopped.
  - *Lesson: an attack without a self-serving offer converts nobody. Any stock-chart jab at Wix must carry the migration offer in the same frame. Comparative-advertising law adds: verifiable facts only when naming names — puffery or hard data, nothing in between.*

### Pattern 8 — The Proxy War: OUR exact situation, and the gap in the market
Partner-attacks-the-platform's-rival is rare, which is exactly the opportunity. Closest analogies: Samsung's Apple-attack ads that structurally benefited Google/Android; Epic's "1984" Fortnite parody against Apple. Nobody in the Shopify ecosystem has done a physical stunt against Wix on Shopify's behalf.
- *Lesson: when a PARTNER throws the punch it reads as conviction, not corporate rivalry, and the platform can amplify with clean hands. Requirements: AutoDS voice, AutoDS money, zero Shopify logos on attack materials, quiet pre-brief so Shopify can repost without blinking.*

## The failure file (what killed the ones that died)
1. **Wix 2021** — tone-deaf imagery handed the enemy the moral high ground.
2. **Scroogled** — all attack, no offer.
3. **Slack's letter** — smugness that aged badly against a stronger opponent.
4. General rule from the comparative-ads graveyard: an attack ad can be the first time your audience hears of the RIVAL — punching up advertises the target. Punching sideways (Wix ≈ AutoDS weight class in Israel) is safer than it looks.

---

## THE 7 RULES FOR OUR MOVE (distilled)

1. **Their doorstep is the mechanic, not the backdrop.** The offer must only work BECAUSE it's at Wix HQ, Whopper-Detour style.
2. **Script Wix's response before the stunt.** Their panic, their humor, or their silence — all three branches pre-written. The Siebel police call and the Mullenweg blog post were the attackers' best assets.
3. **Punch with a smile, leave no moral high ground.** Tone is the single documented failure point in this genre.
4. **The attack carries the offer in the same frame.** Free migration is not a footnote, it's the headline's second half.
5. **Puffery or verified data, nothing between.** "Greener" is safe; any stock/number claim gets legal sign-off on print day.
6. **The proxy punch is the differentiator.** A partner defending Shopify is a story in itself; keep Shopify's hands visibly clean.
7. **Guarantee coverage with an artifact.** A physical gift/object to Israeli tech journalists ensures the story exists even if the street stunt underdelivers.
