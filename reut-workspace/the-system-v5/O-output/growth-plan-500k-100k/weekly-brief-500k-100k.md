## 2. Three readings that should drive the conversation

### Reading 1: LinkedIn followers do not respond to reach

April 49K impressions produced 922 followers. May 292,705 impressions produced 1,100. June 52K produced 1,009. July 72K produced 821.

Impressions moved 6x across four months. New followers stayed inside a 922-1,100 band every single month. May's two viral posts bought 240,000 extra impressions and 91 extra followers.

The implication is uncomfortable and worth saying out loud in the weekly: a LinkedIn plan built on "more reach" will not produce 100K followers. The bottleneck sits after the impression, in whatever turns a reader into a follower. We cannot name it precisely yet because monthly profile visits are the one number we have never pulled, which makes it the single highest-value missing metric in this whole pack.

### Reading 2: Instagram's July was one asset, not a new system

July added 24,354 followers against June's 4,740. The AGE 14 childhood photo post alone produced 14-15K of them, 57% of the month's growth from one piece of content.

Two things follow. Reels volume doubled from 48 to 97 and per-reel views halved from 40,945 to 20,619, so volume bought total reach and not better reach. Meanwhile 17 photo posts produced roughly as many views as 97 reels, at about 111,765 views per post.

The 500K question is therefore narrow: is the personal-artifact photo post a repeatable series, or was July a one-off? Everything in the forecast turns on that answer, so it deserves a decision in the room rather than an assumption.

Instagram 500K is an extension of a run rate. Hold July and it arrives around the end of 2027 with no new idea required.

LinkedIn 100K is a different order of problem. The impressions column above is a warning rather than a plan, because it assumes followers scale with reach and Reading 1 says they do not. Presented honestly, LinkedIn at current mechanics is a seven-to-eight-year target.

## 3. What I would put on the table

For Instagram, the plan is a format decision, not a volume decision. Commit to a fixed monthly quota of personal-artifact posts mined from Lior's real archive, treat "At [age]" as a series with a content calendar behind it, and stop treating reel count as the growth lever.

For LinkedIn, we should challenge the shape of the goal before we commit to it. Three levers are worth costing before the weekly:

1. The profile and conversion layer. The interest graph reads the profile before it distributes anything, and nothing downstream can be diagnosed until we have monthly profile visits.
2. Porting the converter. The artifact and at-age format converts on Instagram and on X. It has never been run as a series on LinkedIn.
3. Cross-platform push. There are 100,000 Instagram followers and 11,607 on LinkedIn. Moving even 2% deliberately is 2,000 followers, which is two months of current growth.

Paid follower campaigns are the only lever that buys followers directly at a known price. If 100K in twelve months is a firm requirement rather than an aspiration, that conversation has to happen, because the organic math does not reach it.
