One post produced 57% of July's growth

The AGE 14 childhood photo brought 14–15K of July's 24,354 followers. August has published more content than any 19-day stretch on record — 101 pieces — and added roughly 5,000. Volume is not the variable. The personal-artifact photo post is.

Per asset, July's 17 feed posts averaged 112K views against 21K for its 97 reels. Reels buy reach; posts buy followers.

What I found in the account

There is a ceiling on the follower number

Auditing the account turned up something that changes the plan: the channel currently producing LinkedIn followers has a hard limit, and Lior is a third of the way into it.

That is not a target the current playbook reaches at any level of effort, which makes 100K a decision about what to invest rather than a question of execution.

Conclusions

What to do about it

Instagram · 500K

Protect the format, stop buying volume

  1. Commit to 2–3 personal-artifact posts a month. One did 14–15K followers in July. Mine the real archive: childhood, army, the first office, early AutoDS.
  2. Move the mix from reels to feed posts. 17 posts matched 97 reels on views in July, and converted far better per asset.
  3. Watch churn as the health metric. 34% in May, 26% in June, 8% in July. It falling while volume rose is what makes a projection to 500K credible.
  4. Reframe the target as one breakout artifact post a month, rather than more content. That is the difference between a 16-month path and a 50-month one.
LinkedIn · 100K

Build the lane that does not exist yet

  1. Make Follow the profile's primary button. Plenty of people already ask to connect with Lior, and every one of them spends a slot from the 30,000 and waits on his approval. Switching the default sends that same demand into an uncapped, instant lane. LinkedIn imposes this switch anyway once the cap is reached, so the only real choice is whether it happens now or after all 30,000 slots are gone.
  2. Keep invitations at LinkedIn's weekly ceiling, ICP only. Founders and SaaS operators, so the invites also teach the feed's interest graph who Lior is. Do not push past the limit; a restricted account costs more than the followers.
  3. Rewrite the profile around the three pillars. The feed reads the profile before it distributes anything, and the profile is what has to convert a reader into a follower.
  4. Push the Instagram audience across. 100K there, 12K here. Even 2% moved deliberately is 2,000 followers, two months of current growth, and it has never been tried.

The open question: a stronger follower engine

The demand is already there. People find Lior and ask to connect with him every day, and that is the healthiest signal in this whole review. The problem is where that demand currently lands — in a lane LinkedIn caps at 30,000, one approval at a time.

So the work is not to create interest, it is to build somewhere uncapped for it to go. Three things worth putting on the table: making Follow the default so the same people arrive as followers, giving the profile an explicit reason to follow rather than leaving it to chance, and turning comments on other founders' posts into a discovery channel, since out-of-network readers can follow but cannot connect.
