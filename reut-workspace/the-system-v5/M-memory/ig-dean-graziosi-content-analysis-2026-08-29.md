# Dean Graziosi - CONTENT analysis (what he actually says and how he says it)

Corpus: the 12 full captions IG serves publicly, Aug 24-29 2026 + his 3 pinned posts.
Companion file: [[ig-dean-graziosi-teardown-2026-08-29]] covers format, cadence and numbers.

## 1. He writes about one thing

Twelve posts, one antagonist: **the reader's own waiting.**

It never gets named the same way twice. It shows up as fear, as "the story you've attached to what happens if you try", as waiting for certainty, as needing one more piece of information, as distraction, as being the bottleneck. Same enemy, twelve costumes.

Topic split across the 12:
- Self-agency / stop waiting: **5 posts**
- Character and hunger: 2
- Attention and focus: 1
- Legacy and mortality: 1
- Family and parenting: 2
- Meta, "yes I repeat myself": 1

Business tactics: **zero**. AI content: **zero**, even though his own product is called AI Advantage. He never sells and never teaches. The content does one job: make the reader feel capable.

## 2. The emotional contract is absolution, then a small push

Nearly every caption runs the same emotional arc:

> harsh truth → "Good." / "That's actually good news." → the truth is handed back as power → one quotable line

"No one's coming to save you. / **Good.** / Because the day you stop waiting is the day your life changes."
"You're the bottleneck. / And **that's actually good news.** / Because if you're the thing standing in your way, you're also the thing you can change."

He never tells the reader to do more. He tells them the thing is simple and the failure was never a verdict on them. Relief, not information. This is the same spine as Reut's best Hebrew post: pain hook, absolution, mechanism.

## 3. The actual writing engine: the anaphora stack

Every long caption is built around one stack of parallel lines that all start with the same word. This is the device, and it is what makes his posts screenshot-able.

- "No one owes you an opportunity. / No one owes you success. / No one owes you a second chance."
- "Every win is earned. / Every failure is a lesson. / Every result has your fingerprints on it."
- "Not the timing. Not the opportunity. Not the algorithm. Not the economy. Not the fact that you need one more piece of information. / **You.**"
- "Your health. / Your ideas. / Your family. / Your goals. / The work only you can do. / Even your own thoughts."
- "Who's resourceful. Who's relentless. Who will hear no 100 times and still make the 101st call."

Note the shape: 3 to 6 short parallel lines, then a **one-word or one-line landing** that breaks the pattern. The break is where the emotion fires.

## 4. Every post ends on a portable line

The last line is always short, standalone and quotable, written so it survives being screenshotted without the rest:

- "Bet on the hungry person."
- "The formula isn't complicated. Living it is."
- "The moment you own everything, you gain the power to change anything."
- "It's about deciding what gets first access to you."
- "And most of the clues are surprisingly simple. The hard part is actually living them."

One quotable per post, and it is always the closer, never buried.

## 5. Sentence craft

- Average sentence under 12 words. Almost no subordinate clauses.
- A paragraph break after nearly every sentence. A 200-word caption is 20 paragraphs.
- Sixth-grade vocabulary, with exactly one vivid image allowed per post: "chew through glass", "when life gets noisy", "dangerous in the best way".
- Second person dominates. Roughly one "I" for every eight "you". His "I" only appears as a license to speak: "I've spent decades studying success", "I see them in my own life". Then he gets out of the way.

## 6. What he never does

- **No numbers.** Twelve posts, not one verifiable figure. "100 times" and "six months" are rhetorical, not data.
- **No proof.** No company names, no dates, no case studies, no dollar amounts, no screenshots of results.
- **No CTA.** No links, no "DM me", no product mention. The one post that ends with a question, the parenting one, was his worst of the week at 477 likes and 5 comments.
- **No hashtags.**
- **No hedging.** Every sentence is a flat assertion. He never writes "maybe", "in my experience", "your mileage may vary".

## 7. The one device we cannot copy

He leans hard on "it isn't X, it's Y":
"Sometimes the thing keeping you stuck **isn't fear. It's the story** you've attached to it."
"Being less accessible **isn't really about** shutting people out. **It's about** deciding what gets first access to you."

It is his signature move and it is exactly the pattern LinkedIn's AI-slop report suppresses, and the reason this register reads as machine-written to a senior audience. It is also why 100+ word essays from him do 600 to 1,800 likes on 1.4M followers. He gets away with it on IG because his audience came for the feeling. Lior's audience came for the receipts. Copy the rhythm, never this hinge.

## 8. The five caption skeletons he reuses

1. **Absolution reframe.** Harsh line, "Good.", why it is power, a 3-line denial stack, epigram.
2. **The bottleneck list.** "Not the timing. Not the opportunity. Not the algorithm." then the one-word landing, then the good-news turn.
3. **The boring formula.** "X is pretty boring when you break it down." then 5 to 7 imperative one-liners, then "The formula is simple. Living it is the hard part."
4. **Bet on the person.** A character portrait built from "Who's..." lines, ending in a verdict imperative.
5. **The three-word card.** One imperative on a plain background: "Build it anyway." 1,814 likes, one of his best of the week. Cheapest thing he makes.

**Take the machinery, swap the fill.**

**Rules that carry over cleanly:**
- One antagonist across all content. Lior's is not fear, it is waiting for permission. Waiting for the investor, the perfect plan, the extra data point.
- The anaphora stack of 3 to 6 lines with a one-word landing.
- The closing epigram as the last line, always.
- Second person. One "I" per eight "you", and the "I" only earns the right to speak.
- No CTA. His best posts ask for nothing and his one question CTA flopped.

**Rules that do not carry over:**
- Content-free assertion. Dean spent twenty years on stages buying the right to say "Build it anyway" with nothing behind it. Lior's differentiator is the opposite, so every skeleton needs a number or a scar in it.
- "It isn't X, it's Y", banned for us on reach grounds and on voice grounds.
- Parenting and life-advice register. The CEO relevance filter kills it. Family stays as founder cost, never as advice to parents.
