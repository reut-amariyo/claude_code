# Donor teardown: Justin Welsh, "Go make some friends." (Saturday Essay #250, 2026-10-17)

Reut approved this as an idea for a future Lior post on 2026-10-18. Status: BANKED, not drafted.
Source: https://justinwelsh.me/essays/make-friends
Archetype: contrarian defense of a behavior the internet calls a grift, then a personal receipt.

## The idea, one line
People attack successful founders for promoting each other. That network is the advantage, and
you can start building it today, before anyone in it is powerful.

## The 8 beats
1. Hook: a public behavior people get angry at, shown as a scene - a launch, the same names sharing it, a comment pile-on.
2. Reframe: of course people help friends they've known for years.
3. Name the real advantage the critics spotted: friends with audiences, connections and enthusiasm for your work.
4. Principle alone on a line: you can't build the network you resent.
5. Receipt: a year + 3 named peers + ordinary rituals - a quarterly notes call, a weekly show, dinners that became an investment.
6. Second principle alone on a line: build the network before anyone in it is powerful.
7. Projection: give it 5-10 years, some of these strangers become the big names.
8. Close in 2 short lines: the key people in your network ten years from now don't know you exist yet.

## Why it works
- Hook takes a side in a fight the reader has seen in their own feed.
- The receipt is deliberately ordinary, so the reader believes they can copy it.
- Two quotable lines, each alone on its own line, both save-shaped.
- Ends on the reader's future, not the author's success.

## Strip before reusing
- Welsh's essay is ~700 words. For LinkedIn compress to one idea, 750-1,200 chars, hook under 10 words.
- Pick ONE of his two principles as the quotable, per the one-idea rule. Probably beat 6.
- No "it's not X, it's Y" anywhere when rewriting beat 2-3.

4. Has he been called out for "friends promoting friends"? If yes, that's the hook.
