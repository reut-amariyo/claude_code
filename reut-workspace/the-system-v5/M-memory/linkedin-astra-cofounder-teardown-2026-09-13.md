## The one-line diagnosis

The plan was built on the 8-beat template of the 25K post. The published copy kept 2 of the 8 beats, and the
visual shipped as a designed infographic instead of the planned screenshot. What went out was a generic
tool tutorial with a hype hook, which is exactly the lane the system says we lose to AI newsletter accounts.

## Why the hook alone could have sunk it

- "Ok X is a BEAST" is the X/Twitter hype-account opener. On LinkedIn it reads as an AI-newsletter voice, not a CEO.
- "everything it showed me was nothing until I tried this:" is a curiosity-gap template. This is the shape the
  AI-slop report button targets. Reut: check the post analytics for the slop warning.
- No concrete anchor in line 1. The playbook rule is under 10 words AND a named entity / number / year /
  confession. A product name plus an adjective is not an anchor.
- The 09-07 hook told the reader something about Lior in 9 words. This one told them Lior's opinion of a product.

## The visual: what "too much graphics" means precisely

Planned in every version of the file: "real AGENTS.md screenshot, one line circled red" (Family 2, personal artifact).
Shipped: a 3-step flowchart with 10 boxes, a decision diamond, a RECHECK loop, arrows, and the full prompt in
small type. Frame 1 of the GIF already shows the whole chart; the animation only moves a highlight box.

Four separate problems, each enough on its own:

1. **It restates the text.** The three steps and the prompt appear in the copy AND in the image. The image
   added zero information. The 09-07 chart carried numbers the text did not have (1,797 vs 1,762). A visual
   earns its slot only when it shows something the copy cannot say.
2. **It is a marketing-team graphic.** Playbook rule: 4 visual families, never designed graphics. Branding it
   with [ life is beta ] and Lior's avatar does not move it into a family. A flowchart is a flowchart.
3. **Unreadable at feed size.** The prompt is 3 lines of ~14px text inside a 1080x1350 canvas. On mobile it is
   a grey block. The 09-07 chart had two 90px numbers and two logos. One glance, one fact.
4. **The GIF had no reveal.** Frame 1 = final frame. Nothing happens, so nothing stops the scroll.

Rule going forward, one sentence: **the visual is ONE real artifact or ONE big number, never the post redrawn as a diagram.**

## The lane problem

CLAUDE.md allows a how-to only as "insider intel + operator confession + strategic close". This post had none of
the first two. The source was Nate Herk's public video, the confession block was cut, so the how-to exception
was never earned. Without the exception it is a tool tip, which the file itself said on 09-06 loses to the
AI newsletter accounts. Also: second Astra/Codex how-to in 3 days. The audience that follows a 200-person-company
CEO is not a Codex audience twice a week.

## Process learning: the drift happened in the last three versions

v16 was approved. v17 cut the co-founder reveal (the confession). v18 cut "this week", "One file.", the Wispr line.
Each trim was reasonable alone. Together they removed every Lior-specific beat, and nobody re-ran the gate on
v18. The gate table exists for v1; the published version is v18.

New rule: **re-run the beat table on the version that ships, not the version that was approved.** A trim that
deletes a beat is a structural change, not a copy edit.
