## Why it is worth keeping

It is the first tool how-to that has worked on this account. The two before it flopped: the
MD→HTML post at 400 impressions and the Claude-in-Slack post. The difference was not softer
instructions. The instructions here are harder and more specific than either. The difference was
the frame around them.

## Five things that came out of this session and generalise

**1. Check every draft against its own last line.** Three separate lines were killed for
contradicting the ending: "make the switch", "Codex is better than Claude Code", and a set of
visual concepts where Codex eats Claude. If the close says he kept both, nothing above it can say
he left. This is now the fastest contradiction check we have.

**2. Do not abstract the useful part to dodge a rule.** The first drafts turned the donor's
concrete steps into vague "judgement calls" to avoid the no-tool-tips rule, and Reut rejected them
outright. The tutorial exception exists for exactly this case. Real steps plus confession opener
plus strategic close is legal. Vague steps are just a worse post that breaks the same rule.

**3. The donor is a structure, never a fact-checker.** A run command was invented and a reason was
inherited wrong from the donor. Both were caught by reading the tool's own documentation. Any post
carrying a command, a path or a benchmark number gets primary-source verification first.

**4. Numbers need the right operation.** "35 points, 2%" is arithmetic on an Elo rating, which is
an interval scale, so the ratio means nothing. The defensible reading is a win probability:
1/(1+10^(-35/400)) is about 55 wins in 100. The graphic was corrected before publishing.

**5. Format is part of the deliverable.** The animation was rebuilt four times: 9:16 pillarboxed on
desktop, 4:5 got cropped by LinkedIn's mobile video player, the bottom 10% sat under the player
controls. The answer was an animated GIF in the image slot. Full rules in the memory file
linkedin-media-format-rules.

## The comment thread, and the reply pattern that worked

Roughly a dozen replies drafted, all on the same shape: credit the commenter in the first clause,
then hand them one thing they had not got to yet. Never plain agreement, never a correction as the
opening move. Two specific saves worth remembering:

- A commenter quoted GLM-5.3 at 1,770 when the board Lior posted has it at 1,609. The reply was
  written to be warm without repeating the number.
- A commenter said Claude had been unbearable since 4.6. The reply took the observation and turned
  it into the argument for running two, rather than agreeing. Piling on a vendor the day after
  publishing "Claude is not dead" would have read as bandwagoning.
