## 2. Format roles, now settled

- **Reels = reach.** Views, not followers.
- **Carousels and posts = followers + engagement.** This is the growth engine.
- **Stories = a direct sales tool.** New position, and the biggest change from this meeting.
  2-3 frames maximum, minimal text on a black background, always a CTA plus a ManyChat
  automation plus a DM. Nobody had been running stories until the day before this meeting.
- Every carousel from now on carries an automation. Reels get one where possible, harder at
  reel volume.

Stories authenticity fight, resolved in the middle: the account does not need to be pinned to
Lior's real-time day. Agency will warm the audience for what is about to be published, and pull
personal footage in batches. Practical decision: a **shared iCloud album** connecting Lior's
phone to the agency, plus Reut sending whatever Lior has every Sunday. Galit was named as the
best source of raw footage and the route to a female audience, which the account lacks.
Padel/tennis to be shot with an Insta360-style net mount.

## 5. Build Your Store positioning

Audience: 20-30, working a 9-to-5, wants a side hustle not a replacement, no e-commerce
background, no confidence, and the block is "I don't know how and I think it's expensive".

The core problem the agency named: every e-commerce influencer sells with proof of stores and
earnings. Lior has none, because he is the founder, not an operator. The fix agreed: sell the
software's capability and what it did for other users, and lead with the 0-to-something story
he does own. Promotion goes into existing surfaces "on the way past" — a button inside every
automation so clicks are measurable, and the second page of the startup PDF.

Pricing trust, unresolved: Build Your Store is free but Shopify charges $1, and the agency
believes discovering that mid-flow costs trust. Decision was to A/B test ads, free vs $1, and
to publish the full cost ladder openly ($0 / $1 / $5 / $100) because naming the whole ladder
is itself the trust play.

Reut's formula, stated cleanly and worth keeping verbatim in strategy work: what always works
in social is a big promise with a small, simple action. Where it fails is when the small action
gets complicated. Same rule already in memory from Tom Orbach; now confirmed by Reut in her
own words.

## 6. Lior's own "how to start" answer — the strongest raw material on the tape

Asked how he would take a product to the next level, he answered without hedging:

1. Go out to **design partners** — people close to you who have a real interest in using the
   thing, work with them free, because for them the value is the outcome.
2. Check the product is genuinely sticky with them before anything else.
3. Then start charging, and go to market.
4. Distribution: communities, organic, social media, conferences. Talk to people directly.
5. **Read competitors' reviews to find where they are weak, then market against those exact
   points.** See what people praise, then publish on the points people complain about.
6. Solve your own problem. If it is your problem, the people around you have it too, and they
   are the route into their friends and their communities.

Agency reaction was correct: every single line of that is its own piece of content
("how I found my first design partner", "the 5 conferences that matter", "why a design partner
matters"). Nothing here needs invention. It is a complete series in one answer.

Second block, on acquisitions. His three criteria, in his words: what value does this bring to
my company, can I get in and grow it, and what is the ceiling of this acquisition. Method: real
due diligence, know their superpowers and where they fall down, and buy what would take too long
or cost too much to build. Yaballe example: they had a **community**, which AutoDS had never
built, and they sold to agencies while AutoDS only sells to end customers, so the acquisition
opened a market rather than just adding revenue. That is a clean, quotable case study.

## 8. Craft notes worth keeping

- Lior's talking-head videos on a black background with minimal captions performed best of all his
  talking video. Instruction to him: keep making them, the agency will supply topics.
- **Cap them at ~40 seconds.** He speaks slowly, so a 90-second or 2-minute cut kills watch-through.
  Fewer, shorter messages beat stretching one.
- Editing tip from the agency's editor, from the Galit interview: Lior stretches the connector
  before a sentence ("And I think that you..."). Shortening that one word is what makes him
  cuttable. Silence between sentences is fine and easy to remove; the drawn-out connector is not.
- ⚠️ The editor claims that "I" in a hook suppresses reach and proposed "how you can" instead.
  Reut pushed back that "how I did X and now you can too" is not self-regarding, and the room
  agreed only to test it. Unproven, and it collides with the existing rule set. Treat as a test.
- Teleprompter on the phone was floated so scripts can be fed to him without memorisation.

## What I take into my own work

1. The first-party-artifact + ungated-takeaway pattern is now proven on Instagram and LinkedIn.
   It stops being a LinkedIn rule and becomes the cross-platform rule.
2. Stories are a sales surface, not a diary. 2-3 frames, black background, automation attached.
3. Lior's "how to start" and "why I buy companies" answers are finished content, not raw notes.
   They belong in the book source bank and in any founder-practical post I draft.
4. Big promise, small simple action. When something underperforms, check whether the small action
   got complicated.
5. Watch the 92 number and the fabrication suggestions. Both are mine to raise, repeatedly, since
   nobody in the room did.
