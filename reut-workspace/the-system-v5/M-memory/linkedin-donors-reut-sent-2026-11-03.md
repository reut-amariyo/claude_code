## D1. "My mum said she'd never speak to me again" (dropout / immigrant parent)
Author: probably Steven Bartlett. The mum left school at 7-8 in Nigeria and he dropped out of uni.
Closes with a Bo Ren quote.

| Beat | Shape |
|---|---|
| Hook | A named loved one plus a dramatic quoted threat. "She meant it." stands alone |
| Cost | How long it lasted and what finally changed it, e.g. "two years, until the business paid my bills" |
| Twist | "It never really bothered me, because I understood her story." |
| Motive | "She was standing in my way out of love." |
| Backstory | The other person's history that explains the pushback |
| Widen | "I think this is true for a lot of people..." |
| Principle | "The people who love you most give you the worst advice, because they care" |
| Mechanism | "Their advice is shaped by what they lived through." Then risk is weighed by their scars |
| Reframe | "What I understand now..." Their worst case vs my real worst case |
| Takeaway | A question to ask yourself: are they describing your situation or their own? + "the decision has to be yours" |
| Close | A short quote from someone else, attributed |
Why it works: empathy for the person who pushed back, with no villain. The reader recognizes their own parent or boss.
Lior use: the offsite "ship without a captain" draft. Next fits: his mom's "money can disappear", eBay at 14.

## D2. "The happiest people I know all have something a little odd in common" (Justin Welsh)
| Beat | Shape |
|---|---|
| Hook | "The [superlative] people I know all have something a little odd in common:" |
| Thesis | One line with an inverted order: they built X first and Y around it |
| Counter | "Most people do it the opposite." |
| Scene | A date plus a named partner plus a concrete act: "my wife Jennifer and I made a list of what we didn't want to do" |
| Twist | "Even if people were willing to pay me for them!" |
| Context | The thing I'd escaped, and the thing that still trapped me |
| Action | "So we started pruning." + 3 hyphen bullets |
| Cost | "Two of those decisions meant giving up money. And I decided I was fine with that." |
| Result | A year plus a specific ritual, e.g. Friday brewery lunch, hike |
| Payoff | "That's exactly what I wanted when I started." |
| Widen | "Far too many people build X, get good at it, and realize they hate Y." |
| How-to | "First decide... Then build... around it." |
| Concede | "Yeah, you might... Sure, you might..." then "Those decisions are sometimes worth making." |
| CTA | Guide link. DROP for Lior, lead-magnet layer is banned |
Lior use: the offsite "most successful founders" draft.

## D3. Tim Stoddart image post: "Reminder: 99.99999% of successful people don't post on social media"
| Beat | Shape |
|---|---|
| Opener | "Reminder:" alone on line 1 |
| Stat | An absurd-precision percentage plus a contrarian claim |
| 2 lines | What they DON'T have, then what they DO: boring job, boring business |
| Close | A 4-word verdict: "In most cases, boring is best." |
Format: a black text-card screenshot, the third-party authority visual family. Under 50 words.
Lior use: short-form or X, or as a visual inside a post. The precision-stat hook works only if the
claim is plainly rhetorical.

## D4. "After 15+ years building companies, I have 2 opinions that seem contradictory"
Probably Tim Stoddart too: newsletter, boring business.
| Beat | Shape |
|---|---|
| Hook | Seat credential plus "2 opinions that seem contradictory:" |
| Aside | Line 2 plug "I write about them in my newsletter". DROP, parenthesis + plug |
| Item 1 | Numbered opinion, bold claim + 3 short proof fragments ("No viral posts. No trend-following.") |
| Item 2 | Opposite opinion, claim + a benefit list |
| Resolve | "The sweet spot is:" + 2 hyphen bullets that hold both |
| Close | "That's how you get [benefit of 2] without [cost of 1]." |
About 700 chars. A paradox resolved in a 2-bullet synthesis is very saveable.
Lior use: the offsite "captain vs bottom-up" draft. Seat credential = "4 acquisitions and an exit".
**Reut 2026-11-03:** both opinions must be LIOR'S OWN, said by him or tied to his pillars, e.g. 0→1, speed, bootstrap. Generic leadership pairs like freedom vs instructions or plan vs go were rejected as "not Lior". The version that fit: process vs speed, with his "lab" proposal and "2 people in their parents' apartment".

## Context post (Reut sent it as topic context, not as a donor): "Corporate Shit I Hate, Vol. 1: corporate patience"
A series hook born from one annoying phrase, "circle back next week". Thesis: impatience = taking time seriously.
Strong device: answering "I'll get back to you" with "when?" and then "Tomorrow is a long day" until it becomes "by noon".
"Be impatient with me too." Close: "Starting today. By noon." Useful as framing for the startup-vs-corporate debate. Lior's angle on it:
offsite meetings that end with no action items.
