## 1. Newsletter issue structure, section by section

| Beat | What Tom does | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | "I've been doing X for 13 years. Here are my predictions for 2027 🔮" | Years = earned authority, odd number, a year as concrete anchor, forecast = save-bait |
| Stakes | "I've never seen X change this fast, and I'm more excited than worried" | Emotional position in line 1, optimism over doom |
| Outside authority | Fiverr CEO told investors the internet is "dying" | Borrowed credibility + news peg |
| Insider proof | "I see it leading growth at Wiz, CMOs tell me the same" | Only-he-can-say-it seat |
| Twist | "Most of this is good news for small teams" | Flips the fear, widens the audience |
| Negative list | 4 ❌ cliché predictions everyone posts | Codie-style negative list; positions him against the slop |
| Promise | 7 predictions, each with a real example + "the move I'd make" | Every item is actionable, not opinion |
| Accountability loop | "A year from now I'll grade myself on all 7" | Built-in reason to follow and a guaranteed sequel post |
| Bonus | "How I'd build a team from scratch today" | Second payload, extends saves |

Item shape, repeated 7 times: numbered claim as a headline → one personal anecdote → one public example with a number → the move. Same 4 beats every time.

## 2. Content worth knowing

1. Customers will try to build you first. The new competitor is a Claude subscription. Move: a "[Product] vs ChatGPT" page with numbers AI can quote, plus cost of a homemade version. Glean has one per rival in its footer.
2. Weird IRL stunts are ads for the online audience. Test: would a stranger tell it at dinner. Tom's tech-roast plug: 400 in the room, 800K views.
3. AI recommendations flip with each new model. Older Claude picked Prisma 79%, newest 0%. Move: answer in sentence 1, a page per exact question, a number only you have.
4. Customers plug you into their AI via MCP. Atlassian: MCP users grow bills 2x faster. Move: "Connect to Claude" as the main CTA of the welcome email.
5. People stop shopping for themselves. Amazon "Buy for Me", Meta Muse 10M+ users. You lose the email. Agents read price + reviews, so a small brand with good price and fresh reviews can beat big ad budgets.
6. Agents will request refunds and cancel subscriptions. Defence: monthly value email that opens with what you did for them.
7. Competitors will import your customers. Claude "Import from ChatGPT", Gemini copied it in weeks. Move: build an import from your #1 competitor first.
Bonus: split marketing work into "needs a person" vs "needs instructions". Humans = strategist, an operator who writes agent instructions and edits everything before it goes live, a designer who builds templates. First task: write "the brain" docs every agent reads.

## 4. Secondary: format notes

The issue itself is a long newsletter, not a post skeleton. If Lior ever runs a newsletter or LinkedIn article series, the structure in section 1 and the craft notes below apply. Name idea on file: "The $1 Club".

## 5. Don't copy

Tom's own tells that break our rules: parentheses everywhere, "To be honest" opener, "Now listen:", "That's a hell of a moat", 😅🤯 emoji stacks, "Here's the most important thing I've ever written". Lior keeps the skeleton and his own flat-verdict voice.

## 6. Newsletter craft notes

- Title = the hook: authority years + year + emoji. Subhead teases "Biggest trends I'm seeing".
- Opens with a personal line, then announces the change of format for this issue: "Today I want to zoom out and talk strategy instead." Regular readers get told why this issue is different.
- One screenshot or chart per claim. Every prediction is proven visually, not just told.
- Each section ends with a bolded, practical "move". The reader leaves with a to-do list.
- Paid-wall placement: the bonus "how I'd build a team" section sits deep, after the free value.
- Series device: "I'll grade myself in a year" turns one issue into an annual franchise.
